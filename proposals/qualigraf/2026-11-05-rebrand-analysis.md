# Qualigraf, should we rebrand, and how

5 Nov 2026. For Raka. Internal, nothing here goes to Steven.

## What this rests on, and what it can't tell you

- **Read at the source.** The call transcript. Qualigraf's UK, French, Dutch and Canadian homepages. The 2023 merger article.
  Civica's G-Cloud pricing document. Berger-Levrault's October 2025 press release. The homepages of Granicus, Diligent, FCS,
  My Council Services, Public-i, Notubiz and eScribe, opened and screenshotted today.
- **Not read.** Civica's and iBabs's own sites, both walled to us. Luna's 45MB deck. No customer interviews and no market
  share data exist in what I found.
- **Search summaries only.** The techUK and Gartner points on how public sector buyers choose. I couldn't open the underlying
  reports, so treat them as pointers.
- **Labels.** SAID means Steven or Qualigraf said it. VERIFIED means I opened the source. INFERRED means my reading.

## 1. The short answer

**Rebuild the story, the architecture, the voice and the proof. Keep the name. Evolve the look, don't replace it.**
With Steven, call it brand and story alignment, not a rebrand.

1. **The triggers for a rebrand are all here.** A 2023 merger that left a patchwork of names, a new CEO since January with a
   mandate to unify, four sites that say three different things, and an AI product arriving into a market where rivals have
   just done the same.
2. **The reasons to hold back are just as real.** Fifteen years of equity in the Netherlands, 400+ clients, a palette that
   is the most distinctive in the category, money that is tight until spring or summer 2027, and a French team that has only
   just moved onto the current site.
3. **Buyers weigh evidence over brand.** So the best return is clarity and proof, not a new logo.

### 2.4 The product

Seven modules on the UK site, **Paperless Meetings, Council information, Workflow, Long-term planner, AI Minutes (coming
soon), Publish and Stream, Information Advisor.** The real difference from rivals is the **topic first** idea. A decision is a
file that carries its policy dossier, its strategic goals and its history from idea to archive, not a meeting that produces
minutes. INFERRED from their own copy, "putting the topic first" and "link all relevant data between policy files and strategic goals".

### 2.5 The competitors

| Who | Headline, read today | Look | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Civica Modern.Gov** (UK) | Walled to us. Its own G-Cloud document, "the single largest provider of committee management systems to principal councils", "around 380" customers. VERIFIED | Not seen | The incumbent. Steven called it "the default". It owns the words "committee management" |
| **eScribe** (Canada) | "Make it easier by bringing the whole public meeting cycle into one system" | Deep indigo and teal, an **eight stage cycle strip** | **Closest to our story.** Our eight station track looks like theirs |
| **Notubiz** (NL) | "Heldere bestuurlijke en politieke besluitvorming" | Red accent, council meeting film | Owns "clear decision-making" in the Netherlands |
| **Berger-Levrault** (FR) | "Éditeur de solutions logicielles de confiance". WeDelib launched 13 Oct 2025 with generative AI on Mistral, for small communes | Indigo and yellow, stock photos | An incumbent has already moved on AI |
| **Diligent** | "The leading AI platform for GRC" | Full red, product UI | Boards, not councils, but it sets the AI tone |
| **Granicus** | "The Citizen Experience Platform for Government" | White, red, stock people | Broad govtech |
| **FCS, My Council Services, Public-i** | "Home of Cloud Based Public Sector Software", "Building tomorrow's governments", "The leading providers of Meeting Management" | Dark and generic, stock chambers | Interchangeable. Nobody owns an idea |

**The visual field.** Red (Granicus, Diligent, Notubiz, Berger-Levrault), blue and purple tech (Public-i, eScribe), and stock
photography nearly everywhere. Qualigraf's cream, navy, coral and flat illustration, set in **Zenon**, a serif face (VERIFIED, computed
style on the French homepage), looks like nobody else. **That is an asset. Replacing it would throw away the one thing that
already differs.**

**The open ground.** Not "committee management", which Civica owns. Not "the meeting cycle", which eScribe claims. Not
"clear decision-making", which Notubiz holds in the Netherlands. What nobody says is the **whole life of a decision,
remembered**, from the first idea to the archive, with the topic, the strategy and the history travelling together.

### 2.6 The name

Three weak signals, none proof.

- The call transcript rendered it "polygraph", "choreograph" and "cholograph". That is a speech model, not a person.
- Our own first repo search for "qualigraph", the spelling we used, found nothing, because the name is spelled Qualigraf.
- A web search for "Qualigraph committee software" returned nothing about Qualigraf.

In English, "polygraph" is a lie detector, an unlucky echo for a brand built on transparency. **I would not rename.** A name change
resets recognition, and the guidance in the sources is consistent, expect a dip, keep the old name visible, plan for months of
recovery. Test it first (section 4), and fix a weak name with a descriptor and consistent use, not a new one.

### 2.7 What buyers weigh (search summaries, unverified)

- Public sector buyers value talking to peers in other councils above most things. A Gartner abstract says so.
- Frameworks shape the shortlist. Councils often buy through routes such as G-Cloud. Civica's document sits on G-Cloud 14.
- Buyers research well before they spend. That matches what Steven said about being known a year before a tender.
- Nothing I found ranks brand above evidence.

So the brand's job is to make the story **clear and checkable**, and to be ready the day the first UK customer goes live.

## 3. Rebrand or refresh, decided

| | Triggers present | Reasons to hold back |
|---|---|---|
| Merger and names | Four product brand families, no master architecture | Local names carry equity in France and the Netherlands |
| Leadership | New CEO, mandate to unify | He asked for unified branding, not a new one |
| Messaging | Four sites, three ideas | The French line already holds the answer |
| Market | AI entering, Berger-Levrault and Diligent have moved | Our AI product is "coming soon" and can't be sold yet |
| Look | Flat people illustrations and thumbnails feel dated | The palette and Zenon are distinctive and theirs |
| Name | Weak recall signals | Rename cost is high and evidence is thin |
| Money | Tight until spring or summer 2027 | A full programme is a 2027 job |

**Verdict.** A **strategic rebuild with an identity evolution**. New architecture, one master idea, one voice with local
vocabulary, a proof system, and a sharper visual layer. No new name. No new palette.

## 4. How I would approach it

### The principle

Do it **from the inside out, in an order that fits his money.** Messages and architecture first, because they cost the least
and decide everything. The identity second. The website and collateral last, as the proof it works.

### The phases

| Phase | What happens | Output | Effort, my estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Listen and audit** | Interviews with Steven, Stuart, the four country managers, the French owner and Rens. 8 to 12 customer and lost deal calls, two or three per market. An audit of every touchpoint in four markets, sites, decks, demo scripts, tender replies, product screens, events. A name test with 20 people across the four languages | Audit, a competitor map, the name test result | 6 to 8 days |
| **1. Strategy** | A workshop with the country managers. Choose the master idea, the proof pillars, the persona matrix and the architecture. Write the message house per market and the AI story | A brand platform of about eight pages | 8 to 10 days |
| **2. Identity evolution** | Keep the name, navy, cream and Zenon. Refine the wordmark and add country lockups. Set a colour hierarchy. Build an illustration system around the track and the paper. Set a photography rule and a product screen style. Show two or three looks before building | The identity system | 12 to 15 days |
| **3. System and toolkit** | An online brand guide. Templates for decks, one pagers, tender replies, email and event stands. A localisation glossary. Governance, who approves | The toolkit, so each country stops copying the Dutch | 6 to 8 days |
| **4. Roll out** | The unified website, the sales deck and demo script on the same story, the case study format, internal launch to staff, a sunset plan for legacy names | Live brand, from spring 2027 | Separate quote |

**Effort.** About 32 to 41 days before rollout. At the internal rate card for strategy and design, around €550 a day, that is
roughly **€18k to €23k**. This is an estimate from our internal card, to be confirmed before anything is quoted.
**€500 covers the workshop at the start of phase 1, not the programme.** It's the right seed, and it needs a clear ceiling when
it is offered.

**Timing that fits his budget.** Phases 0 and 1 in the first quarter of 2027, light on his money and heavy on his people.
Phases 2 to 4 once the spring or summer budget opens. The first UK customer goes live in a couple of months, so the case study
format can be ready for it.

### Positioning, three candidates for the workshop

1. **"The whole life of a decision."** The lifecycle, the topic first, institutional memory. The one nobody holds. My recommendation.
2. **"Decisions that hold up."** Audit trail, transparency, accountability. Strong as a proof pillar, weak as a master idea, since
   every rival claims trust.
3. **"Local expertise, global innovation."** His customer line. It's the operating model and belongs in the architecture,
   not at the front.

**Differentiating from eScribe.** The stages are not the idea, since eScribe shows eight of them too. The idea is the **topic file
that travels the track**, carrying its dossier, strategy and history. The track, a folder moving along a line with flags, is from
Qualigraf's own film and it's ownable. Make that the master visual device, and show the file and what it remembers, not just the stations.

### Architecture, three options

| Option | What it means | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Single master brand | Qualigraf everywhere, products named by function | Cleanest. Risks losing French and Dutch equity too fast |
| **Endorsed, with a sunset** | Qualigraf is the master. Kdelib and Parlaeus stay visible as "by Qualigraf" for 12 to 24 months, then retire | **My recommendation.** Protects equity, ends the patchwork |
| House of brands | Keep every name | The current state. It is the problem |

**OurMeeting** stays ring fenced. It serves a different audience, Steven says it is not the future, and it isn't worth spending on.

### Identity, keep, evolve, change

| Keep | Evolve | Change |
|---|---|---|
| The name, qualigraf.com | The wordmark and country lockups, "Qualigraf UK", "Qualigraf Canada" | Stock photography. Use real councils and people |
| Navy `#2A3C5E` and cream `#FEFAEB` | Colour hierarchy. Navy, cream and coral lead. Teal, plum and mustard become station colours | Flat people illustrations as the main device. Use the track and the paper |
| Zenon, Source Sans 3 | The illustration system | Low resolution product thumbnails |
| The curl mark | Motion, paper to pack, the file along the track | Three headlines for one product |

### Voice, with a local glossary

One voice, plain and precise, like Steven. Then a glossary per market, so nothing is translated from the Dutch.
UK, Democratic Services, Members, clear days, forward plan. Canada, clerk, council, agenda package. Netherlands, griffie,
raadsvoorstel. France, assemblées délibérantes, élus, délibération.

## 5. How to put it to Steven

1. **Don't say rebrand.** It is a loaded word for a new CEO with tight money, four country managers and a French owner who
   wants ownership. Say **"brand and story alignment"**, his own words, unified branding, unified messaging, local detail.
2. **Show him the four headlines side by side.** His own pain, made visible, with no criticism of his team.
3. **Start where he already is.** He liked the story. The first step is the story, built with his country managers.
4. **Keep the money honest.** The €500 workshop, then a short diagnostic, then a scoped quote. Never imply €500 buys the programme.
5. **Name the politics.** The French owner and the country managers have to be in the room from the first workshop.

## 6. Risks

- **Politics.** Four country teams and a French founder. A top down rebrand will be resisted. Co create it.
- **Scope creep into a rename.** Keep the name test as a test. Don't let the project become a rename.
- **A second change right behind France's migration.** Phase it, and don't touch France's site first.
- **The AI claim.** The product is "coming soon", and rivals have shipped. Don't brand around something that can't be demonstrated.
- **Languages.** We have Dutch. We need a French and a Canadian check, or a partner.
- **Capacity.** A four market, four language programme is large for a team of our size, with a partner for build. Price and plan for it.

## 7. For you to decide, and to ask

**Decisions.**
1. Offer a diagnostic as a paid step between the €500 workshop and a full programme?
2. Are we comfortable leading a four language programme, or do we bring in French and Canadian partners?
3. Do we keep "rebrand" out of every conversation with Steven?

**Ask him.**
1. Who owns the brand today, and who signed off the 2023 merger identity?
2. Are Kdelib, Parlaeus and OurMeeting still sold under those names?
3. What does the French owner think about a unified brand?
4. Is anyone already working on the AI Minutes launch story?
5. What is the real status of the Dordrecht team's marketing role?
