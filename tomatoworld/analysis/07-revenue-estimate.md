**Core operating revenue is most likely €250,000 – €590,000, with a central estimate
around €400,000.** Adding project and subsidy income — real for a field lab, but
invisible to us — the total plausibly reaches **€280,000 – €790,000**.

**Tomatoworld is not a small organisation.** Our €5,500 booking pilot is roughly

## Two errors in our own proposal that partly cancel

### 1. We understate visitors — probably by half or more

The proposal models **2,600 visitors and ~130 groups**. Third-party profiles of the
organisation state **5,000–8,000 visitors a year**, and our own earlier deck said
"thousands of visitors a year, from everywhere".

If the real figure is 5,000–8,000, then groups are **227–444**, not 130. **Tour
revenue and every per-booking benefit in chapter 5 are understated by roughly 2–3×.**

**This is good news and we should verify it, not quietly benefit from it.** At 325
groups the "+€100 per booking" line becomes **€32,500/year, not €13,000**.

### 2. We may overstate partners

The proposal states **38 partners × €7,500**. We count **~30 partner logos**, plus ~12
friends on a cheaper tier. 38 may be right if it includes friends, or if Aart said so
directly — but if it is our own estimate, **Aart will correct it in the first meeting
and the business case loses credibility at exactly the wrong moment.**

**The €7,500 fee is unverified anywhere.** It is the single most load-bearing number in
the entire proposal and we have no public source for it.

---

## The caveat that actually matters for pricing

**Revenue is not budget.** A stichting turning over €400,000 can still run close to
break-even once staff, greenhouse operation, energy and crop costs are paid. Ank told
us the guides are largely volunteers — which is what an organisation does when payroll
is tight, not when cash is abundant.

So the right conclusion is **not** "they are rich, charge more". It is:

> **They can afford this comfortably. What they cannot do is approve it quickly.**

That is exactly why the booking-first, €5,500 entry is the right move — not because
the money is scarce, but because the **decision** is expensive inside a foundation
with a volunteer champion and occasional board contact.

## What to ask Aart, and how

Three questions settle the whole model. Framed as checking our own homework rather
than probing their finances:

1. *"We modelled 130 group tours a year, but other sources suggest 5,000–8,000
   visitors. Which is closer? It changes the business case substantially — in your
   favour."*
2. *"We used 38 partners at €7,500. Is that the right order of magnitude?"*
3. *"Do the meeting and lunch packages sit with the tours, or are they a separate
   line for you?"*

Question 1 is the strongest opener we have with Aart: **we are telling him the
benefits may be bigger than we claimed, and asking him to correct us downward if
needed.** Nobody expects that from an agency.
