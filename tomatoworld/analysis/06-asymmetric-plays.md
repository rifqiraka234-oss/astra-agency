# Asymmetric plays — angles Panorama structurally cannot copy

`04` is the service menu. `05` is how we sell it. This is the creative tier: plays
that **invert the normal vendor relationship**, use assets Astra has and Panorama
does not, and in several cases **do not require Tomato World to have any budget at all**.

Mined from the meeting transcript and the site's own navigation.

---

## 1. 🔑 Stop selling to them. Become a partner. ⭐

Tomato World's business model *is selling partnerships*. `[ANK]` "we are depending
on our partners that are paying a fee every year, so they can showcase their company
logo and their material." The site has three tiers: **Partners, Friends, Ambassadors.**

**So: Astra becomes a Tomato World partner.** Fee paid in cash, or in kind — we
contribute the booking system and they credit it as our partner contribution.

What this does, all at once:

- **Kills the budget objection permanently.** They never have to find money. `[ANK]` "if it's too much money that's not feasible for us. I mean, end of story."
- **Kills the Panorama conflict.** A partner is not a competing vendor. Panorama have no standing to object to someone *joining*.
- **Buys the thing Astra actually lacks: Dutch credibility.** Our logo on the wall of the Westland horticulture showcase, alongside Koppert, KUBO and Royal Brinkman.
- **Opens the network Panorama currently monopolise.** `[ANK]` "It's a very close industry... **everybody knows each other.**" That sentence is a warning if we are outsiders and a gift if we are inside. Partners physically bring their customers through the building.
- **Gives us a permanent demo venue.** Our software running live in a field lab that exists to show visitors innovation.

This is the single highest-leverage idea across all six analysis files. It converts a
stalled €5k services deal into distribution.

**Test it gently with Ank first** — she will immediately understand the model,
because explaining it is literally her job.

## 2. 🔑 Bring them visitors. Raka is the asset Panorama cannot match.

`[ANK]` Their whole pitch is world food supply and international visitors — "visitors
from all over the world." `[ANK]` The site is only NL/EN.

Raka is Indonesian, based in the Netherlands, runs a business in Indonesia, and said
in the call: *"we work together with a niche partner, hence I'm here to bridge the gap."*

Indonesia is one of the largest food markets on earth and a live target for Dutch
horticulture export. Tomato World exists to host exactly these delegations.

**Play:** Astra brokers Indonesian and Southeast Asian delegations, trade missions and
corporate groups into Tomato World.

- We generate **revenue**, not cost. That changes the entire conversation with Art, who owns partner relationships.
- **Panorama physically cannot do this.** No Dutch agency can. It is Raka's personal, non-replicable asset.
- It earns us the partnership in play #1 without paying cash.
- It gives us a commercial reason to build the multilingual and international booking flows we want to build anyway.

## 3. Co-host a hackathon 🟢

`[SITE]` They already run these — `/training-and-field-lab/hackathon` and
`/rent-space/events-hacketon-2023`. It is an existing programme with existing rails.

**Play:** Astra co-hosts or sponsors a hackathon at Tomato World — ideally themed on
greenhouse data or their own "AI in greenhouse horticulture" project.

- Cheap. Their venue, their audience, our name on it.
- Puts us in a room with growers and tech companies — our actual target market.
- Produces content for them and pipeline for us.
- **Positions Astra as a technology contributor to the sector, not a supplier begging for a website job.**

## 4. Sell *through* them, not *to* them 🟢

`[SITE]` They already broker services to the sector: a **cybersecurity** page, a
**knowledge base**, a **Dutch Horti Platform**, training programmes. Tomato World is
not just a venue — **it is a channel to every grower in the Netherlands.**

**Play:** an Astra programme delivered under the Tomato World banner — "digitalisation
for growers", automation clinics, an AI workshop for horticulture businesses.

- They get programme content they need and revenue share.
- We get qualified introductions to the exact companies Panorama service.
- Nobody competes: Panorama sell those growers *marketing*; we would sell them *systems*.

## 5. Productize the Online Tour into a revenue line 🟢

`[SITE]` `/en/guided-tours/online-tour` already exists — a digital product, almost
certainly underbuilt.

The logic is unusually strong here. `[ANK]` Real greenhouses **cannot** accept visitors
because of virus risk — that is their entire moat. But physical capacity is finite,
guides are volunteers, and there is a crop rotation blackout every year during which
`[SITE]` the site currently tells everyone there are **no tours at all**.

**Play:** build a genuinely premium virtual tour — live greenhouse data, multilingual,
bookable, paid, capacity-unlimited.

- Sells during the blackout period, when revenue is currently **zero**.
- Reaches the international audience that cannot fly to Westland.
- Scales past volunteer guide availability.
- **This is a new revenue product, not a cost saving** — the only kind of proposal that excites a foundation.

## 6. Tool the tour itself — the actual product 🟢

The tour *is* Tomato World's product, and it is delivered off a PowerPoint.

`[ANK]` "I told them, listen, we need to do the PowerPoint different. It needs to be
a little bit more upgraded professionally. And they're open to it."

She raised this **before** the website, and nobody has acted on it. `[SITE]` There is a
`Guides` page; guides are volunteers like her.

**Play:** a guide toolkit — presentation system, live data pulled from the field lab,
per-audience variants (school group vs trade delegation vs investor), language switching.

- It is the thing she personally wants most and has been asking for longest.
- 🟠 Panorama boundary: **we build the system, they own the visual design.** Say that explicitly and it stays clean.

## 7. Volunteer guide operations 🟢

`[ANK]` Guides are volunteers. `[SITE]` There's a Guides page. Volunteers mean
availability is unpredictable, and language coverage is the binding constraint on
which international groups can be accepted at all.

**Play:** guide scheduling, availability, language matching, onboarding materials.
Small, unglamorous, completely unserved, and it directly unlocks international bookings.

## 8. Be the reference case, for free 🟢

Astra is young and needs Dutch proof. Tomato World has no money but has enormous
sector visibility and a building full of decision-makers every week.

**Play:** build Package 1 at or near cost in exchange for a named case study, a logo
placement, a quote from Art, and the right to demo on site.

`[ANK]` already values what we gave away: *"I really am impressed with all the work you
already put in up front... the hours spent."* This is the same move, priced and formalised.

---

## How these change the pitch

The conventional route — quote a booking system, wait for a 2027 budget — has one
failure mode we cannot control: **Art says no money, and four months of Ank's
enthusiasm decays.**

Plays 1, 2, 3 and 8 have a different shape: **they don't need Tomato World's budget
at all.** They need Art to say yes to a relationship rather than an invoice, which is
a much easier yes and one Ank is far better equipped to carry internally.

**Recommended reframe for the next conversation:**

> Don't open with a price. Open with a partnership, and let the paid modules follow
> once we're inside.

That also quietly solves the Panorama problem. A partner who brings the foundation
international visitors and runs hackathons in their field lab is not competing with
the agency that does their branding — and Panorama can hardly object to someone
making their client more successful.
