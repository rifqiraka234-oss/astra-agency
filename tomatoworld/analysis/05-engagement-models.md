# Engagement models — *how* we sell, not just what

The module menu (`04-service-menu-modules.md`) is the *what*. This is the *how*.
Raka's point: we are not limited to "build and invoice." The delivery model is
itself a creative lever, and it is the cleanest way around both blockers —
**Panorama's territory** and **no 2026 budget**.

Josh already opened this door in the call:

> "we can build an extension that can be plugged into the website, or we can partner
> together with the website builder and integrate it that way. **Yeah, we can go both ways.**"

Here is the full set.

---

## 1. Discovery / ideation sprint — we think, someone else builds 🟢
**We sell:** facilitated sessions, research, an IA and functional spec, and a
decision-ready plan. Panorama or their own people build it.

- **Panorama risk:** none. We sit *upstream* of them and feed them work. This makes
  us their friend, not their rival.
- **Why it fits:** `[ANK]` Raka already offered ideation sessions. `[ANK]` The new
  marketing hire "loves to take this as something she could really put her hands on"
  — a workshop gives her a visible win, converting a possible rival into a sponsor.
- **Cash profile:** small, fast, low-risk for them. **Perfect first yes.**
- **Risk to us:** we hand over the thinking and someone else captures the build.
  Mitigate by specifying modules only *we* can build (Track A/C/D).

## 2. Prototype as the product 🟢
**We sell:** a clickable, real prototype of the booking flow. Not a deck.

> `[ANK]` **"I really want to click it to see behind it, but there's nothing."**

She *tried to click our mockup*. That is the single strongest buying signal in the
transcript and we should answer it literally. A working prototype she can click,
and show to Art, does more internal selling than any document — and she is the one
who has to do the internal selling.

- **Panorama risk:** none — it's a demo, not a deployment.
- **Cash profile:** small fixed fee, or absorbed as sales cost.
- **This is probably our highest-ROI next action.**

## 3. Module build → plug-in handover 🟢
**We sell:** a self-contained module (booking engine, partner portal). Panorama or
TW plug it into the Umbraco site. We never touch their template.

- Exactly Josh's offer, and technically clean: Umbraco 13 supports this properly.
- **Panorama risk:** low, and lower still if we hand them the integration work.
- **Cash profile:** the main build revenue.

## 4. Joint delivery with Panorama 🟠
**We sell:** a shared engagement — they own design and front end, we own system and logic.

- **Panorama risk:** turns them from gatekeeper into partner.
- **Watch:** margin split, who owns the client relationship, who invoices.
- `[ANK]` is meeting them next week — this is the model to *offer* her to take in.

## 5. White-label / subcontract to Panorama 🟢
**We sell:** to Panorama, not to Tomato World. We become their engineering arm.

- **Upside:** they have ~138 portfolio clients across Westland horticulture — KUBO,
  Lans, Van der Hoeven, Nature's Pride. If we become the team they call for anything
  transactional, Tomato World is one job and the *channel* is the real prize.
- **Downside:** lower rate, no direct client relationship, no brand.
- **Strategically this may be worth more than the Tomato World deal itself.**

## 6. Productize and resell 🟢
**We sell:** build the booking/visitor system once for Tomato World, then license it
to other visitor centres, experience venues and field labs.

- Tomato World becomes reference customer and gets a discount — or free build in
  exchange for the reference and their name.
- `[SITE]` Their peers exist: World Horti Center, other Westland demo centres, and
  Panorama's own client base needs the same thing.
- **This is how a €5k job becomes a product.** It also gives us a legitimate reason
  to price the first build low, which solves the budget problem.

## 7. Pilot / proof of concept 🟢
**We sell:** one narrow slice — tours only, one tour type, one season — at a small
fixed price, with an agreed go/no-go.

- `[ANK]` "if it's too much money that's not feasible for us. I mean, end of story."
  A pilot makes the number small enough to never hit that wall.
- Pairs naturally with the Digivoucher below.

## 8. 🔑 Westlandse Digivoucher — subsidy funding
**Potentially the answer to the entire budget blocker.**

A regional subsidy scheme (Provincie Zuid-Holland + Gemeente Westland + Rabobank
Westland + MKB Westland, running since April 2025): **up to €2,500, covering 50% of
eligible digitalisation costs.** So a **~€5,000 project is half-funded.**

That is almost exactly the size of a discovery sprint plus prototype, or a Package 1
pilot. It could let work start in **2026 instead of January 2027** — which matters,
because four months of waiting is our biggest decay risk.

**Two important caveats, both unverified:**
1. The scheme is written for **"bedrijven gevestigd in de gemeente Westland"** —
   *companies* in Westland. Tomato World is a **stichting** (foundation). Whether a
   foundation qualifies **must be checked before we ever mention it to Ank.**
   Promising subsidy money that doesn't materialise would cost us her trust.
2. **Panorama actively market this voucher themselves** — they have a dedicated
   landing page for it. So either they route the application (and we ride along), or
   we look like we are muscling into their funding pitch. **Raise it with Panorama
   first, not with Ank.**

**Action:** verify eligibility for a stichting before the Panorama conversation.

## 9. Partner-sponsored build 🟢
**We sell:** to a Tomato World *partner*, not to Tomato World.

Their partners already pay annual fees for visibility. One of them — a KUBO, a
Koppert, a Royal Brinkman — could **sponsor the partner portal or the on-site
screens** as a marketing spend, branded as their contribution.

- Tomato World pays nothing. The partner gets exactly the visibility they already buy.
- `[ANK]` "we are depending on our partners that are paying a fee every year."
- Creative, on-model, and completely sidesteps the foundation's empty budget.
- **Art owns partner relationships — this is a pitch to Art, in his language.**

## 10. Revenue share 🟠
**We sell:** a cut of bookings or space rentals the system enables, instead of a fee.

- Zero upfront cost — kills the budget objection entirely.
- Needs booking volume data we do not have yet, and foundations are often
  uncomfortable with revenue-share. **Hold until we know the numbers.**

## 11. Build–operate–transfer 🟢
**We sell:** we build and run it for 12 months, then hand over the keys.

- Fits an organisation with no internal IT. Turns a capex ask into a small opex line,
  which is often much easier to approve inside a foundation.

## 12. Enablement / training 🟢
**We sell:** teach the new marketing hire to run what we built.

- `[ANK]` She is young, eager, wants to "score with it." Training her makes her our
  internal advocate for life and costs us almost nothing.
- Small revenue, disproportionate political value.

---

## Recommended combination

Not one model — a sequence that de-risks each step:

| Step | Model | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Free favours (cookie banner, stale tour banner, sitemap) | Costs nothing, proves we looked, shared with Panorama |
| **1** | **Clickable prototype** (#2) | She literally tried to click. Arms her internal pitch. |
| **2** | **Discovery sprint** (#1), ideally Digivoucher-funded (#8) | Small first yes, gets paid in 2026, includes the new hire and Panorama |
| **3** | **Module build, plug-in handover** (#3) | Package 1 — the booking wedge |
| **4** | **BOT or enablement** (#11 / #12) | Keeps us in, without a big ask |
| **5** | **Productize** (#6) and/or **white-label to Panorama** (#5) | Where the real money is |

**Fallbacks if budget dies:** partner sponsorship (#9), then revenue share (#10).

---

## The one-line pitch this all produces

> We don't need your 2027 budget to start. Let us run a short paid discovery with
> you, Panorama and your new marketing person — possibly half-funded by the Westland
> Digivoucher — and you'll walk out with a clickable prototype and a costed plan.
> If you like it, we build the booking module and hand it to Panorama to plug in.
> If you don't, you keep the prototype and the plan.

That sentence answers every objection Ank raised: cost, risk, Panorama, timing, and
"what's in it for me."
