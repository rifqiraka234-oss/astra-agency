## 2. THEM — what Ank actually told us

### 2.1 She is a champion without authority
> "I do not really work for tomato world as a fixed employee, because I'm already retired."
> "I get a little something for it, but that's not my main objective."
> "I want to let you know that tomato world is really not like, oh yeah, let's do it. **I mean I really need to do a lot of talking.**"
> "it's still a bit far fetched for them. It's like, ooh, no."

This is the single most important fact in the call. Ank is sold. Ank cannot buy.
Everything we produce from here is **ammunition for her internal pitch**, not a
proposal aimed at her.

### 2.2 She has two years of sunk credibility on this exact topic
> "it's already been like 2 years, I've been nagging them. You need to do something about the web."
> "they gave me the key to go behind the scenes. Already I changed a lot of language matters... but this is within the system that's given. I am not a builder."

She has already spent internal capital and hit the ceiling of what copy edits can
fix. She needs a structural win to justify two years of nagging. That is leverage
for us — and pressure on her.

### 2.3 The business model constrains the budget
> "it's a foundation. It's not a company company. **We don't sell tomatoes.** It's a foundation that tells the story."
> "we are depending on our partners that are paying a fee every year, so they can showcase their company logo and their material."
> "they can bring their customers, because in Holland it's... impossible to visit a tomato greenhouse due to the enormous amount of viruses."
> "since we don't live from the sales, we dare to take the risk."

Revenue = annual partner fees. Not ticket sales, not produce. The moat is
**biosecurity**: real greenhouses can't accept visitors, TW can. That is the
actual product being sold to partners.

### 2.4 The website problem, in her words
> "you look at our website, you think, oh my gosh, what do I choose? Am I a visitor? Am I a partner? **You know, I got lost.**"
> "If you have a website, you need to take 2 or 3 clicks. You're already done. Close it."
> "we need to go more, like, through icons, or pictures, or one step clear."

Diagnosis: **audience segmentation failure at the entry point**, plus click depth.
Not a visual refresh request. She never once asked for it to look prettier.

### 2.5 Booking is the real pain — and she said so unprompted
Asked directly where the biggest impact is:
> "I definitely see advantages in **streamlining and optimize the workflow**. Definitely."
> "the booking system that, of course, will definitely simplify work in the office."
> "The girl that does that now... it takes her a lot of time to go, somebody canceled, and then the whole thing."
> Her own analogy: "in the gym, just imagine I had to call somebody, can I do the yoga class on Friday?"

She picked our own gym example up and used it back at us. **Lead with booking.**

### 2.6 She explicitly de-scoped partner acquisition
> "the partner thing is really something that goes from business to business, personal contact, what Art is doing. It's a very close industry... Everybody knows each other. So it's not like a partner goes to the website. Oh, wow, today is Monday. Let's become a partner at Tomato World. That's not the way that would work."

But then immediately re-scoped it narrower:
> "Although we need to be able to find what could be in there for them. How appealing is it for them? So it gives them **a good head start before their discussion**."

So: no partner self-signup funnel. Yes to a **partner sales-support page** that
warms the lead before Art's in-person conversation. Do not pitch partner lead-gen.

### 2.7 The Panorama Studios landmine
> "they did this for us for like at low cost or for free, I don't know."
> "We are good friends. **So I definitely don't want to ruin my friendship.**"
> "I will find out with Panorama Studios what framework we're working in, what language it is, how they look at this, and that they can see that I still want to be their friend when this is running behind the scenes. [whether] they also see an advantage in it."

Panorama can veto this deal by feeling threatened. Ank has told us plainly that
her friendship matters more than our project. **Position as additive to Panorama,
never as a replacement.**

### 2.8 Money and timing
> "it boils down to money... What's it gonna cost? What is involved? Is it worth it? Do we gain it back?"
> "if it's too much money that's not feasible for us. **I mean, end of story.**"
> "you can make an estimate in how many hours it will take you guys... Based on that, you will offer us a certain price and then we can agree or not agree."
> "I don't know [if] there's still budget for this year, you know, 2026. And thinking perhaps he has a budget [for] 2027 and we can make a start in January."

Hard read: 2026 budget probably spent. Realistic start **January 2027**. She wants
an hours-based estimate, modular, with a per-module price.

### 2.9 Tone cue we should not ignore
> "You guys, you're young... **your mind is getting lazy because you have all these tools to help you.** We still do it the old fashioned way."

Delivered warmly, but it is a real signal. She respects craft and effort. She
praised us specifically for **manual effort**: "I really am impressed with all the
work you already put in up front... the hours spent." **Do not lead with AI.** Lead
with the work.

### 2.10 Buying signals, plainly
> "the thing that you have suggested looks more appealing"
> "it was an impressive good presentation that really gave me the feeling on how it could become. **It really made me enthusiastic.**"
> "I really want to click it to see behind it, but there's nothing." ← she tried to click our mockup
> "I'm excited about this whole thing. It really gives me energy."
> "If it doesn't work out, I'll let you know too. Won't waste anybody's time."

That last one is a gift: she has promised an honest no. We will not be ghosted.

## 3. US — what we actually said

### 3.1 Positioning
> "our mission here is to really help Dutch businesses that aren't covered by the mass kind of agencies because we find that agencies can be, well, let's just say **expensive and sounds very scary**."

Landed well against a foundation with no budget. Keep it.

### 3.2 The three pillars (Raka)
- **Grow** — customer-facing websites, portals, branding, social strategy. Goal: increase sales and marketing.
- **Optimize** — streamline internal processes with applications. Raka tied this to TW directly: "the portals that they have with partners or maybe monitoring systems."
- **Innovate** — new solutions, design experience, ideation sessions.

Ank engaged with **Optimize** and nothing else. Pillar framing is fine as context,
but the sale is Optimize.

### 3.3 The funnel reframe (Raka) — this is what won the meeting
> "not just the website itself, but how do we look at it in terms of the whole process, in the funnel... top of the funnel where Tomato World gets found, then when the prospects get interested, and of course the conversion part, which is **the booking part**... and what happens afterwards during the visit and also how do we retain them."

This is why she was enthusiastic. It reframed "new website" (a cost) into "the
system that gets you visitors" (an investment). Keep this spine in the proposal.

### 3.4 Proof points we cited
| Claim | Detail given |
|---|---|
| Unilever Indonesia | Portal for Ramadan campaigns |
| Local team payment app | Web3 interface and ecosystem |
| E-commerce store (B2C + B2B) | Built admin panel and storefronts |
| **1000+ product manual import** | Replaced with Excel import from supplier — "saves them so many hours" |
| **Gym engagement app** | Leaderboards on in-gym screens, non-advertising content |
| Gyms, brand packs | In pipeline |

The two that landed were the **1000-product import** and the **gym**, because both
are "manual work eliminated" stories. Ank connected the gym one to her own life
unprompted. Everything Unilever/web3 flew past her. **Re-weight the deck: lead
with the import automation and the gym, drop or shrink web3.**

### 3.5 The critical flexibility answer (Josh)
Ank asked the real objection — *does this replace the look and feel, or sit behind it?*
> Josh: "depending on what the website is built with, we can also build an extension that can be plugged into the website, or we can partner together with the website builder and integrate it that way. **Yeah, we can go both ways.**"
> Raka: "we can do joint work, or we can build something and then ask the website builder to plug it in... what's more important to us is that we achieve the goals."

Correct answer, correctly non-territorial. This defused Panorama. Hold this line.

### 3.6 Commitments WE made
1. Ballpark figure.
2. **Modularized** pricing — per chunk: time, money, and expected return.
3. A business case aimed at **Art and Joyce**, not at Ank.
4. ROI expressed in metrics: conversion rate, hours saved.
5. Timeline: **weeks, not months**, conditional on a tight feedback loop and staging-first.

### 3.7 Asks WE made
- Josh, twice: **what framework is the site built on?** Ank: "No, I don't have any idea." Josh: "Well, check it out."
- Raka: intro to the new marketing/comms hire; a session with Panorama Studios; a visit to the TW office.

## 4. The asymmetries worth naming

| | THEM | US |
|---|---|---|
| **Urgency** | Ank: high (2 years of nagging). Art/Joyce: unknown, likely low. | High |
| **Authority** | Sits with Art and Joyce, who were not in the room | Both founders in the room |
| **Budget** | Unconfirmed. 2026 likely gone. | We must price for a 2027 start |
| **Framing** | "A website problem" | "A funnel problem" — and she adopted our frame |
| **Technical knowledge** | Zero. "I am not a builder." | Full — we already know the stack |
| **Risk taken so far** | None | Unpaid deck, unpaid analysis. She noticed and respected it |

**The gap to exploit:** she adopted our funnel framing inside one call. She will
re-present it internally. Whatever we write next is what Art and Joyce will hear,
in Ank's voice. Write it for them.

**The gap to fear:** we have a champion with no budget authority, an incumbent
supplier with a friendship veto, and a probable January 2027 start. Enthusiasm
decays over four months.

## 5. Open questions the call did NOT answer

1. What is the actual 2026 / 2027 budget? Ank doesn't know — "I'm a little bit more on the side in the company."
2. How many bookings per month? Without this the hours-saved ROI is unquantified.
3. What does a partner pay per year? Needed to size the partner-page value.
4. Who owns the Panorama relationship contractually — is there a contract at all?
5. Does the new marketing hire see us as help or as competition for her mandate?
6. Is there any payment handling in scope? Josh flagged it as a scope fork: "do we also want to invoke the payment part?"
