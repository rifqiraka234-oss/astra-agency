## What we heard

Ank described a clear picture, and our own review of the site confirmed it:

- Tomato World's **story and brand are strong**. That is not the problem.
- The problem is **operational**: tours are requested by form and coordinated by
  hand, one person absorbs that work, and every cancellation creates a chain of
  emails.
- Your **partners pay annually** to be seen — and the value they receive is real but
  **currently invisible**, because nothing measures it.
- The website asks a visitor to work out for themselves whether they are a visitor
  or a partner.

We want to be direct about three things before anything else.

**One. We are not proposing to replace your website.** Panorama Studios have built
you a solid, professional site on Umbraco 13, a modern content system. Everything
below is designed to sit **behind and inside** what already exists. Your look and
feel does not change, and we would want Panorama involved in how anything visible
is presented.

**Two. There is money leaking right now.** As of 14 September, your guided tours
page still reads *"NO GUIDED TOURS AVAILABLE UNTIL 1 SEPTEMBER 2026."* The closure
period ended two weeks ago, but the message is typed into the page, so it stays
until somebody remembers to remove it. Every visitor who read that page in the last
fortnight was told they could not come. **We will fix this for you at no cost,
whether or not anything else proceeds.**

**Three. We would rather start small.** Ank was clear that budget is real and
finite. Nothing here requires a single large commitment.

## Phase 1 — Protect the bookings you already have

**The business question this answers:** *how many visitors are we losing to a
process that nobody can see?*

Today a request arrives by form and becomes an email conversation. Nobody has a
single view of what is booked. A cancellation is a manual chain. Seasonal closures
are typed into a page. Availability depends on which volunteer guide is free, and
which languages they speak.

**What we would build**

- Real booking with live availability, tour types and group capacity
- Closure and crop-rotation periods as calendar rules, so a page can never again
  say the wrong thing
- Automatic confirmations and reminders, and **visitors rescheduling or cancelling
  themselves** without emailing the office
- **Automatic waitlist backfill** — a cancelled slot is offered to the next group
  instead of being lost
- Guide availability and assignment, **matched by language**, so an international
  group is only offered dates you can actually staff
- One dashboard showing the week: what is booked, what is free, who is guiding

**What changes:** the coordination load drops sharply, cancelled slots refill
themselves, and no visitor is ever told you are closed when you are open.

**Indicative investment: €12,000 – €22,000**, depending on whether we configure an
existing booking platform or build to your specific needs. The Discovery Sprint
decides which, and that decision is the single biggest factor in the price.

## Phase 2 — Make your partners' value visible

**The business question this answers:** *when a partner asks "what am I paying for?",
what do we show them?*

This is the phase we would most encourage you to look at, because it is the only one
that touches income rather than cost.

Your partners pay every year to be seen by the people who walk through the building
and to host their own customers. That value is genuine. Today it is described in
conversation. **It could be demonstrated with numbers.**

**What we would build**

- A partner portal where partners maintain their own logo, materials and profile
  instead of emailing files to you
- **An annual partner report**, generated automatically:
  > *"In 2027, 4,200 visitors from 38 countries passed your installation. You hosted
  > 11 of your own customer groups here. Your material was viewed 340 times."*
- A dedicated flow for partners bringing their own customer groups
- Clear separation of your **Partners, Friends and Ambassadors** tiers, which the
  site currently presents almost identically
- A page that warms a prospective partner *before* Art's conversation — not a
  self-signup form, which Ank rightly said is not how this industry works

**What changes:** renewal conversations start from evidence. A partner deciding
whether to continue sees a number, not a recollection.

**Indicative investment: €10,000 – €18,000.**

*Phase 2 draws on data that Phase 1 collects. A limited version can be produced from
manual records first if you would rather prove the idea before funding Phase 1 —
we are happy to sequence it either way.*

## Phase 3 — Reach the people who cannot come

**The business question this answers:** *what do we sell when the greenhouse is
empty, or the visitor is in Jakarta?*

Two constraints limit you: physical capacity with volunteer guides, and the annual
crop rotation period when tours stop entirely and visitor income goes to zero.

You already publish an online tour. We think it is significantly undervalued.

**What we would build**

- A properly built **paid virtual tour** — multilingual, bookable, with live data
  from your own field lab, and no capacity ceiling. It sells during the closure
  period, when the alternative is nothing.
- A **pre-visit assistant** answering the repetitive questions groups ask before
  arriving, removing that load from the office
- Screens on site showing **live greenhouse data** from your field lab
- A post-visit takeaway for each group — what they saw, and the partners behind it

**What changes:** a revenue line that is not limited by building capacity, guide
availability, or the growing season.

**Indicative investment: €15,000 – €30,000**, and genuinely modular — the virtual
tour can stand alone.

## Telling the story better

Ank raised something in our conversation that predates the website question:

> *"I told them, listen, we need to do the PowerPoint different. It needs to be a
> little bit more upgraded professionally. And they're open to it."*

The guided tour **is** the product. It is currently delivered off a presentation that
has been waiting for attention longer than the website has. We would like to fix that.

Alongside our systems work, Astra also does brand and communication work — messaging,
storytelling, presentation design, social content and templates. A few things here we
think would genuinely help:

| | What | Indicative |
|---|---|---|
| **The tour presentation** | Rebuilt properly, with variants for schools, trade delegations and international visitors | €1,200 – €2,000 |
| **The story toolkit** | Your story written once, in a one-line, short and full version, so every guide and every page tells it the same way | €1,200 – €2,000 |

| **Social content from real visits** | Posts generated from what actually happens — visitor origins, partner spotlights, season milestones | €1,500 – €2,500 |
| **Templates and training** | So your new marketing colleague can run all of it herself, on brand, without needing us | €1,800 – €3,000 |

**One important note.** Panorama Studios offer branding, design and online marketing,
and they do it well. **If they already handle any of the above for you, tell us and we
will happily stand down on it** — we would rather you got it from the people who built
your identity than have you manage two suppliers doing similar work. We are offering
it because it may be unclaimed, not to take it from anyone.

Where we would not step in at all is a full visual redesign of your identity or your
website. That is Panorama's work and they should keep it.

Likewise, emails your *system* sends — booking confirmations, reminders, follow-ups —
are part of what we build. Newsletters and advertising campaigns sit naturally with
Panorama.
