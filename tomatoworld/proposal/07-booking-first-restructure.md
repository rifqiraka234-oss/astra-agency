# Starting with the booking platform — restructure

## First, the assumption worth correcting

Tomatoworld is **not** small, on the proposal's own figures:

| | |
|---|---|
| Partner revenue | 38 × €7,500 = **€285,000** |
| Tour revenue | ~130 × €450 = **€58,500** |
| **Known minimum** | **€343,500/year** — before space rental, events and catering |

Against that:

| Package | Share of partner revenue | In partner memberships |
|---|---|---|
| Basis €3,400 | 1.2% | 0.5 |
| Compleet €20,950 | 7.4% | 2.8 |
| **Groei €24,450** | **8.6%** | **3.3** |

**Groei over three years is €679/month. One partner membership brings in €625/month.**

So the problem is **not affordability. It is approval psychology.** A foundation with
a volunteer champion, decision-makers who meet occasionally, and no confirmed budget
cannot say yes to €24,450 quickly — regardless of what it can afford. Ank's *"too much
money, end of story"* is about **the size of a single decision**, not about the bank
balance.

**So: do not cut the rates.** We are already below the Dutch market. Cutting price
signals the work is worth less and gives away margin we will need when scope moves.
**Change the size of the first decision instead, and make the kindness visible.**

---

## The recommendation: sell the open day first

**Start with online ticketing for the 27 November open day.**

Not the full booking module. Not compliance. The open day.

### Why this is the right wedge

| | |
|---|---|
| **It makes money immediately** | The proposal's own model: 4 × 40 × €15 = €2,400/year in open-day tickets. The first event pays for a meaningful share of the work. |
| **It is the simplest possible case** | Fixed date, fixed price, fixed capacity. No guide-language matching, no fee bands, no invoicing, no partner credits. |
| **It proves booking and payment together** | Two components demonstrated in one small, low-risk delivery. |
| **It has a natural deadline they already own** | 27 November is *their* date, not one we imposed. That removes the pressure problem entirely. |
| **It is visible to everyone** | Aart and Joyce see it work in public, with real visitors and real money, before anything bigger is discussed. |
| **It fails cheaply** | If it disappoints, they have lost a small amount and still have a working ticket page. |

### Indicative shape

| Step | What | Indicative |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Open day ticketing** | Ticket sales for 27 Nov: date, capacity, price, iDEAL/card via Mollie, confirmation with calendar invite, door list for the team | **€3,500 – €4,500** |
| **B. Booking pilot** | Two experiences (Global Challenges tour + particuliere rondleiding) with real availability, live price, extras, confirmation, simple planning screen | **€5,500 – €6,000** |
| **C. Full booking module** | Remaining experiences, education and meeting packages, guide and specialist assignment, customer types and credits, exports and reports | **€4,000 – €4,500** |
| **D. Self-service & billing** | "Mijn boeking", cancellation ladder applied automatically, invoicing on account | **€3,000 – €3,500** |

**A + B + C + D lands close to the current 3 + 4 total (€13,000).** We are not
discounting — we are letting them buy it in four decisions instead of one.

**The first yes drops from €9,500 to around €4,000.** That is the entire point.

## The compliance problem we cannot skip ⚠️

If we lead with booking instead of component 1, we are **building a system that
collects more personal data onto a site that is not compliant.**

The booking flow collects names, emails, phone numbers, dietary requirements,
accessibility needs and — for school groups — **data relating to children**. Today the
site has no cookie banner, a privacy statement from 2018, and terms that are not
attached to the booking forms.

**That is not a sales problem. It is a real legal exposure, and it would be partly
ours** once we are the ones collecting the data.

### The minimum that must ride along with booking

Not the full €2,450 package. Just what the booking flow itself legally needs:

- Privacy notice covering what the **booking module** collects, why, how long, and who
  processes it
- Terms and cancellation rules **attached to the booking flow** and accepted at
  checkout
- Explicit consent checkbox for the partner-introduction question
- Processor agreement (AVG art. 28) between Tomatoworld and Astra

**Recommendation: include this inside step A at no extra charge**, and say so plainly.
It costs us little, it is unavoidable engineering anyway, and it is a genuinely
generous thing to hand a foundation that is currently exposed.

**Then keep the full compliance component on the table as a separate, still-guaranteed
item.** The cookie banner and the site-wide privacy work remain a real risk they
should close — but it becomes their choice, on their timing, rather than a gate we put
in front of the thing they actually want.

---

## Four ways to be genuinely kind without cutting rates

1. **Name the discount.** Apply an explicit **stichtingstarief** — a foundation rate —
   stated once, as a line item. Silent generosity earns nothing; a named discount is
   remembered and repeated internally by Ank.
2. **Match payments to their season.** They earn nothing during crop rotation
   (roughly July–September). **Ask for no payments in those months.** It costs us
   almost nothing in a 10-month plan and shows we understand how their year works.
   No other agency will have thought of this.
3. **Keep the change rate up, not down.** €110–125/hr for out-of-scope work does not
   hurt them — they may never use it — and it protects us when scope moves. Being kind
   on the headline and firm on the margin is the right combination.
4. **Drop the pressure line.** *"Elke maand wachten kost ≈€3.000"* is fair arithmetic
   and the wrong tone for a champion who has just asked us to slow down. Keep the
   number in the business case, remove it from the summary page.

---

## How to frame the number to Aart

Not *"€24,450"*. Say:

> **Three and a half partner memberships, once — to make the other thirty-eight
> provable.**

It is true, it is checkable, and it reframes the cost in the only currency Aart
actually thinks in.

---

## What changes in the proposal

1. **Split component 3** into pilot and full (B and C above).
2. **Add the open day ticketing step** as the new entry point, priced around €4,000.
3. **Bundle the booking-flow legal minimum into it**, free, and say so.
4. **Move component 1 from "the foundation" to "still recommended, your timing."**
5. **Reorder the package table** so booking leads and compliance follows.
6. Apply the four kindnesses above.

**Net effect:** the first decision drops from €9,500 (or €24,450 for the bundle) to
roughly €4,000, tied to a date they already care about, with the legal minimum handed
over for free — and the total we can earn over the programme does not fall.
