Dear Ank,

Thank you for Monday morning. I had expected an introduction call and came away with a proper education, so I am grateful you gave us the time.

What stayed with me most was something you said almost in passing: that if you cannot tell your story, what good is your story. That is a fair description of the gap we saw too. The greenhouse expertise at Tomato World is not in question. Getting a visitor from curiosity to a confirmed booking is where the effort is being lost.

What we understood from you

  • The booking process is the sharpest pain. A request arrives, and from there it becomes a conversation by email that one person has to hold together, and every cancellation starts it again.
  • The website does not tell a visitor and a partner apart, so it asks people to work out for themselves where to go.
  • Partners pay every year to be seen, and the value they receive is real but is currently described rather than demonstrated.
  • Nothing moves without Art and Joyce, and nothing moves without a number attached to it. We took both points seriously.

If any of that is wrong, please correct me. I would rather be corrected now than build a plan on a misunderstanding.

One thing we noticed

Your guided tours page still carries the notice that there are no tours available until 1 September. The crop rotation period has passed, but because the message is written into the page it stays until somebody removes it. You may well know already, though it seemed worth flagging, as anyone visiting that page this month is being told you are closed.

We would be glad to fix that for you, along with two smaller things we spotted, at no cost and regardless of whether anything else comes of this. We would send them to Panorama rather than go around them.

The question you were going to ask Panorama

You mentioned you would find out what the site is built on. We had a look at the public pages so that you do not need to spend your meeting on it.

It is Umbraco, a well established content management system, on a recent version. In practice that is good news. It means a booking system can sit inside the site you already have, without changing how it looks and without Panorama having to rebuild anything. Your question in the call was whether this would change the look and feel, and the honest answer is no, it does not have to.

I should also say plainly that their work looks solid to us. We are not looking to replace anybody.

What we will send next

As promised, a written outline with indicative costs, broken into separate pieces rather than one large number, so you and Art can see what each part involves and what it is likely to return. We are deliberately building it so that a first step can be small.

We are also looking into whether the Westland Digivoucher scheme could cover part of a first phase. It is written for companies rather than foundations, so we are checking whether a stichting qualifies before either of us mentions it to Art. I will tell you either way.

One more thing, because you raised it before the website and I do not think anyone has acted on it yet. You said the presentation used on the tours needs to be brought up to a more professional standard. The tour is the thing people actually come for, and we would very much like to help with that. It is a small piece of work and it does not touch the website at all.

A few questions, whenever convenient

No rush, and Art may be better placed to answer some of these.

  1. Roughly how many tours run in a normal month?
  2. Roughly how many hours a week does coordinating them take?
  3. How many partners are there at the moment?

These are the numbers that turn our estimates from a guess into something Art can actually judge.

And separately, we would genuinely like to come and take the tour properly.

Thank you again for Monday. You were generous with your time and very candid, and both were useful.
