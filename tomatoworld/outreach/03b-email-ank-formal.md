## Draft

Dear Ank,

Thank you for bearing with us. No work has been begun on your side, just as you asked.

The proposal is attached. It is written in Dutch, so that Aart and Joyce may read it without difficulty.

Our recommendation is to begin with the booking. It is the part that costs your team most, in hours if not in money.

The figure is €5,500, over roughly five weeks. Your website itself remains untouched and Panorama remain responsible for it. The booking simply sits alongside.

What it does is straightforward. A visitor sees which dates are free and what the visit will cost, then books and pays in one sitting. Your team sees everything in a single list, rather than scattered through an inbox.

If we have read your 2024 figures correctly, you received 119 groups last year. On that basis we believe the booking is worth some €9,500 a year to you: partly the hours your team no longer loses to correspondence, partly the extras, the specialist and the lunch, which guests will at last see at the moment they are choosing. It repays itself within the first year.

I should mention that we built a working version before sending this, as we were reluctant to quote a figure we might later have to revise. You may click through the whole of it. Your tours, your prices, in your own colours. It took us a day, and we shall bring it along if we meet.

The second thing we would suggest is social media: €595 a month, with €1,250 once at the outset. Eight posts a month, in Dutch and English. We prepare them; you need only approve them.

Everything else sits at the back of the proposal without prices, simply so that you can see what is possible. Letters to those who have already visited. A weekly list of leads for your partners. Being found by those who search in Japanese or German.

One of these is rather more pressing than the rest. Your website sets Google's tracking running before a visitor is able to refuse it, and your privacy notice dates from 2018. We have deliberately kept it out of the offer, since to find a problem and then sell the remedy struck us as poor form. You ought nonetheless to know of it.

Might we arrange a short call to go through it together?

With kind regards,

Raka, Luna and Josh
Astra Agency

## The caveat

**This version is at odds with two things we deliberately built into the plain one.**

**Dutch business culture prizes directness.** Elaborate English courtesy often reads to
Dutch ears as evasive, or as though the writer is hiding behind manners. Ank's own
email to us was plain and unadorned: *"So don't take any steps yet."* Meeting that
with "Might we arrange a short call to go through it together?" is a change of key
she will notice.

**Formality costs warmth.** Formal English drops contractions, and contractions are a
large part of what made the plain version sound like a person. "It took us a day"
carries less charm than "Took us a day."

**She is reading in her second language.** Her English is excellent, but "we were
reluctant to quote a figure we might later have to revise" asks more of a reader than
"we didn't want to quote a price we'd have to change later." At sixty, reading in a
second language, the plain version is kinder.

### Where the formal version genuinely wins

- **If it is forwarded to Aart cold**, without Ank's introduction. Formality travels
  better to a stranger than familiarity does.
- **If the relationship feels too familiar too early.** You have had one call.
- **As the covering note on a signed contract**, later.

### My recommendation

**Send the plain version to Ank.** She is your champion, she wrote to you plainly, and
warmth is the asset you actually hold with her.

**Keep this one for Aart**, if there is ever a direct exchange with him. He is the
money, he has not met you, and he has no reason yet to find you charming.
