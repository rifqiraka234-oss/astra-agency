## Draft

Dear Ank,

Thank you for waiting, and thank you for being so straight with us about how things work at Tomatoworld. It made this much easier to write.

First things first: we haven't touched anything. No work has been done on your side, exactly as you asked.

Here's our proposal. It's in Dutch, so you can hand it straight to Aart and Joyce without translating a word.

Now, a small confession.

We couldn't put an honest price on a booking system without knowing what we'd actually be building. So we built it. It sits on our own computers, not yours: your tours, your prices, all five languages, a guide assigned, a total that adds up at the end.

It took us a day.

You told us on the call that you wanted to click on something and there was nothing behind it. Well. There is now.

So that's where we'd like to start. Not with the website. With the booking.

Think about what someone goes through today. Thirty-five questions to fill in, before they even know whether the date is free or what the visit will cost. Then your colleague checks it, works out the price, writes the email. And does the whole thing again the moment somebody changes the number of people.

Meanwhile the things you already sell sit quietly in the background. The specialist at €75. The extra theme at €50. The lunch. Nobody sees a price while they're deciding, so most people never think to ask.

That's what we'd fix first. €5,500, around five weeks, in your own colours, sitting next to your website rather than inside it. Panorama stay the builders. We'd ask them for one small thing, nothing more.

On your own 2024 numbers, 119 groups, we think it comes back as roughly €9,500 a year: time your team stops losing, and extras that finally get offered. It pays for itself in the first year.

There's a second thing in there too, and it comes from something you said that stayed with me. If you cannot tell your story, what good is your story?

Every week something happens in that greenhouse worth telling. A delegation from Korea. A trial in the field lab. A class of children seeing a tomato plant up close for the first time. Almost none of it reaches the people deciding whether to visit, or whether to become a partner.

So we've put social media in the proposal: €595 a month, plus €1,250 to set it up. Eight posts a month, Dutch and English. We write them, you approve them. We know you're looking for someone in marketing and communications, and this isn't meant to replace them. It's meant to give them something to walk into.

Email is in there as well, described but without a price. We'd rather agree that with you than guess. A few other things we noticed are listed at the back, also without prices. Ideas, not an invoice.

One last thing, and we left it out of the proposal on purpose.

While looking around your site we noticed that Google's tracking starts the moment someone arrives, before they can say no to it. There's no cookie notice, and your privacy statement is from 2018.

We kept it out because it would look like we'd found a problem and then offered to sell you the cure. We didn't want that. But the Dutch privacy authority wrote to more than two hundred websites about exactly this last year, so you should hear it from someone. It's a quick fix, and honestly it sits closer to Panorama's side than ours. Happy to explain it to them directly if that's easier.

Whenever it suits you and Aart, we'd love an hour with you. We'll bring the thing you can actually click on, and we'll check our numbers against your real ones.

No rush from our side. Your meeting comes first.

Warm regards,

## Why it's written this way

- **Sentence length swings hard.** "It took us a day." and "Well." next to long flowing ones. Uniform sentence length is the clearest sign a machine wrote something.
- **Her words, given back.** *"If you cannot tell your story, what good is your story?"* and the clicking line are both hers. Nothing proves you listened like quoting someone to themselves.
- **No jargon at all.** No module, platform, flow, funnel, conversion, Umbraco, Mollie, script, CMS. She's sixty and not technical. Everything is in words she'd use herself.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Not "inefficient booking process" but thirty-five questions, a colleague rewriting the email, a class of children seeing a tomato plant.
- **No headers.** They made it look like a report. The short standalone lines do the same job and read like a person pausing.
- **"We left it out on purpose"** is still the most valuable sentence in the email.
