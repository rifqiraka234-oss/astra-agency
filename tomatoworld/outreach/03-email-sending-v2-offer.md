## Draft

Dear Ank,

Thank you for your patience, and for the clear steer in your last email. Nothing has
been built or started on your side, exactly as you asked. Here is the proposal you
said you would await.

It is in Dutch, so you can pass it to Aart and Joyce without translating anything.

**One confession about how we priced it**

We could not give you an honest price for a booking system without knowing what we
would actually be building. So we built it first.

There is a working prototype of your booking flow: your real experiences, your real
prices, your five languages, a guide assigned, a total that adds up. It took us a day.
That is simply how we work, and it is why the number in this proposal is a fixed price
rather than a range with a disclaimer.

You said in our call that you wanted to click on something and there was nothing
there. There is now. We will bring it to the meeting.

**We start where it hurts most**

Not with the website. With the booking.

A visitor fills in around 35 fields without seeing whether the date is free or what
the visit costs, and your team then checks, calculates, emails, and does it again for
every change. Meanwhile the extras you already offer, the specialist at €75, the extra
theme at €50, the lunch, the evening slots, are nowhere to be seen at the moment the
customer is choosing.

The booking module fixes that one thing properly: **€5,500**, around five weeks, in
your own house style, alongside Umbraco. Panorama Studios stay the builders of the
website; we ask them for one line of script, nothing more.

On your own 2024 numbers, 119 groups, that should return roughly **€9,500 a year in
the cautious column** through time saved and extras actually being sold. It pays for
itself inside the first year.

**And something for the story you are not telling yet**

We noticed you are looking for a marketing and communications person. Every week
something happens at Tomatoworld worth sharing, a delegation from Korea, a trial in
the field lab, a school seeing a tomato plant up close, and almost none of it reaches
the people who decide on a visit or a partnership.

So we have put a social media option in there too: **€595 a month, plus €1,250 to set
it up**. Eight posts a month, Dutch and English, written and scheduled by us, with
LinkedIn first because that is where your partners and delegations actually are.

**What comes later, not now**

Once the booking data exists, email marketing becomes genuinely possible, and that is
the assignment from your own project brief. We have described it in the proposal but
deliberately **left the price open** until we know which platform you use. It is not
part of what we are asking you to decide.

The same goes for the other things we found: the partner leads update, international
search, a briefing for the guide before each visit, a proper flow for schools. They
are listed at the back **without prices**, as ideas rather than an invoice.

**One thing we should flag, separately from the offer**

While looking at the site we noticed that Google Analytics starts before a visitor can
choose anything, there is no cookie banner, and the privacy statement dates from 2018.

We have deliberately **not put this in the offer**, because it would look like we were
selling you a problem we had just found. But you should know about it: the Autoriteit
Persoonsgegevens warned more than 200 Dutch websites in 2025 and is now investigating
the ones that did not respond. It is quick to fix, and it is also the legal basis you
would need before doing anything with email.

Happy to explain it to Panorama directly if that is easier. It is largely their
territory, not ours.

**Next step**

Whenever it suits you and Aart, we would like to walk through the prototype together,
sixty minutes, and check our numbers against yours. No obligation, and no rush from
our side.

## Notes before sending

**The prototype line is the strongest thing in this email.** It is also true, which
is why it works. Do not oversell it, and do not call it a bluff in writing. "We could
not price it honestly without building it" is both charming and verifiable the moment
she clicks.

**It also protects you against her instruction.** She said do not undertake any
developments. The first line says plainly that nothing has been built on their side,
and the prototype is framed as **our estimating work on our own infrastructure**. That
distinction matters, so keep it.

**The compliance section is deliberately not a sales pitch.** Saying "we have
deliberately not put this in the offer" is worth more than the work itself. It is the
single clearest signal that you are not fear-selling, and it makes the warning
credible instead of convenient. Offering it to Panorama reinforces that.

**Do not lead with GDPR.** It is urgent but it is not what she wants. She chose
booking herself in the first call.

**€9,500 return against €5,500 cost** is the sentence Aart will remember. Keep it in
the email rather than leaving it in the attachment.

**The marketeer observation is a judgement call.** It shows you researched them, but
it lands near their new colleague. Framed as "the story you are not telling", it
supports her rather than replacing her. Do not sharpen it.

**Length check.** This is longer than the last email because it is carrying a
proposal. If you want it shorter, cut the "what comes later" section entirely, it is
all in the PDF.
