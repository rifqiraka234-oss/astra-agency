## Notes for Raka before sending

**On "our team is taking a look at it" — I would not say that.** She has just told us
twice not to take steps or undertake development. That phrase, right after her
instruction, reads either as though we did not listen or as though something is
already in motion. Naming the one thing we *are* doing, and explicitly ruling out
everything else, is both more honest and more reassuring.

**Why the reply is short.** She wrote a clear, controlled email. Matching her register
signals that we understood it. A long reply would look like we were pushing back.

**Offering to stay out of the Panorama room is deliberate.** It costs us nothing and
it is the single most reassuring thing we can say to someone protecting a friendship.
It also makes it likelier she invites us in.

**The banner line is a judgement call.** Delete it if you think it reads as nagging.
My view: it is genuinely costing them bookings, it is framed as a heads-up rather than
an offer, and it quietly demonstrates we are still paying attention without doing
anything she has asked us not to do.

**Fill in [DATE] before sending.** A named date is worth more than "soon" — it gives
her something concrete to tell Aart, and it is the only commitment in the email.

**Do not attach the deck or the module pricing yet.** She asked to wait, and the
proposal lands better as one considered document after her Panorama meeting than as
attachments now.

**What this email quietly protects:** it gets our answer in before Panorama shape the
conversation, without breaking her instruction. If Panorama tell her the booking work
should be theirs, our line about sitting inside the existing site without a rebuild is
already in her inbox.
