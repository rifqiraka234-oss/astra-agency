## 1. Typical buyers, titles, what they care about and fear

### Takeaway
I found no sourced buyer-persona data (titles, org charts, size bands) for this segment, so titles and fears below are inference built from how the auction and reporting regimes work. The sourced part is the structure of the market: CfD and national auctions force developers to submit a price under uncertainty, and CSRD and UK SRS push listed and large firms to report Scope 3.

### Cited Findings
- SOURCED FACT: Academic work from Strathclyde, Exeter and Edinburgh models CfD auctions with agent-based and game-theoretic methods; one Exeter-linked study found the simulation indicates the leading strategy for a developer is to shade its bid to increase auction pay-off. This shows bid-price optimisation is an established analytical problem that developers face. — [Exeter repository](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/129793); [Strathclyde](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/81921)
- SOURCED FACT: Advisory firms already sell CfD bid strategy work to developers, investors and lenders (Cornwall Insight bid strategy and AR7 strike price forecasts; Baringa auction simulation tool for Poland). So the buyer type is accustomed to buying this kind of analysis. — [Cornwall Insight](https://www.cornwall-insight.com/?p=17852); [Baringa](https://www.baringa.com/en/insights/low-carbon-futures/modelling-of-the-upcoming-polish-renewable-auction)
- SOURCED FACT: Under the EU Omnibus I text, the reporting scope is companies with more than 1,000 employees and more than EUR 450m net turnover (several law firm summaries, search summary only). So CSRD Scope 3 buyers are the large utilities and IPPs, not mid-size developers. — [Mofo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251222-eu-sustainability-omnibus-i-detailed-omnibus); [CMS Luxembourg](https://cms.law/en/lux/legal-updates/final-adoption-of-omnibus-i-directive-simplifying-csrd-and-csddd)

### Inferences
- Account types (inference): (a) UK and EU renewables developers bidding in CfD, German, Polish, Italian and Dutch auctions (offshore, onshore, solar); (b) IPPs and platform owners with PPA and merchant exposure; (c) large utilities (CSRD in scope, trading arms); (d) energy traders and PPA aggregators; (e) asset managers and infrastructure funds owning renewables; (f) project finance lenders (secondary, as influencers).
- Titles likely owning bid pricing and tender strategy (inference): Head of Commercial, Head of Origination, Head of Market Analysis or Market Modelling, Director of Regulatory Affairs or Auctions, Head of Trading or Asset Optimisation. Project finance and returns: CFO, Head of Corporate Finance or Project Finance, Investment Director. Scope 3 and sustainability reporting: Head of Sustainability, Head of ESG Reporting, Chief Sustainability Officer. Document intelligence: Head of Development, Legal or Contracts Director, Head of Digital or Data.
- Likely cares about (inference): winning at a price that still clears the hurdle rate, not over-shading and losing, defensible numbers for the investment committee and lenders, and audit-ready emissions data. Likely fears: bidding too low and being stuck with an uneconomic contract, bidding too high and missing the round, restated or non-assured Scope 3 numbers, and consultant models they cannot inspect.

### Gaps
- No sourced size bands, headcounts or named account lists were collected. LinkedIn Sales Navigator or Companies House filters are needed to build the actual list. No sourced evidence of how these titles describe their fears; interviews or LinkedIn post analysis needed.

## 2. Main pains and their sourced costs

### Takeaway
Sourced costs found: GB constraint and curtailment spend in the low billions of pounds, rising solar cannibalisation and negative-price hours across Europe, and a German offshore auction that drew zero bids in August 2025. I found no sourced figure for CfD win rates, bid pricing error cost, bid preparation cost, or Scope 3 reporting cost, and those must stay as gaps.

### Cited Findings
- SOURCED FACT (search summary, scope varies by source): Modo Energy says GB constraint costs reached GBP 1.9bn in 2024/25, 71% of the total balancing bill, and without reform are projected to reach GBP 4-8bn a year by 2030. — [Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/gb-great-britain-battery-energy-storage-curtailment-wind-constraint-scotland-costs)
- SOURCED FACT (secondary blog, not NESO): Calendar 2025 wind-related cost estimated at about GBP 1.46bn, split GBP 380m turn-down payments to wind and GBP 1.08bn replacement gas generation; NESO thermal-constraint costs GBP 1.009bn for 1 Jan to 8 Aug 2025 versus GBP 1.383bn for the same period in 2026. A Montel report gives curtailment payments of GBP 363m. Figures are not cross-checked against NESO's own report. — [timharper.net](https://timharper.net/uk-wind-curtailment-costs-2026/)
- SOURCED FACT (Pexapark data via PV Europe, search summary): Across five core markets (France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain) solar capture factors fell to about 0.10 in April 2026 from about 0.42 a year earlier, with negative price hours up to 139 from 90. Germany: capture factor about 0.26 in April 2026 versus 0.40 in April 2025, negative price hours 123 versus 75. — [PV Europe](https://www.pveurope.eu/markets/negative-pricing-surges-eu-solar-outpaces-grids)
- CONFLICT: Modo Energy reports Germany's Q2 2026 solar capture rate rose 10.2 percentage points year on year to 43.6% on June evening price spikes, so April alone overstates the annual picture. — [Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/european-solar-capture-rate-capture-price-summer-heatwave-q2-2026-price-spikes-evening-recovery-germany-italy-spain-france-poland)
- SOURCED FACT: Spain's negative price hours were 729.5 by 30 June 2026 versus 598.2 for all of 2025 (Modo); a synertics article gives 556 for 2025, so the baseline conflicts. — [Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/european-solar-capture-rate-capture-price-summer-heatwave-q2-2026-price-spikes-evening-recovery-germany-italy-spain-france-poland); [synertics](https://synertics.io/blog/313/solar-price-cannibalisation-in-europe)
