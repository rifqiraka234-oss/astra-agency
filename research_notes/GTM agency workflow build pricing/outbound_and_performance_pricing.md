# Outbound lead generation and appointment setting pricing (retainer, pay per meeting, hybrid, revenue share), UK and Europe, with M&A advisory origination

Research run 2026-10-07. Every page below was opened on that date unless it is tagged SNIPPET ONLY.

Tags used on every finding
- **[PUBLISHED]** means the agency's or vendor's own pricing page, opened and read. These are list prices.
- **[THIRD PARTY]** means an estimate, directory listing, competitor review or vendor blog that was opened and read. Most of these authors sell a competing service, which is noted.
- **[SNIPPET ONLY]** means the figure came from a search result summary and the page itself could not be opened (403, 404 or JS only). Treat as a lead to re check, never as a fact.

Currency rule. Figures are kept in the currency the source used. Most US and many European agency pages print "$" without a currency code, so "$" is read as USD but the page usually does not say so. No FX conversion has been applied, because no exchange rate was verified in this session.

Pages that refused us (so their figures are SNIPPET ONLY or absent). reddit.com (blocked to the fetcher and to curl, 403, so no first hand r/coldemail, r/sales or r/agency threads were read), accountingweb.co.uk (403), axial.net (403), clutch.co profiles (not opened), martal.ca/pricing (403), g2.com/sellers/celliq (403), 11x.ai/pricing (404), leadcookie.com/pricing (404), salesbread.com/pricing (returned an image), stackoptimise.notion.site (JS only).

## Q1. What do outbound agencies charge on retainer, pay per meeting and hybrid, with figures?

### Takeaway
Published list prices for a managed outbound programme cluster at roughly $2,000 to $6,000 a month for a lean programme and $8,000 to $12,000+ for dedicated SDR seats, with UK benchmarks of about £3,000 to £8,000 a month for cold email and £150 to £500 per meeting. Most of the named agencies (Belkins, Leadium, Martal, SalesHive, Punch!, Air Marketing) do not publish prices, and three of the best known (SalesHive, Leadium, Danish Lead Co) explicitly refuse to price per meeting.

### Inferences
- The published floor for a fully managed programme sits near $2,000 to $3,800 a month (CIENCE team fee $2,000 plus $499 platform, Sopro about $3,800, Danish Lead Co $3,000, Leadium's lowest budget band $2,500). Anything well under £1,500 a month is below every published managed floor found, and sits in the territory of tools (AiSDR Explore $900) or very small shops.
- Pay per meeting is common in guides but rare on the pricing pages of the established agencies. SalesHive, Leadium (no guarantees), Danish Lead Co and Belkins all sell retainers. Where per meeting exists it usually rides on a monthly minimum (Reachoutly's $2,500 to $5,000), which makes it a hybrid in practice.
- A meeting price only means something next to its definition. Prospeo's own arithmetic shows a $500 booked meeting at a 60% show rate costs $833 per held meeting.

## Q2. How common are performance or revenue share deals, what percentages or amounts are typical, and what problems are reported?

### Takeaway
Pure revenue share is rare among established outbound agencies and is quoted mainly in vendor guides at about 5% to 15% of closed deal value (10% to 25% of first year contract value for subscriptions), usually on top of a $1,500 to $4,000 base. Fixed bonuses per closed deal of $500 to $1,000 appear as examples. The reported problems are consistent across sources, attribution, sales cycles longer than about six months, loose definitions and the agency pricing its risk 30% to 50% higher.

### Cited Findings

**Percentages and amounts**
- [THIRD PARTY] Performance model of a $2,000 to $4,000 monthly base plus "5%-15% commission on closed deals"; for subscription businesses "10%-25% of first-year contract value"; enterprise software deals of $100,000 to $500,000 a year at 8% to 12%. The article says the model "fits businesses with high customer lifetime values" and needs "sales cycles under six months" ([Reachoutly, Dec 2025, updated Jan 2026](https://reachoutly.com/cold-email/agency-pricing/))
- [THIRD PARTY] Performance example of a $1,500/month base, $75 to $150 per qualified lead, **$500 to $1,000 per closed deal**, and 5% to 15% revenue share. Hybrid example of $4,000/month, $100 per qualified lead above target and a 2% to 5% success fee on closed revenue. It also cites Forrester client satisfaction of 72% hybrid, 64% pure retainer, 57% pure performance, and a HubSpot figure that 68% of successful agencies use retainer pricing, but gives no citations, so those numbers are unverified ([Monetizely, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-price-recurring-sales-outsourcing-amp-lead-generation-services-a-comprehensive-guide))
- [THIRD PARTY] Hybrid bonus of "$100–$500 per additional lead, appointment, or closed deal" ([Smartreach](https://smartreach.io/blog/lead-generation-pricing-strategies/))
- [THIRD PARTY] Performance only pricing "drives costs 30-50% above retainer models because the agency prices in their risk" ([Prospeo cold email pricing](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-email-agency-pricing))
- [THIRD PARTY] Lead Pronto (UK) is described by directories as offering two models, a revenue share where it earns from sales and a traditional monthly fee. No percentage is disclosed ([Salesforge directory, SNIPPET ONLY](https://salesforge.ai/directory/agencies/lead-pronto))
- [THIRD PARTY] Fixed fee plus commission, "This amount is usually lower than commissions", and a rule of thumb that PPA can be priced at 1% to 3% of product cost, with "seasoned operators" charging "at least 10%", which contradicts the 1% to 3% on the same page ([LeadsHook, first published Jul 2023, modified Jul 2026](https://www.leadshook.com/blog/pay-per-appointment/))
- [SNIPPET ONLY] A 2026 local business guide puts performance providers at "15-25% of revenue" to offset their risk ([Clicksgeek](https://clicksgeek.com/?p=17184)); a general guide gives 5% to 30% of revenue generated, with financial services 10% to 25% ([SaveMyLeads](https://savemyleads.com/blog/other/lead-generation-commission-percentage))
- [SNIPPET ONLY] A Hacker News poster planned to pay an outside client acquirer "a starting payment of $200 for every closed client" for a digital marketing agency ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/threads?id=Eholic))

**Referral programme benchmarks (SaaS partner payouts, a different thing from agency fees, useful as a % of first year revenue reference)**

Which is current is not confirmed

**Agencies that refuse performance pricing, in their own words**
- [PUBLISHED] SalesHive "does not price by the meeting" ([SalesHive pricing](https://saleshive.com/pricing/))
- [PUBLISHED] Leadium "we do not provide performance guarantees for the outcomes of our outbound sales efforts" ([Leadium pricing](https://www.leadium.com/pricing))
- [PUBLISHED] Danish Lead Co, M&A offer "from $4,000 a month with no success fee" ([Danish Lead Co, M&A](https://danishleadco.io/industries/investment-banking-m-and-a))
- [PUBLISHED] CIENCE is the exception among the big names, with a per meeting fee "calculated from the agreed ROI goal, typically 4x ROI" ([CIENCE pricing](https://www.cience.com/pricing))

**Reported problems**
- [THIRD PARTY] Belkins on performance models. Performance "can more accurately be associated with deliverables, not conversions, and not revenue"; "there is no guarantee that all deals will successfully close"; long sales cycles, complex products, high value contracts and reputation building are "substantial roadblocks"; PPA brings "Prioritization of volume over qualification", "Higher churn rates" and "Incentivizing sales teams to over-promise"; agencies carry team and tool costs that need liquidity, and market shocks can cut close rates even when the agency delivered (Jan 2024) ([Belkins blog](https://belkins.io/blog/appointment-setting-costs-pricing-models))
- [THIRD PARTY] Prospeo on PPA. "core danger is incentive misalignment", complaints about "calendar-stuffing" and providers burning sending domains to hit volume; on pay per lead, vague definitions let providers hit numbers while pipeline stays empty; early termination fees above one month's retainer flagged as a red flag ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/appointment-setting-pricing-models))
- [THIRD PARTY] SalesBread says per appointment providers are motivated mainly to fill the calendar, past clients found quality "lower than their expectations", and cites an agency that offered Billboard ad space to get a call ([SalesBread](https://salesbread.com/appointment-setting-services-cost/))
- [THIRD PARTY] UK contract guidance. With commission or success fees "attribution becomes a major contract issue"; "A minimum term can be reasonable if there is real setup work"; SMEs often prefer a shorter initial term with a break option; watch auto renewal windows and termination fees; example lead rejection window of "five business days"; no figures for commission rates or attribution windows (Sprintlaw, 13 Aug 2026) ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/pricing-and-payment-terms-for-uk-lead-generation-agency-contracts/))
- [SNIPPET ONLY] A 2026 ecommerce revenue share guide says the revenue definition matters more than the percentage and that a client who grows "often pays more in total than a retainer would have cost" ([Ecommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/top-10-revenue-share-marketing-agencies/))

**Completion risk where a fee waits for a deal to close (US survey, the only primary data found)**
- [PUBLISHED] IBBA Market Pulse Q4 2022 (US business brokers and M&A advisers). Of respondents' 2022 business transactions, 38% closed, 14% terminated without closing, 39% continued marketing, 9% in escrow. In the last three months of 2022, terminated versus closed was 50% / 50%. Read from the PDF text layer ([IBBA Q4 2022 Market Pulse PDF](https://www.ibba.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/q4_2022_market_pulse.pdf))

### Inferences
- A fee "per signed client" is a different instrument from a revenue share on completion. It pays on an earlier, documentable event (an engagement letter) and so avoids most of the attribution and long cycle objections in the sources, but the advisor carries the completion risk, which the IBBA data puts near one in two for resolved transactions.
- The sources treat a pure performance model as more expensive for the client in total, so a low base plus a bonus is the structure the guides call healthiest (Tomba, Prospeo hybrid, Monetizely). That matches the hybrid being assessed.
- No source gives a typical fixed bonus per closed client in professional services. The only fixed figures are generic SaaS or SMB examples ($500 to $1,000 per closed deal, $100 to $500 per closed deal, $200 flat), which are far below what a sell side mandate is worth (see Q3).

### Gaps
- No survey measuring how common revenue share is among outbound agencies was found. The percentages above are vendor examples, not measured market shares.
- No first hand agency owner account of ending a revenue share deal over attribution was found. The Reddit communities that would hold these were not readable.
- The Forrester, HubSpot and SiriusDecisions figures quoted by Monetizely carry no citations and could not be traced.

## Q3. Is there pricing evidence specific to lead generation for M&A advisors, business brokers or corporate finance boutiques finding sellers, and what are UK introducer or finder fees?

### Takeaway
Only one agency was found publishing a price for sell side origination outbound, Danish Lead Co at "from $4,000 a month with no success fee" and a 90 day minimum on its M&A page ("From $3,500/mo" with a three month minimum in its own comparison article). Sell side origination is described even by that vendor as a thin category. UK introducer fees for business sales are quoted at 1% to 10% of deal value in a 2025 legal guide, with an anecdotal norm of 10% to 20% of the intermediary's own fee, always triggered on completion. UK sell side advisers charge work fees of £0 to about £55k and success fees of roughly 3% to 8% below £10m, which sets the ceiling on what a signed client is worth to a one person advisory.

### Cited Findings

**Outbound for sell side origination**

"from $4,000 a month with no success fee", minimum 90 days per single thesis; owner replies "typically begin within 3–6 weeks once infrastructure stabilises"; claims "132 owner conversations in 90 days", Agency Futures "60 days: First sell-side mandate secured", Merritt Healthcare Advisors 14 qualified founder conversations in 3 weeks rising to 46 by day 60. ### Inferences
- My arithmetic on the UK figures, as an illustration only. A £3m sale at a 4% success fee pays the adviser £120,000; 10% to 20% of that under the anecdotal introducer norm would be £12,000 to £24,000, but only on completion. A bonus paid on signature should be discounted for the chance the mandate never completes (about half of resolved US transactions in the IBBA Q4 2022 data), and for the months between signature and completion.
- The standard UK drafting points for introducer deals carry straight over to a "bonus per signed client" clause. A written list of introduced owners, a carve out for owners the adviser already knew, a defined tail period after termination (sources range from six months to three years) and a clear trigger (engagement letter signed, or retainer paid).
- Because a sell side mandate is worth tens of thousands of pounds, the generic $500 to $1,000 per closed deal bonuses in Q2 are not a sensible benchmark for this client. The UK introducer convention is the closer reference point.
- The regulatory angle is probably light for an agency that books meetings for an adviser and is paid on mandate signature rather than on a share sale, but this is not settled by any source found and should be checked with a UK solicitor before a completion linked fee is agreed.

## Q4. What do AI SDR or done for you AI outbound services charge per month?

### Takeaway
Self serve AI SDR software lists at about $250 to $2,500 a month, a managed layer on top roughly doubles it (AiSDR Scale $2,500 plus $2,500 fully managed), and the high end sells annual contracts from about $45,000 a year (11x). ## Q5. What setup or onboarding fees are typical?

### Takeaway
Published setup fees run from $0 (SalesHive, SalesRoads, AiSDR, reportedly Leadium) to $5,000 (CIENCE) and $8,000 (Danish Lead Co), and guides put the typical band at $500 to $2,000 (Prospeo) or $500 to $3,000 (Tomba) for appointment setting and $1,500 to $5,000 for cold email infrastructure. Minimum terms split the market, with month to month at SalesHive, Leadium, SalesRoads, Sopro and CIENCE, and three to four months at Danish Lead Co, Martal and Belkins.
