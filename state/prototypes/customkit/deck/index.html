02Where CustomKit is going

The brand side is where the next big orders come from.

CustomKit was rebuilt from the ground up after the acquisition and is launching two sides, customkit.uk for teams and clubs and customkit.com for brands, creators and organisations. The team side already has a working kit designer with prices. The brand side is where the partner conversations are.

Two sites, two buyers

Clubs design and order on customkit.uk from 10 garments. Brands come to customkit.com for white label, drops, webstores and brand design.

Brand conversations under way

Your LinkedIn page lists activation work with global brands and conversations with major partners, before any paid marketing.

Football, golf and padel

Interest across all three, and a founder who led partnerships and key accounts at adidas and TaylorMade.

Before a brand places a first order with a new manufacturer, it checks three things. Has this company made anything, and for whom. Will the terms hold. Who will we actually deal with. The website is where most of that checking happens.

03What a brand sees today

A brand buyer can’t see anything you’ve made.

The .com is well built and looks premium. The gap is proof. These are the three things a buyer runs into, each checked against your live pages on 23 September.

No work on show

No page names a client or quotes a customer.

The three product images under What we can produce are staged studio shots of garments with no branding on them, and none of the 51 pages on the .com names a client, quotes a customer or shows a case study.

A claim a buyer can check

The one proof line doesn’t match the record.

Why us ends with “Join 500+ brands already manufacturing with CustomKit”. Companies House shows the company was registered in December 2025, and your LinkedIn page describes CustomKit as pre launch. A buyer who looks will find the two don’t match.

Proof in the wrong place

Your real reviews sit next to someone else’s.

Trustpilot has nine five star reviews since April 2026, two of them praising you by name. They share a page with the previous owner’s one star reviews, and the second review a visitor sees is a one star from February 2025 warning people they’ll lose their money. The only proof that exists lives there, not on your site.

08What comes next

Six things turn this into the live site.

  1. Photograph real finished orders as they ship, like Jodie’s 12 rounders tops, and swap them in for the renders.
  2. Ask every customer for a Trustpilot review at delivery. Trustpilot currently shows no recent history of asking.
  3. Settle one minimum order and one lead time for each product and use them everywhere.
  4. Take “500+ brands” out until it can be backed up.
  5. Add the global brand activation work as a case study as soon as it can be named.
  6. Connect the forms to your inbox or CRM. In the prototype they show their confirmation screens but send nothing.
