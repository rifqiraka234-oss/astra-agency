## 2. Push it. What that means on this build

Simple is not plain. The flow and the wow, both, in the first thing I see.

1. **Their pictures carry the site.** They have 92 photos and two films of their own, and the best are night shots of
   their black pavilion and counter under string lights at company Christmas parties. Use them big and full bleed,
   or masked into their own shapes (the pavilion roof, the counter, the three strokes of their logo). Never in a grid of
   boxes, never under a grey overlay. Every section has their photo, their film or a real graphic in it. Use both films.
   Expect to use 25 or more of their photos across the pages.
2. **Every page has a moment nobody has seen on a caterer's site**, made from their own material. Their logo is a burger
   in three strokes and their stand is three lines too (roof, string lights, counter), so the hero is the mark turning
   into their stand and lighting up (brief section 12). That's my first idea, not a ceiling. If you find a stronger one
   in their material, bring it.
3. **Explore before you commit.** Before you build the first look, sketch three directions for the hero moment as
   stills and pick the one you'd bet the whole build on. The boldest one that still guides a planner to the enquiry,
   never the safest. Show me the chosen one built, and the other two as small stills.
4. **Light is the material.** Warm bulbs, steam off the Glühwein, dusk going to night down the page, the sheen on the
   oak counter top. "Break out of gradients" means reinvent them as real light, never delete them, and never the
   generic purple glow either.
5. **Motion with weight.** Strokes that draw and move apart, bulbs that catch one by one, chalk that writes, photos that
   hand over through their own shapes. Never only fades.
6. **Research the best first, from today, not from memory.** Render ten or more AI built and template caterer sites so
   you know exactly what not to make. Then 50 of the best sites in the world (report how many actually rendered), at
   least 20 of them award level (Awwwards, FWA, the studios behind them), weighted to hospitality, food, drink and
   events with real light and scroll scenes. Five or more premium caterers and restaurant groups for the type. One line
   per site on the move worth taking.
7. **Expensive type.** One grotesk and a quiet mono, Mona Sans and Fragment Mono by default, headlines at medium weight
   with tight tracking. Never Manrope or Syne (theirs), Inter, Montserrat, Poppins, an italic second half, or a serif
   voice they don't use.
8. **It has to feel like what they sell.** Stepping out of the cold into the warm light at their counter, at a company
   Christmas party. Premium and grown up, like a good hotel bar, not a Christmas market stall and not a food truck.
9. **The test, honestly, before I see it.** Put your screenshots beside Saavedra v3 and BWILD v2 and beside the AI
   default sites. Would this pass for a 25,000 euro site from a top studio? Would a planner screenshot it and send it to
   their boss? If the answer is "probably", it's a no. Go further.
