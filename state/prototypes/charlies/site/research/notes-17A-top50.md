## 2. How the run worked

- **Script:** `C:\Users\RifqiRakamulya\Documents\Astra\charlie\_tools\research-top50\fingerprint.js` (Node 24 + playwright-core).
- **Browser:** the installed Chrome 156 (`channel: 'chrome'`, headless). 3 sites at a time, each viewport in a fresh context, and every navigation and evaluate has its own timeout.
- **GPU:**
  - First pass: SwiftShader as specified (`--enable-unsafe-swiftshader --use-angle=swiftshader --ignore-gpu-blocklist`). WebGL 2 initialised and was verified on a test canvas.
  - That pass failed on the sites that matter most. Slosh Seltzer, Santioni and activetheory.net redirected to `/unsupported` ("your browser is not supported / you have turned off WebGL"), and Lusion, Igloo, Zentry, Resn and Dogstudio were still on their loaders.
  - The final run uses ANGLE on the real GPU (`--use-gl=angle --use-angle=d3d11 --ignore-gpu-blocklist`). The logged renderer is "ANGLE (Intel, Intel(R) Arc(TM) 130V GPU (16GB) Direct3D11)", the same path the coordinator confirmed. Every number below comes from that run.
- **Identity:**
  - Desktop UA `Mozilla/5.0 (Windows NT 10.0; Win64; x64) ... Chrome/156.0.0.0 Safari/537.36`.
  - Phone: 390x844, DPR 2, `isMobile`, `hasTouch`, Android Chrome 156 UA.
  - No other stealth was used. Because this is the real Chrome binary, the TLS/HTTP2 fingerprint is a genuine Chrome one, so curl_cffi (not installable on this 32-bit Python anyway) was not needed.
- **Per site and viewport:**
  - `goto` (domcontentloaded, 60 s cap), then networkidle (15 s cap), then 2.5 s.
  - Reject consent in up to 12 frames, then CSS-hide known CMP containers.
  - Wait out a full-screen preloader (max 12 s).
  - Detect an age gate. If one is found, save a `-gate.png` and hide it with CSS.
  - Screenshot the first screen through CDP `Page.captureScreenshot`. Playwright's own screenshot hung on heavy WebGL pages.
  - Measure the first screen.
- **Intros:** if the page offered an intro button ("Start", "Begin", "Enter without audio", "Sound off", "Explore") or a "press Enter" prompt, it was clicked once and a `-intro.png` saved. Planetoño, Unseen, Savor, Slosh, M&M's, Jellycat and Apple got this.
  - Links to other pages and age gates were never clicked.
- **Scroll:** 0.75 viewport per step, about 400 ms per step, at most 30 steps (150 s budget). Desktop uses the mouse wheel; the phone uses CDP touch swipes held before release so there is no fling.
  - 25 of the 35 native-scroll pages reached the bottom inside 30 steps. Longer pages (Apple, Shopify, Oryzo, /zeroz, sakazuki) were measured over their first 22.5 viewports, but their final height is the full document.
- **Counters (runtime):**
  - **Canvas contexts:** an init script wraps `HTMLCanvasElement.prototype.getContext`, `transferControlToOffscreen` and `OffscreenCanvas.getContext`.
  - **Videos:** total count, autoplay (attribute, or playing with currentTime > 0), and max visible at once.
  - **Fixed and sticky:** computed `position` fixed or sticky, visible in the viewport, max at once. "Sticky scenes" are sticky elements whose parent is more than 1.5 viewports tall. GSAP `.pin-spacer` elements are counted separately.
  - **Clip, mask, blend:** unique elements ever seen with `clip-path` not none, `mask-image` not none, and `mix-blend-mode` not normal.
  - **Also counted:** AudioContext constructions, and `deviceorientation` listener registrations.
- **Libraries** are recorded with their evidence (`runtime` global or DOM marker, `url` of a request or script, `bundle` string inside script bodies). OGL has no reliable marker, so a 0 there means "not detected", not "not used". `playcanvas` hits on 05 and 38 are a bare bundle string, which is low confidence.
- **First screen:**
  - Words: text nodes whose rects intersect the viewport and that pass `checkVisibility` with opacity > 0.05. This is approximate; CJK characters are counted separately.
  - H1: the first visible h1, with inline SVG style text removed.
  - CTA: a heuristic that scores links and buttons by button-likeness, size and verb, and skips nav toggles, locale and consent. It is wrong often enough that the table also gives the CTA I saw on the screenshot.
- **Walls:** a challenge-text regex, HTTP status, and a pixel check of the screenshot (max channel stdev < 4 = flat). Each wall retried once with a 30 s networkidle cap, 12 s wait and 20 s preloader wait.
- **Shader capture (technique evidence):** `tech-scan.js` reloads 32 of the WebGL-relevant sites (1440, GPU) and wraps `shaderSource` to keep every GLSL program compiled. It moves the mouse, scrolls 10 steps, and greps the GLSL and script bodies for technique strings. Snippets are in `tech-scan.json`.
- **Builders:** `config-scan.js` loaded M&M's `/configurator`, Cookie DO `/bundle-dough` and Bucks Sauce `/shop` read-only (no clicks, no forms).
- **FWA pages:** thefwa.com is client-rendered, so `fwa-check.js` rendered the case pages and read the award text. 7 were confirmed; guessed slugs that 404'd are simply not claimed.

## 3. The 50 (fingerprint legend)

How to read each fingerprint cell:

- **GL n:** canvases that got a WebGL context. "(gl2)" means WebGL 2. "1st scr" means it existed at the first screenshot. "full-bleed" means a GL canvas covered 50% or more of the viewport at the end.
- **WebGPU n:** canvases that got a WebGPU context.
- **2D n:** canvases with a 2D context.
- **vid a/b a/c v:** total videos / autoplay / max visible at once.
- **fix, stk:** max fixed and max sticky elements visible at once.
- **pin:** GSAP pin-spacers. **scn:** sticky scenes.
- **clip, mask, blend:** unique element counts.
- **Library names, then first-screen words.**
- **"virtual scroll":** the document is one viewport tall and scroll is handled by WebGL or an inner container. Otherwise the cell gives page height in viewports.

## 4. The signature moment: who does it best, and how

All technique claims here come from GLSL that the sites actually compiled in this run (`tech-scan.json`), from script bodies, or from the runtime counters. Nothing is inferred from the look alone unless it says "seen".

### Liquid, fluid, metaballs (the pour)

1. **Igloo Inc (23): full stable-fluids solver.**
   - The compiled programs are literally named `FLUID_ADVECTION`, `FLUID_DIVERGENCE`, `FLUID_PRESSURE`, `FLUID_GRADIENT_SUBSTRACT`, `FLUID_CURL`, `FLUID_VORTICITY` and `FLUID_CLEAR`.
   - That is the Stam / Dobryakov grid method in ping-pong float textures: advect velocity and dye, compute divergence, Jacobi pressure iterations, subtract the pressure gradient, add vorticity confinement from curl.
   - Same family elsewhere: Slosh Seltzer and activetheory.net (velocity stencil `vL/vR/vT/vB` on `uVelocity`), Lando Norris and Immersive Garden (divergence pass), Ponpon Mania (Gaussian splat `exp(-dot(p,p)/radius)*color` added into the target), Unseen Studio (mouse force along an SDF line segment, using `uMouseVelocity`, `uPressure` and `uViscosity`).
   - **For Charlie's:** run a low-res dye + velocity grid (128 to 256 cells) only inside the drip mask. Inject the sauce as a downward force plus dye at the top, and composite it glossy over the photo.
2. **Oryzo AI by Lusion (21): 2D SDF smooth unions in a fragment shader.**
   - `sdBox` + `sdCircle` combined with `opSmoothUnion`, plus blue-noise dithering. Zentry (26) and Immersive Garden (28) ship the polynomial `smin`.
   - This is the cheapest way to get the drip silhouette:
     - The bottle-neck stream is a capsule SDF.
     - Each drip is a circle SDF that falls and stretches.
     - The drips are `smin`-merged with a "rim" SDF that follows the top edge of the dessert cut-out (from its alpha, or a hand-authored path).
     - The result is thresholded with `smoothstep`, and its gradient is used as a normal for the gloss.
   - Done in screen space over a 2D canvas or plain DOM. No 3D needed.
3. **Slosh Seltzer (01): liquid inside a container that follows motion.**
   - A `tLiquid` transition shader with `uAngle`, `uAngleVelocity`, `uVelocity` and `uProgress`: the liquid surface tilts and lags with the can's angular velocity.
   - **For Charlie's:** the shake cup's fill level tilts with phone tilt or cursor, using the same two uniforms.
4. **Santioni Spirits (02): GPGPU heightfield.**
   - A `heightmap` texture with a `viscosityConstant`, pushed by a `tHand` texture and `uScroll` (the three.js GPUComputationRenderer water pattern).
   - **For Charlie's:** the sauce pool that settles at the bottom of the plate.
5. **Seen, not shader-proven:** sakazuki's (09) silky amber WebGL hero and Savor's (15) macro film of bubbling fat. They show the look to aim for, and Savor shows the film fallback to keep under the WebGL pour.

### Physics drops (toppings)

- **Bruno Simon (20):** Rapier 3D as WebAssembly (the `rapier_wasm3d_bg` module is in its bundle; 3 WASM files load) with a WebGPU renderer (three r183; 1 WebGPU context, 0 WebGL). The strongest physics stack in the set.
- **Zentry (26):** the same Rapier 3D WASM in its bundle.
- **Ponpon Mania (18):** Matter.js 0.20.0 (the banner comment is in its bundle) with an illustrated WebGL world. That is the 2D-physics-plus-illustration recipe Charlie's needs.
- **Slosh Seltzer (01):** lists `oimo.min.js` (Oimo.js 3D physics) in its resource manifest.
- **Lusion (22):** its hero is a pile of 3D shapes that reads as physical (seen), but no physics library was detected (custom or baked).
- **For Charlie's:** use Matter.js or Rapier 2D bodies for sprinkles, mini marshmallows and cookie chunks. Keep the body count under ~150 on phones. Render them as instanced sprites (cut-out PNG atlas) on one canvas, and sync the few DOM labels through `Engine.afterUpdate`. Give the dessert photo a static compound body traced from its alpha, so toppings settle on the real silhouette, and put the bodies to sleep once settled.

### Foil, holographic, iridescent (the golden ticket)

- **Pokémon Cards Holographic CSS (50): the most direct reference.**
  - At runtime it had 176 elements with mix-blend modes (88 color-dodge, 40 hard-light, 34 overlay), 84 mask-images, 3D transforms, and pointer-position custom properties.
  - It also registered a `deviceorientation` listener.
  - Technique (as its own page describes it, and the counters confirm): stacked CSS gradients and texture layers, masked to the foil areas. Their `background-position` and the opacity of a shine layer follow `--pointer-x/y`, and the card rotates with `rotateX/rotateY`.
  - No WebGL, so it is the robust fallback.
- **Apple iPhone 18 Pro (47): the WebGL recipe.**
  - three.js programs with `USE_CLEARCOAT` (10), `USE_ENVMAP` (60) and `USE_NORMALMAP` (62), with gltf + EXR environment assets. That is metal under a clear lacquer.
  - Its bundle carries the iOS `DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission` flow, which you need before tilt works on iPhone.
- **CIAO Energy (03):** the can uses `USE_SHEEN` + `USE_CLEARCOAT` + an env map. Sheen gives the soft rim light gold foil needs.
- **Unseen Studio (35):** an iridescent bubble in its 3D room (seen) drawn with a MeshMatcapMaterial. A rainbow matcap is the cheapest iridescence.
- **Hue shifting:** activetheory.net, Messenger and Santioni ship `hsv2rgb` hue-shift helpers, and Lusion ships `hueShift()`.
- **Gyro and tilt code shipped:**
  - Lusion and Oryzo: DeviceOrientation controls.
  - Immersive Garden: `AbsoluteOrientationSensor`.
  - Dogstudio: Android-only `deviceorientation`.
  - Resn: a `deviceorientation` handler.
  - sakazuki: 2 runtime listeners.
  - On the 390 run, 5 sites registered `deviceorientation` listeners at runtime (09, 21, 22, 34, 50).
- **For Charlie's ticket:**
  - A plane with a gold albedo, a normal map embossed from the ticket artwork (lettering, border scallops, the top hat), and an env map with soft studio strips.
  - Clearcoat with low roughness, plus a thin colour-dodge "spectral" layer masked to the foil areas.
  - The light vector (or env rotation) is driven by `deviceorientation` beta/gamma on phones, after the permission tap, and by the cursor on desktop.
  - The stamp is a second normal-mapped decal whose ink alpha animates in, with a quick scale-down "thunk". Include a CSS fallback copied from (50).
