One order carries down the page. The guest picks who they'll be, reads the night in your own words, chooses a ticket and how to pay, and the checkout arrives filled in.

VIUnderneath

What runs underneath.

We'd build the site and take payments through Stripe with Klarna switched on. Stripe offers Klarna to UK businesses and pays the business in full up front, while Klarna collects the instalments and covers unpaid credit.

01One checkout
Pay in full and pay in 3 on the same page, for every event.
02The QR ticket
The site makes it when the payment clears, shows it on screen and emails it. No RSVP by hand.
03One count per tier
Each tier has one number of places. If you hold some back, that's a setting on the tier.
04The door
A scanning page on any phone marks each ticket as used, so your team can check guests in.
05Tickets already sold
Everyone who's bought for 17 April keeps their ticket, and their names move into the new guest list so the door can find them.
06The dates
Klarna sets when each instalment is due. The dates in the prototype are an example.

If you'd rather keep your site and change only the checkout, Ticket Tailor takes Klarna through Stripe as well. We'd still pick the new site, because then the balls and the retreat sell in one place.

VIIThe retreat

The retreat, booked online.

Today the £1,795 Valkyrie Retreat is booked by enquiry, with payment plans arranged by email. On the new site a guest picks how many months, books with a friend for £150 off, and asks for her place with the plan already in it.

Stripe runs a plan like that as a schedule with a set number of payments, which ends by itself after the last one. You'd still confirm every place, as you do now.
