# BeBetter, the flow and the moments (25 Oct 2026)

The Saavedra v3 recipe (RULES section 5). The four lines are in four-lines.md. The opener promised "a more professional
BeBetter site that sells the line and has its own page where gyms design and order their custom kit".

## The action

A kit request BeBetter can answer with a design and a price. The gym's name, the look they built on the page, the
pieces, colours, sizes and numbers, a date, a contact and their logo. The page builds it with them as they scroll, so
the form at the end is already filled in and the last step is one tap.

## One kit, carried down the page

One state (gym name, city, year, motto, emblem, print style, print colour, garment colour, pieces, size run). Every
control writes it and every display reads it, the hero tee, the work order, the rack, the bar at the bottom, the stub
above the form, the hidden form field and the fallback mail. The home page carries the gym name over (?gym=).

## Why the back print

Their own custom work shares one grammar (Team Siem, Bookaleap, American Gym Antwerp, Human Sports, all from their
29 Jun 2026 post). A stone washed black oversized tee, a small mark on the chest, and a big back print, the gym's name
arched across the shoulders, a mascot or emblem in the middle, a motto under it, EST and a year. So the page's
signature moment is that grammar, live, with the visitor's own gym in it.

## For gyms, step by step

| Visitor's question | What they see and do | The moment |
|---|---|---|
| Do they make kit for a gym like mine? What would mine look like? | "Your gym" typed in the first screen | A washed black tee seen from the back fills the screen and their name arches across the shoulders as they type, in BeBetter's house style, EST and a year under it |
| Will my members buy it? | Their first gym story in their words, "Four hours later… Everything was sold out", the gym team photo, "We'd like 200 pieces", "10 gyms and 3 personal coaches across Europe" | The quote set big beside their own photo |
| Who else have they done? | The four kits from their post, on black | A rail you drag, like the rails on their stand |
| Can I make it mine? | Garment, garment colour, print style, print colour, emblem (their logo upload or a monogram), motto, city and year, front or back | The tee changes as they choose, flips to the front for the chest mark |
| How many, which sizes? | A bar per size, dragged up or down, per piece | The size run is the control, the total counts itself, a rail fills with hanging tees |
| How does it work? | The work order, everything chosen, flats front and back, the size table, the steps from received to delivered | The sheet prints as the kit settles |
| Last doubts | Minimum, timing, price, who, delivery abroad | Plain answers, nothing invented, the request is where the numbers get set |
| Send it | The form with the work order pinned and the logo attached | On success the sheet is stamped RECEIVED and slides into a black BeBetter bag, the bag from their stand |

The bar at the bottom appears after the first screen and leaves before the form. "[gym] × BeBetter · 2 pieces · 84
items · Send the kit".

## Media rules for this build

- Every photo is theirs, from their own LinkedIn posts and their shop's password page. No photo is captioned as a
  customer, a member or a named person. The gym team photo is described as what it shows.
- Their four custom kits are shown as their own work, as their post shows them. The handover asks before any gym's
  name or design goes on a public site.
- Film only if it means something, licence checked twice, no logos, never captioned as theirs or their customers.
- Their product photos are behind the shop's password until 27 Oct, so the line is shown through their stand photos
  and the teddy, and the handover asks for the new collection's photos.
- Every scene writes CSS variables or classes only and has a still state without JavaScript and under reduced motion.
