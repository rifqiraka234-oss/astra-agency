Ergebnis besprechen

What it measures

  1. Single points of failure. How long a core process runs once its most experienced person is gone.
  2. Documentation drift. How far the written process has moved from the real one.
  3. Undocumented exceptions. Where the handling of disruptions actually lives.
  4. Retirement exposure. How many key process holders go within five years.
  5. Handover in practice. What happens today when someone with key knowledge resigns.
  6. Access at the decision. Whether the right information arrives before it is needed.

Where a score lands

Four bands. Stable, the processes hold without any one person. Watch, some points rest on experience. Elevated, several core processes run on undocumented knowledge. Critical, the processes work because individuals hold them together daily.

Three constraints

Six questions, not twenty. Every question past the point of curiosity costs completions.

Ask about his week. A plant manager doesn’t think he has a knowledge management problem. He thinks one person is the only one who knows the line.

No email before the number. Asking first turns a diagnostic into a lead form, and he can feel the difference.

4 · What sits behind it

The check is the easy half. The comparison needs a service behind it.

Scoring answers is a few days’ work. Knowing where he sits against firms his size means knowing what everyone else scored. That is a different build, and it is the one that matters.

Why it compounds

Every check writes a row. The thing that wins the customer is the same thing that builds the dataset. A competitor can copy six questions in an afternoon. They can’t copy four thousand rows.

What launch actually looks like

On day one there’s nothing to compare against, and most groups stay too thin for months. So the score shows alone and the comparison switches on per group as it fills. A rule in the code, not a promise in a meeting.

5 · Where else we could help

You already have engineers. These are the four jobs we would take off their plate first.

01

Capture, the expensive step

Turning what one experienced person knows into a structured knowledge element is where the hours go. Interview recordings, calls, shift notes and old procedures into draft elements, with a human review queue on the end. The reviewer corrects instead of writing, which is a much faster job.

02

Answers that show where they came from

Every answer carries the element it came from, the process step it belongs to and the person it was captured from. That’s what turns a plausible answer into one a plant manager will actually run the line on.

03

A knowledge map that draws itself

Cluster captured elements onto the process map instead of drawing it by hand, and let the criticality scoring run continuously. The WKI stops being a number recalculated for a report and becomes one that moves on its own.

04

Alerts when knowledge is about to walk

Watch for elements that only ever trace back to one person, then fire when that person changes role, cuts hours or enters their retirement window. It tells a customer something he never asked about and couldn’t have known.

Then the unglamorous half, which is what usually blocks a pilot. Onboarding that doesn’t eat a founder’s week, routing from a completed check to the right conversation, internal dashboards, the customer portal, and the websites and apps around all of it.
