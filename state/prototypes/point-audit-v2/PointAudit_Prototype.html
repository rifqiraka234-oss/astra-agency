Why it matters

A standard usually slips in one room, unseen, and the guest finds it first.

Paper checklists live in a binder. A spreadsheet lives on one laptop. Neither travels well across floors, shifts and properties, so the gap between the standard you set and the standard a guest actually meets stays invisible until it costs you a review, a rating, or a returning guest. Point Audit closes that gap by making every check visible, scored and owned, the same way in one hotel or forty.

One binder

is where most audit history goes to be forgotten. Nothing to compare, nothing to trend.

Every shift

hands the standard to a different person. Consistency depends on memory, not a shared reference.

The guest

is the last quality check you want to rely on, and the most expensive one when it fails.

The mechanism

Follow one finding, end to end.

The same check moves through four real screens: caught on the floor, weighted into a score, turned into an owned task, and rolled up into a trend you can stand behind. Step through it.

Housekeeping · floor cleanliness

It starts as one honest answer.

An auditor walks the room on the Point Audit app and answers the standard as it is, not as it should be. Offline if the corridor has no signal. One "non" on floor cleanliness is all it takes to start the trail.

Weighted into the score

The moment it is signed off, it counts.

The audit closes into a report with an overall score and a breakdown by classification, so a single miss is not buried. Everyone reviewing the property sees the same number, weighted the same way, on the same screen.

Given an owner and a deadline

A score no one owns changes nothing.

Each finding becomes a task in an action plan with a priority, a status and a named owner. The gap stops being a line in a report and becomes something a specific person is accountable for closing, by a specific date.

Closed, and now a trend

One closed gap becomes a line you can trust.

Resolved findings feed a performance view across audits and properties. The number that started as a single "non" in one room is now part of a trend a quality director can compare, defend to ownership, and act on before the next slip.

The platform

Four moves, the same in one hotel or a group.

01

Centralise

Standards & procedures

Your own procedures and brand standards become one shared library, so the reference an auditor checks against is the reference you actually set, not a printout from two seasons ago.

02

Document

Photos, notes, voice

Every finding carries its evidence: a photo, a comment, a document, a voice note. The context travels with the score instead of living in the auditor's head.

03

Evaluate

Audit, even offline

Audits run from the app on the floor, offline where the signal drops, and close into a scored report the moment they are signed off. No re typing a paper form at a desk later.

04

Involve

Roles & languages

Custom roles and multilingual access mean housekeeping, F&B and management each see their part, in their language, across every property under the brand.
