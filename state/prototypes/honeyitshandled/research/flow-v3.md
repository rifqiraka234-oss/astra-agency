## The four lines

**1. Who lands here.** A couple, usually the bride first, engaged, in Antwerp or elsewhere in Flanders, marrying in 2027 or
2028, no planner yet. She found Elise on Instagram, on Google, at an event or through a friend (her own form's order).
The second visitor is a host or a company, who opens Events from the menu.

**2. What she's thinking, in the order she'd ask it.**
1. Does this feel like the wedding we want? Is she a wedding planner, and a good one?
2. She's new. What is this launch offer, and is it for us? (Our date, 2027 or 2028.)
3. What would she do, all of it, part of it or only the day?
4. Has she handled anything real? Who is she, would we get on?
5. How do we start, and what happens after we write?

**3. Her goal, in Elise's words.** "Wedding planning for couples who want to be fully present in every moment."
(/weddings) "You say 'I do'. I'll handle the rest." (/weddings)

**4. The one action.** Reply to her invitation, to be one of her first couples. The reply card arrives already filled in
with their date, here or abroad, guests and the help they want, and they add their names, which appear on the card in
script. Secondary, "Planning an event?" opens the Events page, whose actions are the Private Event and Corporate Event
enquiries and hiring her as a freelance event manager.

## The idea in one line

Her launch offer invites "a few special couples", so the site is her invitation: a wedding film, her rhyme "Something
Off-White. Something Blue. Something Red." as three chapters of one couple's year with her, and a reply card in all
three colours, sealed with her HH in red wax.

## One state carried down the page

`{date, noDate, year, place, guests, help, lights[17], first, names[2], email, phone, found, message, edited}`. Every
control writes it, every display reads it. The date sentence in Off White, the reply card's lines, the lit candles and
the count under them, the menu card, the launch offer line, the bar at the foot, the message in the reply, the hidden
"What are we celebrating?" (Wedding, or Destination Wedding when abroad), and the fallback mail. Nothing is typed twice.
The card's own lines are controls too, so a visitor who jumps straight to the reply can still set everything there.

**The route rule (Fae Club v3).** The first screen carries the action. The bar at the foot comes in when the hero's button
leaves, leads with their date, and steps aside wherever another button to the reply or the reply's own fields are on
screen. No half screen without a way to the reply, checked by the guide walk at 390 and 1440. On phones the bar's button
is one word (Reply on the wedding page, Enquire on Events) so their date, guests and help are never cut.

## What we don't know and don't claim

- Her prices, and the size of her launch offer. She publishes neither.
- How many couples she'll take. She says "a few", so the page says "a few".
- Whether a date is free. The page never says it is.
- Which of the 17 things she takes on a partial plan, or which she'd hold for day coordination (the page lights the
  six that belong to the day itself, the timelines, suppliers, the flow of the evening, place cards, plan B and plan B for
  plan B, as our example). The 17 are things her pages say she handles, not a service list.
- Who made any film or wedding photo on the home page. None is hers. They're stand ins, and the note bar says so.
- Her role at the Pro League Awards beyond her page's own words.
