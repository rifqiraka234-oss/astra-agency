When does the agenda have to be out?

Pick the meeting date. We'll count back five clear working days, leaving out weekends, bank holidays in England and Wales, the day you publish and the day you meet.

Clear days follow R v Swansea City Council, ex parte Elitestone (1993). Bank holidays from GOV.UK, loaded for 2026 to 2028. Your constitution may ask for more notice than the law does.

Guide

The statutory clock, in plain English.

The dates every committee cycle in England runs to, where they come from and what they ask of you.

28

Key decisions, 28 clear days ahead

A key decision can't be taken until a notice has been public for at least 28 clear days, at the council's offices and on its website. It names the decision, the decision maker, the date and the documents behind it.

Regulation 9, Local Authorities (Executive Arrangements) (Meetings and Access to Information) (England) Regulations 2012.

5

Agenda and reports, 5 clear days ahead

The agenda and the reports have to be open to the public at least five clear days before the meeting. An item that hasn't been can only be taken if the chair agrees it's urgent, for reasons recorded in the minutes.

Section 100B, Local Government Act 1972.

5

When 28 days isn't possible

If the notice isn't practicable, the chair of the relevant overview and scrutiny committee is told in writing, the notice is published, and five clear days pass first. If even that isn't possible, the decision needs the chair's agreement that it's urgent and can't reasonably wait.

Regulations 10 and 11, 2012 Regulations.

✓

The written record of a decision

As soon as reasonably practicable after an executive decision, there has to be a written statement with the decision, the date, the reasons, the options considered and rejected, and any conflict of interest declared.

Regulations 12 and 13, 2012 Regulations.

6

Six years of minutes, agendas and reports

After the meeting, the minutes, the agenda and the public reports stay open to inspection for six years. The background papers themselves stay open for four.

Sections 100C and 100D, Local Government Act 1972.

2014

Why the dates matter.

A 74 page noise assessment behind a planning report in Northumberland wasn't open to inspection five clear days before committee, and reached the public the day before. The High Court found breaches of the public's right to know, and the planning permission was quashed.

R (Joicey) v Northumberland County Council [2014] EWHC 3657
