### 5b. What the new rules ask, from the Official Journal

Article numbers are those of Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/2202. Every quote is in `build/data/clocks.json` and `make_clocks.py` fails the build if one is not verbatim in the saved text.

| Clock | Article | Starts | Airline or passenger |
|---|---|---|---|
| By scheduled departure, tell passengers | 6(1) | the delay is expected | airline |
| 2 hours, tarmac delay to the gate | 6a(3) | doors closed on the tarmac | airline |
| 3 hours, delay at arrival gives a right to compensation | 6(4) | arrival at the final destination | passenger |
| 3 hours, own rerouting if none offered | 8(3) | the passenger chooses to continue | airline |
| 5 hours, offer reimbursement | 6(3) | delay at departure | airline |
| **96 hours, information and instructions on a durable medium** | **7(4)** | **the end of the journey** | **airline** |
| 7 calendar days, denied boarding compensation | 4(4) | denied boarding | airline |
| 7 calendar days, reimbursement | 8(1)(a) | the passenger chooses reimbursement | airline |
| 14 calendar days, expenses | 9(4) | the request | airline |
| 7 working days, confirm a complaint | 15a(2) | a complaint arrives | airline |
| **30 calendar days, pay or justify** | **7(9)** | **the airline receives the request** | **airline** |
| one month, reasoned reply to a complaint | 15a(2) | a complaint arrives | airline |
| **nine months, request for compensation** | **7(9)** | **the date of departure** | **passenger** |
| 12 months, complaint | 15a(2) | the flight | passenger |
| Immediately, acknowledge the request | 7(9) | the request arrives | airline |

Other facts used. Compensation EUR 250, 400, 600 by distance, Art. 7(1). A 50 percent reduction on the EUR 600 band in limited cases, Art. 7(2). Extraordinary circumstances are defined in Art. 2(r), burden of proof on the airline, Art. 6c(2), a non exhaustive list in the Annex of 19 items in three groups. Invoking one means naming the Annex item and giving a substantiated explanation of the causal link and the reasonable measures, Art. 7(9). A third party's mandate can be demanded, Art. 7(9). Communications on a durable medium carry date and time, no account or app can be required, and the airline has the burden of proving what it sent and when, Art. 14(7). The Commission reports by 23 Oct 2031 on "the feasibility of further automation of requests for or payments of compensation for delays", Art. 17(2)(b).

## 6. Concept and art direction (spec Stage C)

**One sentence.** justclaims counts claim time the way its own product does, in days, hours and minutes against a deadline, and this site draws every duty of the new rules as a clock a claims team has to beat.

It could not belong to another client. Swap the nouns and it fails, because the tagline is "It's about time", the product's reporting writes `00d 20h 53min`, the claim screen has a "Deadline at" field, and the regulation is, article by article, a list of clocks.

**The concept controls these layers (more than five).**
- Typography. B612 and B612 Mono, the family Airbus and ENAC designed for cockpit screens. Time is always set in B612 Mono in the product's notation, 00d 20h 53min.
- Composition. A time rail down the left of long sections with tick marks and the duration of the section's clock, like a flight log.
- Colour. Charcoal and mint from the logo. Amber appears for one reason only, a deadline running out.
- Imagery. Photographs in a mint and charcoal duotone, nocturnal, calm, with clocks laid over them. The real product screens at their own size.
- Interaction. A year dial counting down to 23 Oct 2027, the claim file as five dials on a playhead, a claim clock calculator, a readiness map, an extraordinary circumstances picker, the deadline ruler.
- Motion. Hands sweep, arcs draw, digits tick. Under reduced motion every dial shows its final state.
- Navigation. A live "days to apply" chip in the header, linking to the timeline.
- Iconography. The logo's winglet is the clock hand.
- Section transitions. A tick scale runs between sections.
- UI demonstration. Their real screens, with the new duties set beside them.

**Three brand adjectives, each with evidence.** Exact (every deadline in their own screens, a law firm's founders). In control (the product's tagline of "transparency and control", workflow history). Fast (up to 90 percent shorter lead time, claims handled in minutes, from their words and the FAZ portrait).
**Three anti adjectives.** Consumer (a claims agency promise of money). Cold (grey govtech portal). Template (Wix, the SaaS default).
**One metaphor.** The instrument panel and the timetable. The hand is the winglet.
**Why not generic SaaS.** There is no card grid of three icons, no dark page with a single glowing accent for decoration, no stock handshake. The page is organised by time, and the number on screen is always a duration or a date.

**Typography by evidence (C2).** B612, designed by Airbus and the Ecole Nationale de l'Aviation Civile for legibility on cockpit displays. Its connection to the client, an aviation product used by airlines. Its job, display and reading, B612 Mono for every duration, date and article number. Character set, Latin, covers German names. Licence, SIL OFL 1.1, self hosted from Fontsource. Not interchangeable with the last three builds, which used Source Serif with Source Sans and Source Code Pro (Qualigraf), Cormorant Garamond with Manrope (Hire Quality Talent) and Big Shoulders Display with Archivo and IBM Plex Mono (CustomKit).

## 10. Independent legal read (2026-10-12)

A reviewer agent with no sight of this file read the regulation text and the page text and classified every factual claim the site makes about the rules. First pass, 169 claims, 118 supported, 39 supported but loose, 6 unsupported, 6 wrong. I checked each WRONG and UNSUPPORTED item against `research/regulation/regulation_2026_2202.txt` myself before changing anything, and all twelve held.

| Item | What was wrong | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | The three hour arrival clock "starts when the passenger reaches the final destination". Art. 2(o) measures the delay from the scheduled arrival | Start event is now the scheduled time of arrival |
| W2 | The 3 hour self rerouting clock "starts when the passenger tells the airline". Art. 8(3) says only that after a cancellation it applies from the ticketed departure time | The start event names both cases |
| W3 | "Every clock in the regulation", and "from two hours". Art. 4(1) alone has a 45 minute limit | Headings say 15 time limits and why those were picked |
| W4 | H1 "Every new duty is a deadline". Art. 11a(1), 15a(1) and 7(7) carry no time limit | H1 is now "Every claim now has a deadline", true of a claim under Art. 7(4) and 7(9) |
| W5 | The 96 hour notice called "the first message". Art. 5(1) and 6(1) require earlier messages | Called the claim notice everywhere, including the OG card |
| W6 | Asking for a mandate called a duty. Art. 7(9) says the airline "may require" evidence | "Duties" became "rules" on three pages and the readiness check |
| U1 to U6 | An inferred timeline description, "when and how" for Art. 14(7) ("whether and when"), calendar month counting attributed to Art. 7(9), a 30 day dial for a one month period, 273 days as the nine month window, "redefines" for added definitions | Reworded, and the calculator now says it counts calendar months |

Also fixed from the 39 loose items, the ones that could mislead a lawyer: the omitted "immediately, where possible" in Art. 6(1), the Art. 6a(3) scope and exception, the 30 day extension in Art. 8(1)(a) and 9(4), "in duly justified cases" in Art. 15a(2), the 7(8) citation, the voucher extension in Art. 8a(4)(a), "whether and when" for the Art. 14(7) log, the frequent flyer carve out, the Annex panel that claimed to list every condition, the distance band wording, five readiness questions, the "applies from 23 October 2027" line in every explainer, and which regulation each Art. number on the timeline belongs to.

Left as they were, with the reason. The Art. 6(3) five hour row, because five hours is the trigger and the row says so. The 8(3) and 15a "who" labels, because a time limit on the airline is an airline duty even where it opens a passenger right.

Second pass on the revised text, 183 claims, 163 supported, 19 loose, 0 unsupported, 1 wrong. The one wrong was the same overclaim as W3, left in the new-rules page title, the og:title and the footer link ("every deadline", "All deadlines on one ruler"), plus two buttons that said "See every clock" and "All the deadlines". All five were reworded. The reviewer's remaining loose items were fixed where cheap, the 30 day extension wording, "in duly justified cases" on the one month reply, "whether and when" for the Art. 14(7) log, the voucher citation, the distance and route wording, the OG card now says it applies from 23 October 2027, and the headline and chapter title now say "will have". The final round of edits was not read a third time.
