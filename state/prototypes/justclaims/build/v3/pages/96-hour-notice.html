The ruleArt. 7(4)

{{LAW:notice-96h}}

Who has to send itArt. 3(5)

The operating air carrier. Art. 7(4) is addressed to it, and Art. 3(5) makes the operating air carrier responsible for the regulation's obligations unless the text says otherwise.

{{LAW:operating-responsible}}

When it's dueArt. 2(v), Art. 2(x)

The clock starts at the termination of the journey. Art. 2(v) defines a journey as

{{LAW:journey-def}}

and Art. 2(x) defines the time of arrival as

{{LAW:arrival-def}}

The text doesn't say which of these marks the termination of the journey. The claim clock on this site takes the time of arrival at the final destination, and your counsel may read it differently.

Who gets itArt. 5(4), 6(4), 6b(3)

Passengers who may have a right to compensation.

{{LAW:notice-condition}}

For a delay that means an arrival more than three hours late, Art. 6(4). For a cancellation it's Art. 5(4), and for a missed connection Art. 6b(3).

What it has to say

Two things. The passenger's right to compensation, and clear instructions on how to submit a request for it. The text sets no required wording for either.

How it has to arrive

  • Electronically

    Art. 7(4)

    The notice is sent electronically. A letter doesn't meet the rule.

  • On a durable medium

    Art. 2(ae)
    {{LAW:durable-def}}

  • With date and time

    Art. 14(7)
    {{LAW:date-time}}

  • Without an account or an app

    Art. 14(7)
    {{LAW:no-account}}

  • Clear and unambiguous

    Art. 14(7)
    {{LAW:clear-unambiguous}}

  • In an accessible format

    Art. 11(2)
    {{LAW:accessible-info}}

What the airline has to be able to proveArt. 14(7)

Art. 14(7) names the 96 hour notice when it puts the burden of proof on the airline.

{{LAW:burden-communication}}

If a passenger says no notice arrived, the airline has to show whether and when it provided it. The answer is a log of what was sent and when.

What the text leaves open

  • The exact moment a journey terminates, as above.
  • What an airline does when the address it holds bounces. The duty is to provide the information, and the text doesn't address a failed delivery.
  • Whether a second message is needed if the passenger doesn't respond. The text sets one notice and no reminder.

These are questions for counsel, not answers.
