{{SIGN:01|Bildschirme|On its own screens}}

Every claim already carries its own deadline.

These are screens from justclaims, with sample data. Pick a screen, then a pin, to see the rule that touches that field.

  1. The deadline is on the claim.Each claim shows when it's due. Under Art. 7(9) an airline has 30 calendar days from receiving a request to pay or justify. Art. 7(9) {{TBC}} that this field can hold that limit.
  2. The amount is worked out.This claim shows a flight distance of 3,183 km, a delay of 200 minutes and an amount of 400.00 EUR. Art. 7(1) sets 250, 400 or 600 by distance and route, and Art. 6(4) needs a delay at arrival of more than three hours. Art. 7(1), Art. 6(4)
  3. The checks list their results.Duplicate booking, duplicate passenger, the manifest lookup and the carrier check each show a result. Art. 14(7) puts the burden of proof on the airline for what it sent and when. Art. 14(7) {{TBC}} that each result keeps a date and time.
  4. The scope check names the regulation.It reads "EU 261 applicable". The new regulation adds definitions of delay at arrival and time of arrival. Art. 2(o), Art. 2(x) {{TBC}} that the check follows the new definitions from 23 October 2027.
  1. A person decides when the data doesn't match.When the manifest shows no exact match, the claim asks a handler to confirm by hand, Yes or No, and links to the manifest.
  2. The delay threshold is a rule in the platform.This claim stops at "Delay below threshold of EC 261; no entitlement to compensation". The new text states the three hour line and measures it at arrival. Art. 6(4), Art. 2(o) {{TBC}} that the threshold follows the new definition.
  1. Lead time is written in days, hours and minutes.On this sample the average from claim creation to an offer sent is 00d 20h 53min, based on 18 claims. That is a figure you can set against a limit of 30 calendar days. Art. 7(9)
  2. Claims are counted by status.Created, new and unassigned, in progress and closed. The open ones are the claims with a clock still running.
