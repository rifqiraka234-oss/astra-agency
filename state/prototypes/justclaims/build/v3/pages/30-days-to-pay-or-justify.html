The first step is an acknowledgementArt. 7(9)

Before any of that the airline acknowledges the request, and the text says it does so immediately.

{{LAW:ack-now}}

Where the clock starts

At receipt of the request. The passenger has up to nine months from the date of departure to send it, so the airline's 30 days can start at any point in that window. The text doesn't say whether the day of receipt counts, and the claim clock treats it as day 0.

If the airline paysArt. 7(7), Art. 7(8)

By electronic bank transfer.

{{LAW:bank-transfer}}

Another form of payment needs the passenger's explicit agreement on a durable medium (Art. 7(7)), and the airline has to be able to show when and how the passenger accepted it (Art. 7(8)). Vouchers have their own rules in Art. 8a.

If the airline doesn't pay

It owes the passenger a justification, and the justification carries information about complaints.

{{LAW:justification-complaint}}

If the airline relies on an extraordinary circumstanceArt. 7(9), Art. 6c

The justification has to name the circumstance.

{{LAW:specify-annex}}

If the event isn't in the Annex, the airline names the event and explains why it counts.

{{LAW:event-not-listed}}

Either way it gives an explanation that covers the causal link and the reasonable measures.

{{LAW:substantiated}}

The airline carries the burden of proof for both.

{{LAW:burden-extraordinary}}

The Annex list and the conditions in each item are set out on their own page.

What happens nextArt. 15a

A passenger the airline doesn't pay can complain under Art. 15a. The airline confirms receipt of the complaint within seven working days and gives a reasoned reply within one month or, in duly justified cases, says that a final reply will follow in under two months.

What the text leaves open

  • Whether the day of receipt counts toward the 30 days.
  • What an airline does with a request with missing information, including whether the 30 days wait for the rest.
  • How much detail a "concise explanation" of the causal link needs. Recital 6 asks for plain language without sector specific or legal jargon.

These are questions for counsel, not answers.
