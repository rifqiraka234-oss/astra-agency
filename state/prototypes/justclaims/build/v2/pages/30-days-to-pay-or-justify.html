1 The rule

The request arrives on day 0. By day 30 it's answered.

The airline acknowledges the request immediately, then has 30 calendar days to pay or justify. The text doesn't say whether the day of receipt counts, so the claim clock treats it as day 0.

2 If you pay

By bank transfer, unless the passenger agrees to something else.

Electronic bank transfer is the default. Any other way of paying needs the passenger's explicit agreement on a durable medium, and the airline has to be able to show when and how they agreed.

Art. 7(7){{LAW:bank-transfer}}

Proof of the agreement

Vouchers have their own rules

3 If you don't

Then a justification, and for an exception, the reason and the proof.

The justification has to include information on how complaints are handled. If the airline relies on an extraordinary circumstance, four more things apply.

4 If they complain

A complaint starts two more clocks.

A passenger the airline doesn't pay can complain under Art. 15a, within 12 months of the flight or three months after the request, whichever is later. Then the airline has its own limits.

Drawn on one scale. A month is drawn as 30 days, and seven working days as nine calendar days, the shortest they can run.
