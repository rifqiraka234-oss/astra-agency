4 Exceptions

19 listed exceptions, and each one still has to be proved.

An airline doesn't owe compensation if it can show that an extraordinary circumstance caused the disruption and couldn't have been avoided. The new Annex lists 19 of them in three groups, and the list isn't exhaustive.

5Not arising from the operation of the aircraft. Natural disasters, weather, war.

2Related to a passenger. Unruly passengers, medical emergencies.

12Other incidents. Sabotage, airport closures, strikes at service providers, damage by animals or foreign objects, and more.

To rely on one, the airline takes five steps.

  1. 1

    Name the item

    The Annex item it relies on, or the event and why it should count.

    Art. 7(9)
  2. 2

    Show the causal link

    A direct link between the event and this flight's disruption.

    Art. 6c(2)
  3. 3

    Show the reasonable measures

    The disruption couldn't have been avoided even with all reasonable measures.

    Art. 6c(2)
  4. 4

    Explain it to the passenger

    A clear and substantiated explanation, with a concise account of both.

    Art. 7(9)
  5. 5

    Hold the proof

    The burden of proof for both sits with the operating air carrier.

    Art. 6c(2)

6 Questions

What a claims team asks.

Answered from the text of the regulation.

When do the new rules apply?

From 23 October 2027. The regulation enters into force on 22 October 2026, the twentieth day after its publication on 2 October 2026, and Article 3 says it applies from 23 October 2027.

{{LAW:apply}}
Is the 96 hour notice sent after every delay?

It's sent where a passenger may have a right to compensation under Art. 5(4), Art. 6(4) or Art. 6b(3). For a delay that means an arrival more than three hours late.

When does the 96 hour clock start?

At the termination of the journey. A journey is the flight or connecting flights from the initial point of departure to the final destination under one air transport contract, Art. 2(v). The text doesn't define the exact moment, so the claim clock uses the time of arrival.

What does an airline have to prove?

Whether and when it informed the passenger, including the Art. 7(4) notice, and where it relies on an extraordinary circumstance, the causal link and that it took all reasonable measures.

{{LAW:burden-communication}}
Who can file a request for compensation?

The passenger, within nine months of the date of departure. A third party can file for the passenger, and the airline can require evidence of a valid mandate.

What if the airline doesn't pay within 30 days?

The text asks for the payment or a justification within 30 days. Where the airline doesn't pay, the passenger may complain under Art. 15a, within 12 months of the flight or three months after the request, whichever is later.
