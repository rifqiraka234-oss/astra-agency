2 The problem

Every late flight turns into paperwork.

Under EU 261 a passenger whose flight lands more than three hours late can ask the airline for EUR 250, 400 or 600, depending on the distance. More passengers ask every year, and each claim that's handled by hand costs the airline time.

1
Spreadsheets and a CRM built for something else.Every claim gets read, checked and answered by hand, and the summer peaks need temporary staff.
2
Claim agencies step in.They act for passengers and take a fee, set short deadlines for payment, and take the slow cases to court.
3 How it works

Four steps, from claim to payment.

The passenger fills in a form on your website. justclaims does the routine work and hands your team only the claims that need a person.

  1. 1The claim comes in

    Passengers claim on your own site.

    A claim form on your website, in your brand, takes the booking, the flight and the passenger's details. Eurowings Discover's claim form runs on justclaims.

  2. 2Automatic checks

    Every claim is checked in minutes.

    • Has this booking or passenger claimed before?
    • Was the passenger on board, per the manifest?
    • Which carrier operated the flight?
    • Does EU 261 apply, and how late did it land?
  3. 3Decision and payment

    The amount is worked out, and the claim is settled.

    justclaims calculates the compensation from the distance and the delay. Clear cases are paid out automatically. Where the data doesn't match, a handler confirms it by hand, with every check's result in front of them.

  4. 4Reports

    You see every claim, and what it costs.

    Real time reports on spending, risk exposure, lead times and workload, shaped for the claims team and the CFO.

4 Why now

From 23 October 2027, every claim has a deadline.

New EU rules, Regulation (EU) 2026/2202, put clocks on claims handling. The airline has 96 hours to tell the passenger about their right, has to acknowledge each claim at once, and has 30 days to pay or explain why not. If a passenger says nothing arrived, the airline has to prove it did.
