Regulation (EU) 2026/2202. Applies from 23 October 2027.

Every claim will have a deadline.

The regulation sets clocks of 96 hours, 30 calendar days and nine months around a claim for compensation. Each claim in justclaims already carries its own deadline.

One claim, followed through the rules

One late flight starts five clocks.

Each dial runs at the scale of its own clock, from a four hour ring to a nine month one. Scroll to move the hand.

Clock 1 of 5. Art. 6(4)

The flight lands more than three hours late.

At that point the passenger can ask for compensation, EUR 250, 400 or 600 depending on the distance and the route. The airline had to tell them about the delay as soon as it could, and by the scheduled departure time at the latest.

Passengers shall have the right to receive, upon request, compensation from the operating air carrier of the delayed flight in accordance with Article 7 when they reach their final destination with a delay at arrival exceeding three hours.

An example flight, 3 h 12 min late at the final destination.

Clock 2 of 5. Art. 7(4)

96 hours to send the claim notice.

When a passenger may have a right to compensation, the airline has 96 hours from the end of the journey to send the claim notice, which tells them about the right to compensation and gives clear instructions for claiming. It carries its date and time. If a passenger says nothing arrived, the airline has to show that it did.

within 96 hours of the termination of the journey, electronically provide the passenger, on a durable medium, with information on the passenger’s right to compensation, and clear instructions on how to submit a request for compensation.

Date and time on the message, and the burden of proof, both Art. 14(7).

Clock 3 of 5. Art. 7(9)

The passenger has nine months to ask.

A request for compensation has to come within nine months of the date of departure. The airline acknowledges each one immediately, on a durable medium. A third party can file for the passenger, and the airline may ask for evidence of a valid mandate.

Requests for compensation under this Article shall be submitted by the passenger within nine months of the actual date of departure indicated on the passenger’s ticket.

An example request, received on day 41.

Clock 4 of 5. Art. 7(9)

30 calendar days to pay or justify.

Within 30 calendar days of receiving the request the airline either pays, by electronic bank transfer unless the passenger agreed to another way, or gives a justification. If it relies on an extraordinary circumstance it names the item in the Annex and explains the causal link and the reasonable measures it took. The burden of proof sits with the airline.

Within 30 calendar days of the receipt of the request, the operating air carrier shall either pay the compensation or provide the passenger with a justification for not paying the compensation

Annex list, Art. 6c and Art. 7(9). Payment, Art. 7(7).

Clock 5 of 5. Art. 15a(2)

A complaint opens two more clocks.

If the airline doesn't pay, the passenger can complain. The airline confirms receipt within seven working days and gives a reasoned reply within one month or, in duly justified cases, says that a final reply will follow in under two months.

Within one month of receiving the complaint, the addressee shall either provide a reasoned reply or, in duly justified cases, inform the passenger that he or she will receive a final reply within a period of less than two months

Outer ring, one month drawn as 30 days. Inner ring, working days.

The platform, on its own screens

Every claim already carries its own deadline.

These are screens from justclaims, with sample data. Pick a screen, then a pin, to see the rule that touches that field.

  1. The deadline is on the claim.Each claim shows when it's due. Under Art. 7(9) an airline has 30 calendar days from receiving a request to pay or justify. Art. 7(9) To confirm that this field can hold that limit.
  2. The amount is worked out.This claim shows a flight distance of 3,183 km, a delay of 200 minutes and an amount of 400.00 EUR. Art. 7(1) sets 250, 400 or 600 by distance and route, and Art. 6(4) needs a delay at arrival of more than three hours. Art. 7(1), Art. 6(4)
  3. The checks list their results.Duplicate booking, duplicate passenger, the manifest lookup and the carrier check each show a result. Art. 14(7) puts the burden of proof on the airline for what it sent and when. Art. 14(7) To confirm that each result keeps a date and time.
  4. The scope check names the regulation.It reads "EU 261 applicable". The new regulation adds definitions of delay at arrival and time of arrival. Art. 2(o), Art. 2(x) To confirm that the check follows the new definitions from 23 October 2027.
  1. A person decides when the data doesn't match.When the manifest shows no exact match, the claim asks a handler to confirm by hand, Yes or No, and links to the manifest.
  2. The delay threshold is a rule in the platform.This claim stops at "Delay below threshold of EC 261; no entitlement to compensation". The new text states the three hour line and measures it at arrival. Art. 6(4), Art. 2(o) To confirm that the threshold follows the new definition.
  1. Lead time is written in days, hours and minutes.On this sample the average from claim creation to an offer sent is 00d 20h 53min, based on 18 claims. That is a figure you can set against a limit of 30 calendar days. Art. 7(9)
  2. Claims are counted by status.Created, new and unassigned, in progress and closed. The open ones are the claims with a clock still running.
