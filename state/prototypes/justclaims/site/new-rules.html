The new rules

What changes on 23 October 2027.

Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 amends Regulation (EC) No 261/2004. It enters into force on 22 October 2026 and applies from 23 October 2027. This page puts the time limits that touch a claim, the rules and the dates in one place, each with its article number.

The time limits that touch a claim

15 time limits, on one ruler.

These are 15 of the time limits in Regulation (EU) 2026/2202, picked because they touch a claim or a passenger's rights, and placed by how long each one runs. Airline duties sit above the line and passenger rights below it. Pick one to read the sentence it comes from.

Rules that aren't time limits

Seven rules that decide what an airline can prove.

Each comes with the sentence it comes from, word for word.

  • Electronic messages carry the date and time

    Art. 14(7)

    Communications and documents sent by electronic means go on a durable medium and carry the date and time. No passenger can be required to have an account or an app to access or receive them, except for frequent flyer and other commercial programmes.

    they shall include the date and timea passenger is not obliged to have a user account or a specific application as a precondition for accessing or receiving information

  • The airline proves whether and when it informed the passenger

    Art. 14(7)

    That includes the 96 hour notice of Art. 7(4).

    The burden of proof with regard to establishing whether and when they have provided the necessary information, correspondence, forms or documents

  • Pay by electronic bank transfer

    Art. 7(7), Art. 7(8)

    Other means need the passenger's explicit agreement on a durable medium, and the airline has to be able to show when and how they agreed.

    The compensation shall be paid by electronic bank transfer or, if explicitly agreed by the passenger on a durable medium, by other means.

  • A voucher only with explicit consent

    Art. 8a

    A voucher has to be worth at least the compensation due, lasts up to 12 months, which both sides can extend once by up to 12 more, and any unused balance is reimbursed within seven calendar days of the end.

    Acceptance shall be valid only if the passenger has given explicit consent on a durable medium.

  • Name the extraordinary circumstance

    Art. 7(9), Art. 6c

    An airline that relies on one names the item in the Annex, or the event and why it counts, and explains the causal link and the reasonable measures.

    it shall specify which extraordinary circumstance listed in the Annex it invokes

  • Ask for the mandate

    Art. 7(9)

    A third party can request compensation for a passenger. The airline can ask for proof that it may.

    the operating air carrier may require evidence of a valid mandate to act

  • Keep a complaint route open

    Art. 15a

    Every airline and intermediary sets up a complaint handling mechanism. A passenger the airline doesn't pay can use it.

    Where the operating air carrier does not pay the requested compensation, the passenger may submit a complaint in accordance with Article 15a.

Questions a claims team asks

Answered from the text of the regulation.

When do the new rules apply?

From 23 October 2027. The regulation enters into force on 22 October 2026, the twentieth day after its publication on 2 October 2026, and Article 3 says it applies from 23 October 2027.

It shall apply from 23 October 2027.
Is the 96 hour notice sent after every delay?

It is sent where a passenger may have a right to compensation under Art. 5(4), Art. 6(4) or Art. 6b(3). For a delay that means an arrival more than three hours late.

When does the 96 hour clock start?

At the termination of the journey. A journey is the flight or connecting flights from the initial point of departure to the final destination under one air transport contract, Art. 2(v). The text doesn't define the exact moment, so the claim clock uses the time of arrival.

What does an airline have to prove?

Whether and when it informed the passenger, including the Art. 7(4) notice, and where it relies on an extraordinary circumstance, the causal link and that it took all reasonable measures.

The burden of proof with regard to establishing whether and when they have provided the necessary information, correspondence, forms or documents
Who can file a request for compensation?

The passenger, within nine months of the date of departure. A third party can file for the passenger, and the airline can require evidence of a valid mandate.

What if the airline doesn't pay within 30 days?

The text asks for the payment or a justification within 30 days. Where the airline doesn't pay, the passenger may complain under Art. 15a, within 12 months of the flight or three months after the request, whichever is later.
