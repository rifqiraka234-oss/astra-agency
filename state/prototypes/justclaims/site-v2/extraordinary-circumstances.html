Explainer, Art. 6c and the Annex

Extraordinary circumstances, and who proves them.

The new Annex lists 19 circumstances that count as extraordinary. An airline that wants to rely on one still has to show that it caused the disruption, and that nothing reasonable would have avoided it.

Background, stock photograph.

1 What counts

Outside the normal running of an airline, and beyond its control.

Art. 2(r) sets two conditions, and Recital 5 says both have to be met. The Annex then lists 19 events in three groups. The list isn't exhaustive, Art. 6c(1), and each entry carries its own conditions.

Art. 2(r)events which by their nature or origin are not inherent in the normal exercise of the activity of the air carrier concerned and are beyond the actual control of the air carrier
Recital 5Those two conditions are cumulative.

5Not arising from the operation of the aircraft. Natural disasters, weather, war.

2Related to a passenger. Unruly passengers, medical emergencies.

12Other incidents. Sabotage, airport closures, strikes at service providers, damage by animals or foreign objects, and more.

2 What to show

Five steps to rely on one, and the proof sits with the airline.

Art. 6c(2) sets what has to be shown, and Art. 7(9) what the passenger is told. Recital 6 asks for it in plain language.

  1. 1

    Name the item

    The Annex item it relies on, or the event and why it should count.

    Art. 7(9)
  2. 2

    Show the causal link

    A direct link between the event and this flight's disruption.

    Art. 6c(2)
  3. 3

    Show the reasonable measures

    The disruption couldn't have been avoided even with all reasonable measures.

    Art. 6c(2)
  4. 4

    Explain it to the passenger

    A clear and substantiated explanation, with a concise account of both.

    Art. 7(9)
  5. 5

    Hold the proof

    The burden of proof for both sits with the operating air carrier.

    Art. 6c(2)
Art. 6c(2)Extraordinary circumstances may only be invoked where there is a direct causal link between the occurrence of that circumstance and the disruption to the flight.The burden of proof with regard to establishing the existence of that direct causal link and establishing that the air carrier has taken all reasonable measures to avoid the disruption shall rest with the operating air carrier.
Recital 6Air carriers should provide that information in a concise manner using plain language, avoiding sector-specific and legal jargon, that is easily understandable to the passenger.

4 Two more rules

A limit on hotel nights, and a review in 2031.

Art. 9(6), Art. 11(10)

Three hotel nights at most

When an extraordinary circumstance causes the disruption and nothing reasonable would have avoided it, the airline may limit hotel accommodation. The limit doesn't apply to passengers with specific needs.

the air carrier may limit the hotel accommodation provided in accordance with paragraph 2, point (a), to a maximum of three nights
Art. 17(1)

The list is reviewed

The Commission reports on the Annex by 23 October 2031, and every five years after that.

By 23 October 2031 and every five years thereafter, the Commission shall report to the European Parliament and the Council on the list of extraordinary circumstances set out in the Annex
