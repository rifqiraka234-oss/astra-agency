The new rules

What changes on 23 October 2027.

Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 amends EU 261. From 23 October 2027 a late flight starts a set of clocks for the airline, and the airline has to be able to prove what it sent and when.

1 One late flight

One late flight starts five clocks.

Follow a single delayed flight through the new rules. Each dial runs at the scale of its own clock, from a four hour ring to a nine month one. Scroll to move the hand.

Clock 1 of 5. Art. 6(4)

The flight lands more than three hours late.

At that point the passenger can ask for compensation, EUR 250, 400 or 600 depending on the distance and the route. The airline had to tell them about the delay as soon as it could, and by the scheduled departure time at the latest.

Passengers shall have the right to receive, upon request, compensation from the operating air carrier of the delayed flight in accordance with Article 7 when they reach their final destination with a delay at arrival exceeding three hours.

An example flight, 3 h 12 min late at the final destination.

Clock 2 of 5. Art. 7(4)

96 hours to send the claim notice.

When a passenger may have a right to compensation, the airline has 96 hours from the end of the journey to send the claim notice, which tells them about the right to compensation and gives clear instructions for claiming. It carries its date and time. If a passenger says nothing arrived, the airline has to show that it did.

within 96 hours of the termination of the journey, electronically provide the passenger, on a durable medium, with information on the passenger’s right to compensation, and clear instructions on how to submit a request for compensation.

Date and time on the message, and the burden of proof, both Art. 14(7).

Clock 3 of 5. Art. 7(9)

The passenger has nine months to ask.

A request for compensation has to come within nine months of the date of departure. The airline acknowledges each one immediately, on a durable medium. A third party can file for the passenger, and the airline may ask for evidence of a valid mandate.

Requests for compensation under this Article shall be submitted by the passenger within nine months of the actual date of departure indicated on the passenger’s ticket.

An example request, received on day 41.

Clock 4 of 5. Art. 7(9)

30 calendar days to pay or justify.

Within 30 calendar days of receiving the request the airline either pays, by electronic bank transfer unless the passenger agreed to another way, or gives a justification. If it relies on an extraordinary circumstance it names the item in the Annex and explains the causal link and the reasonable measures it took. The burden of proof sits with the airline.

Within 30 calendar days of the receipt of the request, the operating air carrier shall either pay the compensation or provide the passenger with a justification for not paying the compensation

Annex list, Art. 6c and Art. 7(9). Payment, Art. 7(7).

Clock 5 of 5. Art. 15a(2)

A complaint opens two more clocks.

If the airline doesn't pay, the passenger can complain. The airline confirms receipt within seven working days and gives a reasoned reply within one month or, in duly justified cases, says that a final reply will follow in under two months.

Within one month of receiving the complaint, the addressee shall either provide a reasoned reply or, in duly justified cases, inform the passenger that he or she will receive a final reply within a period of less than two months

Outer ring, one month drawn as 30 days. Inner ring, working days.

  2. 96 hours
  3. 9 months
  4. 30 days
  5. 1 month

3 What you prove

Seven rules that decide what an airline can prove.

They aren't time limits, but each one leaves a record behind it. Every card quotes the sentence it comes from, word for word.

Art. 14(7)

The airline proves whether and when it informed the passenger

That includes the 96 hour notice. If a passenger says nothing arrived, the airline has to show that it did.

The burden of proof with regard to establishing whether and when they have provided the necessary information, correspondence, forms or documents
Art. 14(7)

Electronic messages carry the date and time

On a durable medium, and no passenger has to have an account or an app to receive them.

they shall include the date and time
Art. 7(7), Art. 7(8)

Pay by electronic bank transfer

Any other way needs the passenger's explicit agreement on a durable medium, and proof of when and how they agreed.

The compensation shall be paid by electronic bank transfer or, if explicitly agreed by the passenger on a durable medium, by other means.
Art. 8a

A voucher only with explicit consent

Worth at least the compensation due, valid up to 12 months, with any unused balance paid back.

Acceptance shall be valid only if the passenger has given explicit consent on a durable medium.
Art. 7(9)

Ask for the mandate

A third party can file for a passenger. The airline can ask for proof that it may.

the operating air carrier may require evidence of a valid mandate to act
Art. 15a

Keep a complaint route open

Every airline and intermediary sets up a way to complain. A passenger the airline doesn't pay can use it.

Where the operating air carrier does not pay the requested compensation, the passenger may submit a complaint in accordance with Article 15a.
Art. 7(9), Art. 6c

Name the extraordinary circumstance

An airline that relies on one names the item in the Annex, or the event and why it counts, and explains the causal link and the reasonable measures it took.

it shall specify which extraordinary circumstance listed in the Annex it invokes
4 Exceptions

19 listed exceptions, and each one still has to be proved.

An airline doesn't owe compensation if it can show that an extraordinary circumstance caused the disruption and couldn't have been avoided. The new Annex lists 19 of them in three groups, and the list isn't exhaustive.

5Not arising from the operation of the aircraft. Natural disasters, weather, war.

2Related to a passenger. Unruly passengers, medical emergencies.

12Other incidents. Sabotage, airport closures, strikes at service providers, damage by animals or foreign objects, and more.

To rely on one, the airline takes five steps.

  1. 1

    Name the item

    The Annex item it relies on, or the event and why it should count.

    Art. 7(9)
  2. 2

    Show the causal link

    A direct link between the event and this flight's disruption.

    Art. 6c(2)
  3. 3

    Show the reasonable measures

    The disruption couldn't have been avoided even with all reasonable measures.

    Art. 6c(2)
  4. 4

    Explain it to the passenger

    A clear and substantiated explanation, with a concise account of both.

    Art. 7(9)
  5. 5

    Hold the proof

    The burden of proof for both sits with the operating air carrier.

    Art. 6c(2)

6 Questions

What a claims team asks.

Answered from the text of the regulation.

When do the new rules apply?

From 23 October 2027. The regulation enters into force on 22 October 2026, the twentieth day after its publication on 2 October 2026, and Article 3 says it applies from 23 October 2027.

It shall apply from 23 October 2027.
Is the 96 hour notice sent after every delay?

It's sent where a passenger may have a right to compensation under Art. 5(4), Art. 6(4) or Art. 6b(3). For a delay that means an arrival more than three hours late.

When does the 96 hour clock start?

At the termination of the journey. A journey is the flight or connecting flights from the initial point of departure to the final destination under one air transport contract, Art. 2(v). The text doesn't define the exact moment, so the claim clock uses the time of arrival.

What does an airline have to prove?

Whether and when it informed the passenger, including the Art. 7(4) notice, and where it relies on an extraordinary circumstance, the causal link and that it took all reasonable measures.

The burden of proof with regard to establishing whether and when they have provided the necessary information, correspondence, forms or documents
Who can file a request for compensation?

The passenger, within nine months of the date of departure. A third party can file for the passenger, and the airline can require evidence of a valid mandate.

What if the airline doesn't pay within 30 days?

The text asks for the payment or a justification within 30 days. Where the airline doesn't pay, the passenger may complain under Art. 15a, within 12 months of the flight or three months after the request, whichever is later.
