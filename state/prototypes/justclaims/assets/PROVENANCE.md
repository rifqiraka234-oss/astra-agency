# Provenance for stock imagery and loops

Accessed 2026-10-12. Everything here is decorative atmosphere. None of it shows, or may be captioned as, the client's premises, staff, customers or product. Label it as stock on the page.

## What was delivered

- `stock/photos/` 9 JPEGs, 1600 px wide, quality 78, progressive. Largest file 296 KB.
- `stock/film/` 4 H.264 MP4 loops, 1280x720, no audio, 8 to 10 seconds, faststart, each with a 1280 px poster JPEG. Largest file 2.2 MB.
- `stock/contact-sheets/` one sheet for the photos and one frame strip per loop, for fast review.
- `REJECTED.md` the candidates I looked at and turned down, with reasons.

## Licences, quoted from the pages

**Pexels.** Every photo item page carries `"license":"Pexels"` in its page JSON, and its JSON-LD points to https://www.pexels.com/license/ . That page says: "All photos and videos on Pexels are free to use. Attribution is not required." and "You can modify the photos and videos from Pexels." It also says: "Don't sell unaltered copies of a photo or video" and "Don't imply endorsement of your product by people or brands on the imagery." and "Identifiable people may not appear in a bad light". Nothing in this set shows an identifiable person.

**Mixkit.** Each clip's JSON-LD reads `"copyrightNotice":"Free"`, `"isAccessibleForFree":true` and `"license":"https://mixkit.co/license/#videoFree"`. The page text reads "Download this free stock video clip for commercial or personal use, under the Mixkit Stock Video Free License". Mixkit also sells a second class, `Mixkit Restricted License` (personal use). I read 938 Mixkit clip pages and 376 of them carried the Restricted notice, so the field was checked on each of the four clips used, not assumed from the site front page. Mixkit's item pages do not name a creator.

## Anything that could mislead

- Photo 05 shows the type designation 'A320' and stand number '20'. No airline.
- Photo 04 is a crop. The full Pexels image shows airline liveries. Keep the crop.
- Photo 03 has a red glow that is a navigation light, not a livery.
- Loop 02 has a faint, unreadable mark on the winglet tip. If a lawyer wants zero risk of a livery reading, drop this loop and keep 01.
- Photos 06, 07 and 08 and loop 04 show rain on glass with no location. Do not caption them as an airport.
- No frame in the delivered set shows a face. Posters and photos were checked by eye; the automated face detector fired only on gears and false positives in other candidates.

## Counts and honest limits

- Pexels: 2,258 photo records and 933 video records harvested from search pages. About 210 photo thumbnails (night apron, wing, runway and rain) were viewed by eye on contact sheets. 13 photos were opened at full size or large overview, 9 delivered, 4 rejected at that stage (REJECTED.md).
- Mixkit: 938 clip pages read, 562 Free and 376 Restricted. About 20 thumbnails viewed by eye on one contact sheet (the other three sheets came back from my image viewer stripped). 4 clips delivered, each then checked frame by frame as described above.
- Pixabay: video search listings parsed, about 2,000 rows, 40 or so thumbnails viewed. Nothing delivered: the photo CDN stops at 1280 px without a login, and I ran out of time to clear a video.
- Pexels videos: 10 frame strips viewed by eye (the rest came back stripped, so no decision was taken on them). None delivered; the ones that looked usable (airport hall time-lapses, hands signing) were not cleared at full size.
- The first pass over the departures hall, tower, baggage, cockpit, clock, stamp, office and cabin photo sets came back from my image viewer with the pictures stripped. I threw away every decision made on those and did not use them, so those subjects are absent. This is a limit of the session, not a finding about the sites.
- Hosts: Pexels, Pixabay and Mixkit answered, but Pexels and Pixabay gave the Cloudflare 'Just a moment' wall to Chrome and Firefox style TLS fingerprints and let Safari-style ones through (the fetch-walled.py approach, with the Safari fingerprints tried first). Unsplash stayed walled behind an Anubis proof-of-work page (HTTP 401) and I did not try to solve it. Wikimedia Commons, NASA and the Internet Archive were reachable but I did not get to them.
