Researched 12 October 2026 for the page on Regulation (EU) 2026/2202. Every row in section 1 was opened at its primary source in this session. Where a number is my own arithmetic on cited numbers, the row says so and shows the sum. Nothing here is modelled, projected or estimated by me.

**64 usable facts** in section 1. Sections 2 to 4 list what is second hand, what could not be opened and what was searched for and not found. Section 5 lists places where two official sources disagree. Section 6 maps every saved file.

## Read this first (the eight cautions that matter most)

1. **There is no official EU-wide count of EU261 claims.** The Commission's own consultant says "there is no publicly and reliable information available on the number of passengers who try to claim compensation" (D01). What exists is (a) consultant estimates of claim rates (D01 to D03, latest year 2018), (b) real counts from German courts (D30 to D39), and (c) volumes at complaint bodies (D09, D40). A chart of "claims per year across the EU" cannot be supported.
2. **German court numbers come in two kinds that must not be mixed.** Destatis counts concluded cases (D30 to D32, D37 to D39). The Deutscher Richterbund's journal counts new filings at the 20 largest airport courts (D34 to D36).
3. **The Frankfurt court is not isolated in the official statistics.** Destatis gives the state of Hesse (D32). The Amtsgericht Frankfurt am Main appears only in a 2023 ministry statement (about 15,000 a year, D33) and in the Richterbund journal (about 16,000 new in 2024, D34; 8,314 in the first half of 2026, D36).
4. **Airline-side cost numbers (EUR 8 billion, EUR 8.1 billion) are advocacy with no stated method** (D51, D52). The Commission-side estimates are older (D08 for 2018, D50 for a 2013 projection) and use different methods. Do not put them on one axis.
5. **The new Regulation itself contains no common form for compensation requests.** A companion enforcement regulation does (D81), but it is only a political agreement, not adopted as of 12 October 2026 (D83). Say "agreed in principle, pending".
6. **EUROCONTROL's own documents differ slightly** on the 2025 departure delay (14.7 or 14.6 minutes) and on arrival and departure punctuality. Quote one document per chart (section 5).
7. **Eurostat revises earlier years.** Use passenger totals from a single pull (D26).
8. **Dates:** entry into force is 22 October 2026 (the twentieth day after the 2 October publication); the rules apply from 23 October 2027 (D69, D70). Press summaries that say airlines have "14 days" to pay conflict with the Official Journal, which says 30 calendar days (D76).
