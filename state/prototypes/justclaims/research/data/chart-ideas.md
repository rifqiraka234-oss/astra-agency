# Chart and graphic ideas for the justclaims.eu story

Every idea below can be drawn only from rows marked usable in `FACTS.md`. Exact series are in `chart-data.json`. Fact ids are in brackets. Nothing here needs a number I did not source, and no idea needs a forecast or a conversion rate.

## The honest answer on claim volume first

**No chart of "how many EU261 claims airlines receive" can be supported.** No official EU-wide count exists. The Commission's own consultant says "there is no publicly and reliable information available on the number of passengers who try to claim compensation" (D01). The only real counts are German court cases (D30 to D39), complaints at conciliation and enforcement bodies (D09, D40, D42), and a consultant's estimate of the claim rate that stops in 2018 (D01, D02). Ideas 2 to 4 below use those and say so on the chart. Do not add a "claims per year in Europe" tile, and do not multiply the 2018 claim rate by any later passenger number.

## The eight ideas

### 1. The 13-year reform, drawn as a rail line to the date the clocks start
- **Form:** horizontal timeline, 2013 to 2027, with the long gap 2014 to 2025 visible, then a dense cluster from 2025 to 2026, ending on two marked dates: entry into force 22 Oct 2026 and application 23 Oct 2027. A small second marker for 23 Oct 2031 (Commission review, including automation of claims).
- **Facts:** D60, D61, D62, D63, D64, D65, D66, D67, D68, D69, D70, D80.
- **The one sentence it proves:** The Commission proposed this reform on 13 March 2013, it was signed on 16 September 2026 (13 years, 6 months and 3 days later, my arithmetic), and the claims clocks start running on 23 October 2027.
- **Labels to keep:** the 24 March and 20 April 2026 steps come from the Parliament's procedure page only (D64). For the Council first reading say 29 September 2025 (Official Journal), not the 2 October publication date (D62).

### 2. German flight-claim cases, 2021 to 2025, as a share of the whole civil docket
- **Form:** bars for concluded cases (28,514; 55,887; 108,092; 134,496; 123,938) with a line or labels for the share of all civil cases concluded at district courts (3.6%; 7.8%; 14.4%; 16.8%; 15.1%).
- **Facts:** D30, D31. Optional inset for Hesse: 8,944; 15,618; 15,081; 20,750 (D32).
- **The one sentence it proves:** German district courts concluded 28,514 flight-passenger cases in 2021 and 134,496 in 2024, and in 2025 they were still 15.1% of every civil case they concluded.
- **Labels to keep:** call the bars "concluded cases", never "filings" or "claims". The 2025 dip is real in the concluded count (-10,558). A second, separate stat tile can carry the Richterbund's new-filings figures: 53,150 at the 20 largest airport courts in the first half of 2026, 20% down (D35), and Amtsgericht Frankfurt 8,314 in the same half (D36). Never plot those tiles on the bar axis.

### 3. Frankfurt, in three honest numbers
- **Form:** three stat tiles, no chart axis: "about 15,000 mass proceedings a year" (ministry, 2023, D33); "about 16,000 new in 2024" (Richterbund journal, D34); "8,314 in the first half of 2026, about half of all civil cases are flight and travel matters" (D36).
- **Facts:** D33, D34, D36, with D32 as context ("Hesse as a whole: 20,750 concluded in 2025").
- **The one sentence it proves:** The Amtsgericht Frankfurt is the second busiest flight-claims court in Germany, and about half of its civil docket is flight and travel cases.
- **Labels to keep:** three different sources and measures, each tile names its source and date. Do not turn them into a trend line.

### 4. The claim rate, 2011 to 2018 (a consultant's estimate, labelled as such)
- **Form:** line of the weighted-average claim rate: 8.0, 10.1, 11.6, 15.0, 20.6, 26.4, 28.9, 37.5 (%). Two end annotations for 2018: delays 58%, cancellations 18%. A grey marker at the start: "2013 impact assessment: airlines said 5-10%".
- **Facts:** D01, D02, D03.
- **The one sentence it proves:** The Commission's own consultant estimated that the share of eligible passengers who claimed and were paid rose from 8% in 2011 to 37.5% in 2018.
- **Labels to keep:** "estimate, Steer for the European Commission, January 2020"; the series ends in 2018 and there is no newer figure; "eligible" excludes extraordinary circumstances. Do not extend the line.

### 5. How a flight-claim case ends in court (2025) and how long it takes
- **Form:** one horizontal stacked bar of 123,938 cases: § 91a cost decision after both sides declare the matter resolved 37.2%; contested judgment 28.0%; acknowledgement or waiver judgment 15.5%; claim withdrawn 8.1%; default judgment 5.3%; court settlement 1.3%; other 4.6%. Beneath it, a second bar for duration: up to 3 months 42.0%, 3 to 6 months 32.7%, 6 to 12 months 19.3%, 12 to 24 months 5.2%, over 24 months 0.7%; average 4.8 months.
- **Facts:** D37, D38 (optionally D39: both sides had lawyers in 81.9% of cases).
- **The one sentence it proves:** Only about 28% of flight-claim cases that reach a German district court end in a contested judgment; the average case takes 4.8 months.
- **Labels to keep:** these are court outcomes, not airline behaviour. Do not write "airlines pay after being sued"; the statistic does not say who paid whom.

### 6. The new clocks, set against the old ones
- **Form:** a horizontal ladder of durations on a log or broken scale: new fixed clocks (96 hours to inform; 7 calendar days automatic reimbursement; 7 working days to acknowledge a complaint; 30 calendar days to pay or justify; 1 month for a complaint reply; 9 months for the passenger to file; 12 months to complain) beside the two old clocks with a source: airlines' self-set reply targets of 3 days to 3 months (D54) and the 7-day (flights) and 14-day (packages) refund rule (D55).
- **Facts:** D75, D76, D77, D78, D54, D55.
- **The one sentence it proves:** From 23 October 2027 the airline's reply time is fixed in the Regulation at 30 calendar days to pay or justify; the Commission's 2020 study found airlines setting their own targets of 3 days to 3 months.
- **Labels to keep:** the 3 days to 3 months range is self-reported by airlines (2019, D54); the study also records an eight-week guideline used by many national bodies and a 30-day national rule in Croatia, so do not say that no deadline existed anywhere. For the refund row say it applies to refunds, not compensation (D55). The Article references are in `chart-data.json`.

### 7. Where the delay comes from: punctuality and average delay in Europe, 2019 to 2025
- **Form:** two small panels from one source (EUROCONTROL's Performance Review Report 2025). Panel one: arrival punctuality, 77.8, 87.4, 83.0, 71.5, 70.6, 72.5, 76.1 (%), 2019 to 2025. Panel two: average departure delay per flight in minutes, 2015 to 2025: 10.4, 11.4, 12.5, 14.9, 13.2, 7.5, 9.2, 17.9, 18.0, 17.7, 14.7. A stat tile for 2025: 11.1 million flights.
- **Facts:** D21, D22, D20 (and D23 for the en-route share: 1.67 minutes per flight).
- **The one sentence it proves:** Europe's flights ran worst in 2022 and 2023 (70.6% on time in 2023), recovered to 76.1% in 2025, still below the 77.8% of 2019, on a network that again carried 11.1 million flights.
- **Labels to keep:** "on time" means arriving no later than 15 minutes after schedule, which is far from the three-hour EU261 threshold; this chart shows pressure, not claims. Use the Performance Review Report only: other EUROCONTROL documents differ slightly (see section 5 of `FACTS.md`). There is no 3-hour-plus delay figure for 2025 (NOT FOUND).

### 8. What compensation cost airlines, 2011 to 2018 (the Commission's consultant, not the airline lobby)
- **Form:** bars of estimated compensation payments in EUR million: 267, 412, 547, 437, 657, 954, 1,217, 2,258, with the 2018 total of all direct costs (EUR 5,279 million, including care, rerouting and baggage) as a label.
- **Facts:** D08.
- **The one sentence it proves:** The Commission's consultant estimated that airlines' EU261 compensation payments rose 8.5 times between 2011 and 2018, from EUR 267 million to EUR 2,258 million.
- **Labels to keep:** estimate, 2018 is the last year, not comparable with the airline associations' EUR 8 to 8.1 billion (D51, D52), which cite no method. If the page quotes the industry figure, quote it as a claim in words, never on the same axis.

## Smaller graphics that are safe

- **Stat tile, passengers:** 1,093,605,497 passengers carried by EU airports in 2025 (Eurostat, pulled 12 Oct 2026, D26). Use one pull only.
- **Two-bar index, complaints against passengers (2011 to 2018):** national enforcement body complaints 328 against 135 for passengers, 2011 = 100 (D09). Label that the series includes double counting and ends in 2018.
- **Stat tile, a body that is over its own deadline:** Germany's conciliation body averaged 125 days in 2025 against a 90-day statutory target, up from 105 days in 2024 (D41); all segments, about 83% flights (D40).
- **Pull-quote, claim agencies:** the Commission's consultant described claim agencies as working "on very large scale with a high degree of automation" (D16), and the agreed (not yet adopted) enforcement text says they "often retain a significant portion of the compensation" (D81). Use only with that status.
- **Pull-quote, the common claim form:** "The Commission shall adopt an implementing act establishing a common form for compensation and reimbursement requests" appears in the companion enforcement regulation, which is agreed in principle but not adopted as of 12 October 2026 (D81, D82, D83). It is not in Regulation 2026/2202 itself.

## Do not chart

- Any EU-wide claims count, or the Commission impact assessment's "15,456 court cases a year" (D14): it is a fraction of the German courts alone.
- The airline association cost claims, the "114%" delay increase and "26 million minutes" (D51, D52), beside real data.
- D17 (at least 17% of eligible claimants unpaid): thin evidence.
- LBA 2020 notices (D42): pandemic year.
- Departure punctuality and any mix of EUROCONTROL documents (section 5 of `FACTS.md`).
- Anything that puts Destatis concluded cases and Richterbund new filings on one series.
