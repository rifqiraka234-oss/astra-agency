01

Google is where a Hamburg firm compares you first.

Search Google Maps for an IT Systemhaus in Reinbek and every other firm on the list shows stars. Yours shows none yet, and stars can't be bought or borrowed. So until the reviews come, the profile has to make the next step easy and show proof of its own.

  1. Calling and booking in one tap. The call button is there already. Every update carries a button to the booking form on the site, which Google allows on updates.
  2. A photo of you first, then the work. People buy IT care from a person. Then the documentation and the WLAN plan, real things a client gets.
  3. The four services, word for word from the site, so the profile and the site say the same thing.
  4. A review request after every job, the card below.
02

A review card left behind after every job.

One card on the desk when a job's done. The code opens the review page on the site, which explains it in three steps and shows the Google button to everyone, happy or not. Nobody gets filtered out, which is also what Google's rules ask for. Every review makes the next firm's call a little easier.

Scan it with your phone, it works.

On LinkedIn people check the person, so the posts come from you.

The company page has one follower, so whatever it posts reaches almost nobody. Posts from your own profile reach the people you're connected to, and a buyer wants to see who'll actually turn up. So the posts go out under your name and the page shares them. Each one is about the work, and each ends with one next step, a call or a first conversation.
