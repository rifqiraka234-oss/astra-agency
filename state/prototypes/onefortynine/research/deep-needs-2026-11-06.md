## Part 2. Ten levels down

Each level asks "and why does that matter to him?" of the one above.

**Level 1. The ask.** "A more professional looking website that does a bit more."

**Level 2. What it has to do.** Explain the product, answer the money questions with an easy calculator, cover the
objections, and get a contact form filled in.

**Level 3. Where it sits.** It's the landing spot for his outreach, "when we direct people to it". Nobody finds it on their
own yet. Every visitor arrives cold, from an email or a LinkedIn post, once.

**Level 4. Who really reads it.** Not only the person he emailed. That person "badgers the boss", forwards it to an area
manager or an ops director, and big groups have "lots of people involved". **The decider has never met Owen, never read
his email, and sees only the website.** The site has to work for a second reader with no context, and survive being
forwarded.

**Level 5. What that reader has to get.** A product nobody knows they need, so the site has to turn a puzzle box into
their numbers, more people in, staying longer, spending more, at a low entry cost with no staff. The puzzle is
"secondary". Money first, mechanics second.

**Level 6. Why this is his bottleneck.** He's a 20 year salesman. In the room he wins ("I'm confident with the rest").
His weakness is every room he isn't in, and with two businesses that's most of them. **The website is Owen when Owen
can't be there.** It has to pitch the way he would, warm, direct, on money, with an answer for each objection.

**Level 7. What he's worried they'll think.** He's one person, four months in, pre revenue, on Squarespace, with a
template shop still live, AI pictures, unreadable prices on his product page and a LinkedIn that still says "final
testing". Next to a 65 site park group or a 270 venue chain, **his fear is looking like a side project.** "We look good,
we look professional, we look organised ... we trust that" is the answer to that fear. He needs to look as established
as he is serious, without claiming anything untrue.

**Level 8. What his time is worth.** He can't take calls, his diary isn't his own, and he answers every lead himself. So
the site has to **filter and prepare**, sorting the "quick and easy ones" (one to ten boxes, ready to go) from the big
groups that need a process, and sending him enquiries that already say venue type, sites, boxes and timing. He built his
product to be hands off for venues. **He needs his sales to be hands off for him.**

**Level 9. What he's actually betting.** He's pre revenue, with deposits on two boxes, close to "tipping", and may soon
have to buy a lot of stock. He needs revenue that funds stock and the next games, and proof that pulls in the next
customer ("don't talk to me, talk to them"). And the idea can be copied. **His only defence is getting there first,
looking like the original, and building proof faster than anyone.** The site is the vessel for that momentum and proof.

**Level 10. What it means to him personally.** By the end of next year he wants to leave his other business and do this
full time. He turned down a licence because "we can do it ourselves". He wants partners, not suppliers. **This website
is the front door of his next chapter.** He has to feel proud sending it to a 65 site group, and it has to be his, easy for
him to grow and change without going back to an agency every time. What he really needs from us is a sales machine
that works while he's at his other job, and a partner who grows it with him.

**In one line.** Owen doesn't need a nicer website. He needs a stand-in salesman that wins the forwarded email, earns the
trust of people who've never met him, sorts serious buyers from browsers, and grows with his range, so he can quit his
other job.

## Part 3. The tensions to handle

1. **He approved the sketch, and he asked for changes.** "Does exactly what we're looking for now" against "tweaks would be
   cosmetic, colours images". Keep the concept (lock, calculator, filled form, rent or own), change the skin, the pictures
   and the order. Don't rebuild what he liked.
2. **Simple, and amazing.** "Simple ... easy to navigate" and "it either has to be that amazing". One clear path, one wow
   moment (the lock), everything else calm and fast.
3. **Two buyer speeds.** Small venues should be able to go now, big groups need a story to pass up the chain. One site,
   two exits.
4. **He sells calls but can't take them.** Every "book a call" has to become a form that works without his diary.
5. **The model keeps changing.** The park group changed it, the trainers invented one, the tourist trail is new. The site
   has to show uses as a set he can add to, not one fixed story.
6. **No proof yet, but proof is coming.** Build the places for it now, and fill them only with true things.

## Part 4. What this means for the website

Each change is tagged with the level it answers.

1. **Open on their problem in his words (L5).** Headline about more people in, staying longer, spending more, with "a
   portable escape room in a cool box" right under it, and the 1 4 9 lock kept as the one wow moment.
2. **Make every page survive the forward (L4).**
   - Each section makes sense on its own, with no "as above".
   - A **"Send this to your team" button** that gives a clean one page summary (PDF or a share link) with their calculator
     numbers on it. That's what the first reader forwards to the boss.
   - Plain business words a decider uses: capex, staff time, floor space, payback.
3. **Put Owen on the page (L6, L7).** A short section with his real photo, a few lines in his voice ("I spent 20 years in
   sales ..." only if he's happy to say it), and **a 60 second video of him explaining it**, if he'll record one. It's the
   nearest thing to being in the room, and he's good in the room.
4. **One easy calculator that speaks money (L5).** Venue type, then games a week and price per game, with an optional
   "sell drinks or food with it". The answer as a month figure, "about N times what it costs you" (his own line), and the
   payback. The same numbers carry into the form and the "send to your team" summary.
5. **Answer every objection he named (L5, L6).** Cost, what to charge, earnings, floor space ("a 70 litre cool box, under a
   metre long, no permanent spot"), staff time ("none, under 10 minutes to set up"), "will they come back" (new games every
   season), "what if it gets damaged" (ask Owen), "how is it different from digital" (thousands against £350).
6. **Two exits for two speeds (L8).**
   - **"Get your box"**, for one to ten boxes, rent or buy, short form, the calculator figures attached. This is the "why
     not, let's go" path.
   - **"Talk to us about your group"**, for multi site operators, asking for number of sites, who else decides and
     timing, with a "Prefer a call back?" tick box instead of booking his diary.
   - Every enquiry reaches him already sorted and complete.
7. **A ways to use it section, built to grow (L9, tension 5).** On site rental with drinks and food packages, quiet hours
   and event nights for pubs and cafes, lodges and holiday lets, leadership training (watching leaders in action), team days.
   One card each, his to add to.
8. **Momentum, true only (L7, L9).** "New games every season", the games roadmap, where to see it next, and an empty,
   hidden place for case studies and quotes, switched on when the park trial gives a real one. The park group, the cafe
   chain and the brewery are never named without permission.
9. **The hints pages as a growth loop (L9).** Hidden per game, phone first, one hint at a time. Every guest who scans the
   QR code sees it, so after the solution, one line for venue owners among the players ("Run a pub, park or cafe? Get your
   own box"), and a "we cracked it" moment carrying the venue's name, which is his "the reaction does the marketing" made
   real.
10. **Look established (L7).** Retire the template shop and cart, the AI images, the default favicon, the "Landing Page"
    page titles and the empty VAT line. His real logo with the 1 4 9 lock, real photos, consistent colours. This is where
    the brand sheet from the rebrand analysis lands.
11. **Built to grow, and his to edit (L10).** A home page, one page template per venue type, a games page, hints per game,
    a contact page, and room for the reorder login and new products later. Whatever we build on, he can add a game, a
    quote or a venue page himself. That was promised on the call.
12. **Ready for Google next year (L3).** Real page titles and descriptions, venue type pages that match what people will
    search, fast pages. He wants people "Googling it" next year, so the foundation goes in now.
