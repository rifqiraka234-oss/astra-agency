# OneFortyNine Games. How we make them the leader (5 Nov 2026)

Raka, 5 Nov, "Okay then we gotta make them the leader." Built on `call-analysis-2026-11-05.md` and
`rebrand-analysis-2026-11-05.md`, plus three checks today (ASA guidance on "leading" claims, holiday park awards, the
hospitality games supply channel). Judgement is marked **(judgement)**.

## 1. What "leader" means here, and the one rule

**Leader means the name a UK park, pub or cafe thinks of first for "a game we can run with no staff".** It doesn't mean
the biggest, not yet. There is no leader in this exact space today. Happy Hour Escape Box UK says "The UK's First Mobile
Escape Game", but it sells hire and partnerships with no prices shown. Escape Game Events sells staffed events. Breakout
Unboxed sells to consumers. Nobody owns "the escape game a venue buys or rents, runs with no staff and refreshes with new
games". The space is open, and the first one to define it and fill it with proof wins it.

**The rule. Lead, never claim to lead, until it's provable.** The ASA reads an unqualified "leading" or "market leader" as
a best-selling claim that needs sales or market share data against competitors, and says awards and self-reported data
aren't enough (ASA Advice Online, "Types of claims, Leading", CAP Code rule 3.7, the Penna and "UK's Leading P.I's"
rulings). Owen is pre revenue, so nothing on the site, the box or LinkedIn says "leading", "number one" or "the UK's
best". What he can say, true and provable, is what only he offers, a fixed price to own (£350) or rent (£60 a month), no
staff needed, new games added to the same box.

## 2. Name the category, then own it

Leaders get to name the thing. The competitors use "mobile escape game", "mobile escape room" and "escape room in a box",
and all three describe someone else's model.

**(judgement) Owen's category, "the escape game your venue owns".** It says what's different (ownership, so no hire fee
and no host), it works for parks, pubs, cafes and trainers, and nobody else can say it. Secondary line, "No staff. No
screens. New games every season."

Then use those words everywhere and the same way, the site's headline and page titles, every LinkedIn post, the stand
banner, the lid, the sales sheet. Search terms for later, "escape game for pubs", "holiday park activities", "escape room
for venues". Next year, when people start searching (his own goal), his pages already rank for the category he named.

## 3. The eight leader moves

Ranked by what they do for him in the next six months.

1. **Publish the deal.** Prices on the site, what's included, how long it takes to set up, what happens if something
   breaks. Competitors hide prices behind "enquire". Openness is the cheapest way to look like the standard setter, and it
   answers the objections he named on the call before anyone has to ask.
2. **Turn the park trial into the first proof.** The trial runs to the end of the year. Agree with Owen now what to
   measure, games played, what guests paid, drinks and food sold with it, staff time (zero), and two or three guest
   quotes. Get the park's permission to name it, or use it as an anonymous case study ("a national holiday park group").
   He said it himself, "if it works for them ... the rest are easy". One real case study beats any brand work.
3. **Ship a new game every season.** A library is what a leader has and a copycat doesn't. "New games every season" is a
   promise competitors with one game can't match, and it answers "will customers come back". If his partnership with the
   escape room builder is real and he can name it, that's the strongest authority signal he'll have. Only with his say so.
4. **Be the one with the numbers.** Leaders publish what everyone else guesses. From the calculator and the trial, a short
   yearly "What a games box earns" note for parks and pubs, with sourced industry figures beside his own (dwell time is up,
   people want phone free time, competitive socialising is growing). The trade press quotes whoever has the numbers.
5. **Get into the rooms where buyers meet.** The Land Leisure and Tourism Show again next year, now as the established
   name. Supplier awards, a new independent Holiday Park Awards has supplier categories, with entries from 22 Sep 2026 and
   winners on 22 Apr 2027 (to be checked at source before entering). Home Leisure Direct, which supplies pub and bar
   groups with competitive socialising products, rentals and servicing, and runs industry events attended by ops
   directors at big pub groups, is a possible reseller or partner channel.
6. **Look like the standard.** The brand system from the rebrand analysis. One name, one mark (the 1 4 9 wheels), real
   photos of people playing, a consistent warm look, and a fixed LinkedIn About. Category leaders look finished.
7. **Own the name legally.** Check and file the trade mark for whatever name he keeps, plus 149 or OneFortyNine, in games
   and entertainment. A leader can't have a name someone else can take off him. See rebrand analysis section 5.
8. **Make it spread on its own.** His line, "the reaction does the marketing". A small card in every box pointing players
   to the venue's own social handle, a shareable "we cracked it" moment at the end of each game (a final card or photo
   frame with the venue's name), and a referral for venues that bring another venue. Every game becomes an advert for the
   venue and for him.

## 4. What it changes in the website

On top of `call-analysis-2026-11-05.md` section 5.

- **The hero names the category.** For example, "The escape game your venue owns." with his problem line under it (more
  people in, staying longer, spending more). The 1 4 9 lock stays.
- **Prices in plain view**, own or rent, what's included, and "new games every season".
- **A proof section built now, filled as proof arrives.** Case study template, quote cards and a "where you can play it"
  map of venues. Hidden until there's something true to show.
- **A games page as a roadmap.** The Heist now, the next titles and seasons as "coming". It tells buyers the library is
  growing.
- **A "for your venue" guide** per sector, how parks, pubs, cafes and trainers each make money with it. It's the same
  content as the sector pages, and also the pages Google will rank.
- **The numbers note** as a downloadable page once the trial gives figures.
- **No "leading", "number one" or "UK's best"** anywhere, and nothing about his pipeline without his permission.
