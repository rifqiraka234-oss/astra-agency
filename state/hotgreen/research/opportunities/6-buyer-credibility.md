# HotGreen: how F&B manufacturers buy capital equipment from a new supplier, and how a pre-deployment startup earns credibility honestly

Prepared 25 September 2026 for the Astra Agency proposal to HotGreen Solutions. Research only. Nobody was contacted, no form was submitted, nothing was posted.

**Scope.** Topic 6 of the opportunity research: buyer behaviour, supplier risk, pilots, real F&B steam electrification pilots, honest pre-deployment credibility, and challenger positioning. The 14 competitor site profiles in `state/hotgreen/research/market-context.md` (Spirax, GEA, Sabroe, Everllence, Siemens Energy, Star, Clade, AtmosZero, Skyven, Heaten, SPH, Qpinch, Futraheat, Rondo) were not redone. Where this file looks at AtmosZero, Futraheat or Skyven again, it looks only at their pilot evidence and how they communicated it.

**Labels.**

## The ten findings that matter most

1. **Engineers do most of their evaluation before they talk to anyone, and they trust vendor engineers more than any other author.** 62% of the buying process happens online before sales contact, 66% rate engineering experts at vendor companies as very or extremely trustworthy, and spec sheet downloads are a top newsletter action [1]. HotGreen's only spec table is a picture.
2. **The deal is decided by people HotGreen never meets.** Finance, legal, compliance, procurement and operations are the "hidden buyers", and 71% of them say they have little or no interaction with sales teams [3]. In industrial heat specifically, RMI names capital projects, legal and plant management as the people who kill first-of-a-kind projects when not engaged [6].
3. **First-of-a-kind industrial heat is bought on risk allocation, not on efficiency.** Buyers look for performance guarantees, warranties, a service model and financing [6][7]. Insurers, financiers, regulators and procurement hold the veto on scaling a pilot [8].
4. **"Pilot purgatory" (the 70% figure) is a digital manufacturing statistic, not a hardware one** [9]. The better evidence for hardware is qualitative: pilots usually work technically and fail at the decision to scale, so the evidence each approver needs should be designed into the pilot before hardware goes in [8].
5. **The 100+ Accelerator tells applicants exactly what it wants from a website**: "technology, traction, and company information", and its top selection trait is "clarity ... can you clearly prove its value?" [20]. It also runs NDAs on pilots, and its site terms require written approval to use its names and logos [20].
6. **HotGreen's public record is already stronger than its website.** UKRI publishes the demonstration project, naming CCEP as host site and prior investor [21]. The Innovate UK grant terms require grantees to acknowledge Innovate UK in communications [21]. The live site mentions neither Innovate UK nor CCEP, nor any patent, certification or demonstration (checked today) [21].
7. **The best pilot write-ups in this field are dull, specific and honest about limits.** AtmosZero's New Belgium page gives COP at three ambient temperatures, install time, payback and "lessons learned", co-authored with a university [22]. The IEA HPT demo sheet format ends with a "Facts about the case" block including investment cost and annual savings, and says outright when data is supplier-declared [26].
8. **Readiness claims drift, and the drift is visible.** Futraheat's own IEA supplier sheet says "TRL level: Currently 6 (7 once brewery project is completed)" while January 2026 trade press says the Hepworth project moved it "from TRL 5 to TRL 9" [26][27]. HotGreen has the same drift problem with its savings numbers.
9. **Inconsistent performance numbers are a legal exposure in the UK, not only a credibility one.** Misleading B2B advertising is an offence under the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008, and "results to be expected from use of the product" and "results ... of tests" are listed characteristics [30].
10. **Challenger theory supports Sanya's instinct, with a condition.** eatbigfish's "Missionary" challenger is "a series of actions that invite belief, rather than a statement on a homepage", and the "Next Generation" challenger positions the incumbent as "perfect for a time gone by" [33]. The incumbent for HotGreen is the gas boiler, not other heat pump makers.

## 1. The industrial B2B buying process for capital equipment

### 1.1 How much happens before a vendor is contacted

- **[3C] Technical buyers spend 62% of the buying process online before engaging with sales** (2026 State of Marketing to Engineers, TREW Marketing and GlobalSpec with Elektor). Quote: "Technical buyers spend 62% of the buying process online before engaging with sales." Younger buyers spend longer online (66% for 35 and under). **VERIFIED** [1] https://www.trewmarketing.com/state-of-marketing-to-engineers-research-report
  - Check 1, reopened: the PR Newswire release of 25 Mar 2026 carries the same headline, "Engineers Spend 62% of the Buying Journey Researching Online" [1] https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/engineers-spend-62-of-the-buying-journey-researching-online-new-study-by-trew-marketing-globalspec-shows-how-ai-brand-visibility-expertise-drive-vendor-selection-302724650.html
  - Check 2, falsification: the same report says "Only ten percent of respondents say they'd prefer not to interact with a salesperson at all", so buyers still want a human, later. The panel is recruited through GlobalSpec and Elektor, which skews to electronics, design and automation engineers rather than process plant engineers. Sample size is not given on the public page. The 2025 edition gave 60% [1] https://www.trewmarketing.com/blog/2025-state-of-marketing-to-engineers-research, so the trend is stable, not a one-off.
  - Check 3, independent: Gartner's survey of 646 B2B buyers (Aug to Sep 2025) found 67% prefer a rep-free experience and 45% used AI tools in a recent purchase. **SECONDARY**, Gartner's own page is Cloudflare walled, read via Digital Commerce 360, 17 Mar 2026 [2] https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/17/gartner-b2b-buyers-rep-free-purchasing-ai-reshapes-sales/. Gartner's older finding that buyers spend only 17% of buying time with potential suppliers is **SECONDARY** via Buyer Enablement's source library [2] https://buyerenablement.org/research/.
- **What makes technical buyers contact a vendor.** "Pricing and Inventory Questions Are the Top Trigger for First Vendor Contact ... They're also interested in validating information found online and better understanding complex solutions." 59% prefer email for first contact, 22% a phone call, and LinkedIn messages were among the least popular. **VERIFIED** [1]
- **Gartner's "value clarity".** Buyers who reach a clear understanding of how a product improves outcomes in their role are "twice as likely to report a high-quality purchase". **SECONDARY** [2]

### 1.2 What content engineers want

- **Spec sheets and engineers.** Among newsletter subscribers, "Top engagement actions include reading articles (sixty-three percent), downloading spec sheets (sixty percent), and visiting vendor websites (fifty-six percent)." **VERIFIED** [1]
- **[3C] Who engineers trust.** "Engineering experts at vendor companies are the most trusted content authors, with sixty-six percent of technical buyers considering them very or extremely trustworthy. Generative AI tools rank lowest on trust, with ten percent." **VERIFIED** [1]
  - Check 1, reopened and quoted above.
  - Check 2, falsification: Edelman's 2025 hidden-buyer data says 64% "trust thought leadership more than product sheets or brochures when assessing capabilities" [3], which cuts the other way on product sheets but not on expertise. Both point at expert-authored substance over marketing copy.
  - Check 3, independent: Edelman 2025, 74% of hidden buyers prioritise "Domain expertise" at the moment of purchase **VERIFIED** [3] https://www.edelman.com/insights/hidden-buyer-b2b
- **Advice aimed squarely at a company with no case studies yet.** A practitioner quoted in the TREW report: "Case study videos, application walkthroughs, teardown explanations, and real-world demonstrations build trust far more effectively than promotional claims. If a company is newer and doesn't yet have a deep portfolio of examples, genuinely educational content is the next best path." **VERIFIED** that the report quotes it; it is one practitioner's opinion [1]
- **Gated content is acceptable when it is worth it.** "85% of technical buyers are willing to provide their information for technical content they find valuable." 94% subscribe to work-related newsletters. **VERIFIED** [1]
- **What industrial sourcing buyers rank first** (Thomas, 27 May 2021, "more than 400 real, verified, and registered buyers"): availability and lead times first, cost second, then quality certifications ("Ensure the certifications you do have are easy to find and always current") and a library of case studies. **VERIFIED**, but dated 2021 and drawn from buyers sourcing parts and contract manufacturing rather than process plant, so low weight here [5] https://blog.thomasnet.com/what-industrial-buyers-care-most-about-when-shortlisting-new-suppliers

### 1.3 Brand familiarity, and where the data disagrees

- **[3C] Familiarity tips the scales for engineers.** "When evaluating two technically similar solutions, seventy percent of buyers are either likely or very likely to choose the better-known brand", and 53% say brand familiarity influenced their most recent purchase. **VERIFIED** [1]
  - Check 2, falsification, and it partly holds: Edelman 2025 says "All things being equal, only 41% of hidden buyers say 'the safest choice' is what matters most at the moment of purchase", and "53% say strong content can outweigh brand recognition alone". It also says "Hidden buyers can be powerful advocates for new or lesser-known brands." **VERIFIED** [3]
  - Check 3, independent support for familiarity: Binet and Field's B2B cut of the IPA Databank found "the more famous they make the company, the better the business results" **VERIFIED** [32], and Ehrenberg-Bass argues advertising works "by building and refreshing memory links to a brand" [4].
  - Reconciliation (**INFERENCE**): different populations. Engineers comparing near-identical specs lean to the known name. Finance, procurement and operations reviewers respond to evidence that the supplier understands their business. A startup cannot buy fame quickly but can publish the evidence, so the practical lever is substance aimed at the hidden buyers plus steady, dated visibility aimed at engineers.

### 1.4 Who sits in the buying group for industrial heat

- **Hidden buyers.** "These are the unseen stakeholders in finance, legal, compliance, procurement, or operations who may not use your product or sign the contract but still hold real power over whether you win the business." 71% "say they have little or no interaction with sales teams". Edelman and LinkedIn's 2025 report surveyed "nearly 2,000 global professionals". **VERIFIED** [3] (Edelman's "more than 40% of B2B deals stall due to internal misalignment" is footnoted to another source that the page does not name, so that figure is **UNVERIFIED**.) Note: a search engine summary of this report gave "91%" for one statistic that the primary gives as "81%"; the primary is used here.
- **[3C] The buying group in first-of-a-kind industrial heat projects** (RMI, 3 Feb 2025, from "interviews and closed-door discussions with industry leaders including tech providers; engineering, procurement, and construction firms (EPCs); industrials; and contractors"). **VERIFIED** [6] https://rmi.org/resources/blueprint-for-success-overcoming-challenges-in-industrial-heat-decarbonization-projects/
  - The champion "should be someone with decision-making control ... such as a sustainability manager tasked with meeting Scope 1 targets, a plant manager tasked with reducing energy costs".
  - "Partners should quickly find a balanced team of decision makers who will support the project, incorporating employees from the capital projects, legal, and plant management teams. Plant manager buy-in is critical".
  - "Interviewees identified maintenance needs as a big concern for customers, requiring demystification. Tech providers should brief customers with a clear picture of the tasks, skillsets, costs, and frequencies of maintenance."
  - "site-specific integration costs for industrial heat decarbonization projects can account for up to 25% of total project costs".
  - Check 2, falsification: RMI's evidence is US projects, and some are not heat pumps. No contrary evidence found for the UK. Check 3, independent: the Industrial Heat Pump Alliance buyer guide [7] and Edelman [3] name the same functions.
- **Gartner's buying group of six to 10 decision makers** for complex B2B purchases. **SECONDARY** (Gartner primary walled) [2]
- **Finance applies a carbon price inside capex decisions at HotGreen's anchor customer.** CCEP's FY2025 20-F says it applies "an internal shadow carbon price of €100/tCO2e" to Scope 1 and 2 capex. **SECONDARY**, verified by the earlier market-context session on SEC EDGAR; SEC blocked my reopen without a declared user agent.

### 1.5 How much of the market is buying at any moment

- **[3C] The 95:5 idea** (Professor John Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass, for the LinkedIn B2B Institute): companies change providers of services such as banking or telecoms "around every five years. This means that only 20% are in the market for those services in a given year and just 5% in a given quarter." **VERIFIED** on the Institute's own page, which republishes a Marketing Week article from about 2021 [4] https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/ehrenberg-bass-95-of-b2b-buyers-are-not-in-the-market-for-your-products
  - Check 2, falsification: the 5-year cycle is for services, not capital plant. Boilers last far longer, so if anything fewer sites are in market in any quarter (**INFERENCE**). The IHP Alliance guide lists "Your existing equipment is nearing the end of its life" as one of the signs a site is ready for a heat pump [7], which supports the idea that buying is triggered by replacement cycles.
  - Check 3, independent: a search summary of the 2024 Edelman and LinkedIn report repeats the 95% figure (**UNVERIFIED**, the PDF returned 403), and Sanya's own remark that HotGreen has mapped future deployments "based on kind of manufacturing cycles" (dossier) fits.

### 1.6 What the buyer-side guide tells F&B plants to look for in a heat pump supplier

- **[3C]** The Industrial Heat Pump Alliance (ACEEE, NEMA and the Renewable Thermal Collaborative), "How to Get an Industrial Heat Pump for Your Facility", August 2025, reviewed by Hilmar Cheese and Oatly staff. Step 4, "Finding suppliers", tells buyers to consider: "Technology fit for your heat source/sink and temperature range; Integration support and service model (e.g., full package vs. equipment only); Vendor experience in your industry or process type; Willingness to engage early for feasibility support; Availability of financing or performance guarantees." **VERIFIED** [7] https://industrialheatpumpalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/IHP_Resource_Guide.pdf

  - Check 2: US focused, and written for plants, not for suppliers. Nothing found that contradicts it. Check 3: RMI [6] and WEF [8] independently name guarantees, warranties and financing.

## 2. Supplier risk: how a large manufacturer judges a startup supplier

### 2.1 Risk allocation is the product

- **[3C]** RMI: "Scaling industrial heat decarbonization FOAK projects will require risk mitigation measures from finance partners or developers, such as performance guarantees or technology warranties." And: "When the technology risk is high, tech providers have provided their own warranties or assumed all project risk until first operation." RMI also notes the typical FOAK profile: "marginal grant-funded projects with tight budgets, tech providers without specific industry experience". **VERIFIED** [6]
  - Check 2: RMI says contracting practice is still evolving and that "there is no risk-free option with FOAK". No source found saying buyers accept FOAK without guarantees.
  - Check 3: IHP Alliance "Availability of financing or performance guarantees" [7]; WEF 2026 "Insurers price the risk of novel equipment. Asset financiers decide whether it affects collateral value ... Procurement teams decide whether the organization can actually buy it." [8]

### 2.2 References, and the chicken-and-egg problem

- WEF, January 2018: "Trust without references: This is a chicken-and-egg problem: key corporate departments may not want to work with start-ups and their unproven products if they cannot provide references from previous corporate clients." And "There can be a huge chasm between working with the corporation's innovation department on proof of concept at the start of a relationship, and working with core business units after the trial run ends." It recommends moving "from an innovation pitch into reference selling". **VERIFIED**, dated 2018 [10] https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_White_Paper_Collaboration_between_Start-ups_and_Corporates.pdf

### 2.3 Financial standing, and how procurement mitigates a small balance sheet

- The UK Cabinet Office guidance "Assessing and monitoring the economic and financial standing of bidders and suppliers, 2026" sets out ratio-based assessment and the standard mitigations when a bidder cannot show low or medium risk: a parent company guarantee, bank or insurer guarantees, and performance bonds ("usually provided at contract award for an agreed percentage of the total contract value"). It also allows "Assessment of historic turnover trends or forward-looking order books." **VERIFIED** [11] https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/698c4873701ea1f716d712d9/2025-12-31-_EFS_-2025_Final_v4.pdf
  - This is public sector guidance. That private F&B procurement teams use similar tools is **INFERENCE**, supported in spirit by [6][7][8]. CCEP's own supplier onboarding rules could not be read (see "Could not verify").
- **Supplier codes at the Coca-Cola system.** The Coca-Cola Company's "Supplier Requirements" page says suppliers "will comply with" its Supplier Guiding Principles, Supplier Code of Business Conduct and KORE "General Supplier Requirements". **VERIFIED** [12] https://www.coca-colacompany.com/policies-and-practices/supplier-guiding-principles. CCEP is a separate company and its own requirements were not readable; that they are similar is **INFERENCE**.

## 3. Corporate pilots and "pilot purgatory"

### 3.1 What the famous statistic actually measures

- **[3C] The 70% pilot purgatory figure is about digital manufacturing.** WEF and McKinsey, December 2019: "more than 70% of the companies in the manufacturing ecosystem are falling further behind, with their Fourth Industrial Revolution efforts stuck in what we defined as 'pilot purgatory': the attempt to implement advanced manufacturing technologies without realizing appealing returns on investment or measurable improvements in operational key performance indicators". **VERIFIED** [9] https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Global_Lighthouse_Network.pdf
  - Check 2: it is not a conversion rate for startup hardware pilots, and I found no published conversion rate for industrial hardware pilots with corporates. Check 3: WEF's 21 Sep 2026 article restates the same figure as "advanced analytics, artificial intelligence (AI) or digital solutions" [8].
- **What goes wrong with hardware pilots.** WEF, 21 Sep 2026 (Mehrad Yaghmai, Cool Climate Collective, and Judith Ketelslegers, WEF): "In hard tech, what reaches an approved commercial pilot usually works. What fails is the decision to scale it. Hard tech pilots are designed to answer an engineering question: does this perform as specified? But scale-up depends on financial and institutional questions: who will insure, finance and procure it?" And: "A pilot that excludes these actors produces evidence they cannot use." The fix: "Before installing hardware, define the evidence each party needs to say yes, then instrument the pilot to produce it on a timeline all can survive." **VERIFIED** as the authors' view; the claim that stage-gated pilots "cost little more" is their opinion, **UNVERIFIED** [8] https://www.weforum.org/stories/financial-and-monetary-systems/3-shifts-in-financial-design-to-take-hard-tech-pilots-to-scale/

## 5. Honest credibility before deployment

### 5.1 Say the readiness level in the standard's own words

- **EU TRL definitions** (Horizon 2020 General Annex G), quoting each level's wording: TRL 4 "technology validated in lab", TRL 6 "technology demonstrated in relevant environment", TRL 7 "system prototype demonstration in operational environment", TRL 9 "actual system proven in operational environment". **VERIFIED** [29] https://ec.europa.eu/research/participants/data/ref/h2020/wp/2014_2015/annexes/h2020-wp1415-annex-g-trl_en.pdf
- **HotGreen's position (INFERENCE from verified facts).** NZTC listed HotGreen at TRL 4 in 2025 (SECONDARY, dossier). The UKRI project's stated aim is to "validate the performance ... in an operational environment" [21], which is TRL 7 language. So the honest line today is "lab-validated compressor; a 50 kWth demonstrator goes into an operating CCEP site in 2027 to validate it in an operational environment".
- **Readiness beyond the technology.** The US DOE's Adoption Readiness Level framework "assesses 17 different adoption risks of a technology and translates the risk assessment into a 1 to 9 adoption readiness score" across Value Proposition, Market Acceptance, Resource Maturity and License to Operate, and was built to complement TRL because "technical feasibility only tells half of the story". **VERIFIED** [29] https://www.energy.gov/arl/adoption-readiness-levels

### 5.2 Independent validation routes that exist

- **University or national lab co-authorship of results.** AtmosZero (Colorado State University) [22], Futraheat (figures published through the Carbon Trust and DESNZ, "which part-funded the initiative", per trade press [27]; the IEA sheet says "part-funded by a UK government grant" [26]). **VERIFIED** as described by them.
- **IEA HPT Project 68 technology listing.** The Task 1 overview "remains open for new submissions" of both "commercially available systems, as well as systems under development", with a TRL column; it lists at least one company at TRL 4 (Airthium). HotGreen is not listed (checked today). **VERIFIED** [26]. Caveat printed by the IEA: supplier declared, "not third-party validated". So it is a respected listing, not a validation.
- **Formal technology qualification.** DNV's recommended practice DNV-RP-A203 defines qualification as "the process of providing the evidence that the technology will function within specific limits with an acceptable level of confidence". **SECONDARY** (DNV's page is walled; quoted from a DNV GL presentation to AIChE, 3 Aug 2018, oil and gas context) [31]. I found no heat pump supplier using it (**UNVERIFIED** whether any does).
- **Corporate and investor backing, stated as fact.** Caldera, a UK electric steam storage startup, headlines "GEA invests in Caldera" and dates it (March 2025) **COMPANY CLAIM** [37]. Edelman: hidden buyers "can be powerful advocates for new or lesser-known brands" [3].

### 5.3 [3C] The legal floor for B2B claims in the UK

- The Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008, regulation 3: advertising is misleading if it "in any way, including its presentation, deceives or is likely to deceive the traders to whom it is addressed ... and by reason of its deceptive nature, is likely to affect their economic behaviour". The listed characteristics include "(l) results to be expected from use of the product", "(m) results and material features of tests or checks carried out on the product", and the advertiser's "(d) ownership of industrial, commercial or intellectual property rights; or (e) awards and distinctions". Regulation 6: "A trader is guilty of an offence if he engages in advertising which is misleading under regulation 3." **VERIFIED** [30] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/regulation/3
  - Check 2: are B2B ads outside the ASA's reach? No. The CAP Code's Appendix 1: "Business-to-business marketing communications are subject to the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 ... Business-to-business marketing communications that breach the CAP Code may be referred to Trading Standards". **VERIFIED** [30] https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/appendix-1.html
  - Check 3: Business Companion (the Trading Standards backed guidance site, reviewed April 2025) says the same in plain terms. **VERIFIED** [30] https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/fair-trading/business-to-business-marketing
- **CAP Code on environmental claims:** "11.1 The basis of environmental claims must be clear. Unqualified claims could mislead if they omit material information." "11.3 Absolute claims must be supported by a high level of substantiation." **VERIFIED** [30]. (Rule 11.2 refers to consumers, so how strictly the ASA applies each rule to B2B is **UNVERIFIED**.)
- **INFERENCE for HotGreen:** 30% versus 40% savings, €250k versus $250,000 per MW, "drop in replacement" versus "hybrid", and "4x more efficient" against a published CoP of 2.8 are exactly "results to be expected from use of the product" stated inconsistently. "Patent-pending" is an IP ownership claim. None of this is a crisis, but it moves the numbers problem from a style issue to a compliance issue, which is the argument for one controlled claims register.

## 6. B2B brand positioning for industrial challengers

### 6.1 Frameworks

- **[3C] Challenger narratives** (eatbigfish, founded on Adam Morgan's "Eating the Big Fish"). "A Challenger Brand is not a brand that challenges somebody, but a brand that challenges something." Of the ten narratives: "The Missionary exists primarily to change something in the world it sees as broken or unfair ... what makes a missionary different is not the stated desire to be a brand or team 'on a mission', but the single-minded focus with which it lives that genuine mission to enact change, and the evidence we see to believe it. It's a Challenger that is, above all, a series of actions that invite belief, rather than a statement on a homepage." And "The Next Generation Challenger is challenging the appropriateness of the Market Leader for the new times we live in ... positioning the incumbent as certainly perfect for a time gone by, while now being clear that the world has moved on". **VERIFIED** (a consultancy's framework, not research) [33] https://www.eatbigfish.com/thinking/challenger-brand-types
  - Check 2: these are consumer-born frameworks; eatbigfish's examples are consumer brands. Check 3: market-context's independent read of 14 industrial sites found the same thing in B2B, "Mission lines do appear, but only on top of the proof", and Edelman found 60% of hidden buyers "associate unique format and design with higher quality" [3].
- **Category design** (Christopher Lochhead, co-author of "Play Bigger", interview in The Marketing Journal, 14 Sep 2016): "companies and products don't live in space. They live within a container in a customer's mind. That container is a category." He claims "the category leader ... commands 76% of the market cap of the category". **SECONDARY** interview; the 76% is a **COMPANY CLAIM** from Play Bigger's own analysis of venture-backed technology companies [34] https://www.marketingjournal.org/play-bigger-an-interview-with-christopher-lochhead/
  - **INFERENCE:** the category here already exists and is named by others (IEA "industrial high-temperature heat pumps", ACEEE "industrial heat pumps"), and AtmosZero already owns "Boiler 2.0". Creating a new category would cost more than a six-person company can spend. Joining the existing category and challenging the gas boiler is the cheaper and more credible route.
- **The empirical B2B evidence** (Les Binet and Peter Field for the LinkedIn B2B Institute, using the IPA Databank, 2019): five principles, "Invest in share of voice; Balance brand and activation; Expand your customer base; Maximise mental availability; Harness the power of emotion"; "Campaigns that aim to increase a firm's share of mind are much the most effective". The authors' own caveat: "our findings are tentative ... sample sizes are small, at less than 50 cases ... the IPA Databank is biased towards effective campaigns". **VERIFIED** [32] (PDF via LinkedIn's asset host; IPA summary at https://ipa.co.uk/effworks/marketing-marketing-v2/five-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing)

### 6.2 Industrial and climate hardware brands that look established while staying distinctive (visited today, screenshots in `scratchpad/shots/`)

- **Commonwealth Fusion Systems.** Full-bleed macro photograph of its own machined magnet hardware; navigation laid out as a boxed grid in a monospace typeface that reads like an engineering drawing; one scale claim as the headline ("The world's largest and leading commercial fusion energy company"); three two-word principles ("Move fast", "Move smart", "Move together"). Distinctive through type and grid; established through real hardware and a dated programme. [35]
- **Form Energy.** Aerial photograph of its own factory on the former Weirton Steel site; condensed uppercase headline; outline logo in gold; the factory is a top-level menu item; dated press on the homepage. It borrows heritage from the site it occupies ("Built on the historic site of the former Weirton Steel mill"). [36]
- **Caldera.** White page, dark navy headline, one orange accent used for the logo and the single "Contact Us" button; a founder-led video in the hero; dated trade show logos directly under it; a strategic OEM investor block. It looks established through its calendar, its investor and its plain commercial language rather than through scale claims. [37]
- **INFERENCE, the common pattern:** each shows something physical and real (hardware, a factory, a trade show floor), puts dates on things, and keeps one distinctive device (a typeface, a heritage story, an accent colour). None of them hides the mission; all of them put it on top of proof.
