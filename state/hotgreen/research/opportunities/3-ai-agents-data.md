# HotGreen: which AI agents are buildable, and which public data would feed them

Prepared 25 September 2026 for the Astra Agency proposal to HotGreen Solutions. Research only. Nobody was contacted, no form was submitted, no account was created, nothing was posted. Every API call below was an anonymous read.

**Access date.** Every URL in this file was opened on 25 Sep 2026 unless a row says otherwise. Source numbers in square brackets, like [S14], point to the source table in section 4, which carries the URL, publisher, date and access date for each one.

**Labels.**
- **VERIFIED** read on a primary source in this session. For an API, a live request returned data (or a documented error) today.
- **SECONDARY** read today, but from a third party (preprint, press, mailing list, broker, third-party host).
- **COMPANY CLAIM** a company saying something about itself.
- **INFERENCE** my reasoning from verified facts. Not a fact.
- **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed today.

The regulation picture and the calculator inputs are already in `research/market-context.md` and are not repeated here. This file is about the plumbing: what can be fetched, on what terms, and what an agent built on it would and would not do.

---

## 0. The short version

1. **The regulation and market monitor is the most buildable agent and the most useful one.** Every core input is free and answered a live request today, and most carry an open licence such as OGL or CC BY (GOV.UK Content, Search and Atom, legislation.gov.uk, the EU Cellar SPARQL endpoint, the DG CLIMA news RSS, the Parliament Bills API, Eurostat, Elexon, ONS). It would have caught, this week, the Commission's €1 billion IF26 industrial heat auction published on 24 Sep 2026, open to heat pumps and to food and beverage above 80°C, and the DESNZ quarterly energy prices due 29 Sep 2026 [S14, S15, S5]. **VERIFIED** for the sources, **INFERENCE** for the value.
2. **A usable list of big-steam food and drink plants can be built legally from public data, but only for the biggest sites.** The UK ETS compliance report carries a NACE code per installation: 78 food and drink installations (NACE 10 and 11), 68 open, 2,255,418 tCO2e in 2025, median 19,120 t [S34]. The EEA industrial emissions data flags 2,219 EU food and beverage sites (E-PRTR activity 8(b)) reporting for 2024, 105 of them with a large combustion plant [S40]. The EU ETS registry itself carries no NACE code [S37]. **VERIFIED.** Mid-size UK sites (below the 20 MW ETS threshold) appear only as operator names in the Climate Change Agreement data, 641 "Food and Drink" target units plus dairy, brewing, spirits, meat, bakers and malting, with no site address [S43]. **VERIFIED.**
3. **Two popular sources carry licence traps.** SBTi's dashboard download permits research, personal, non-commercial and editorial use with credit, and asks anyone else to seek permission [S45]. National Gas says its portal data "must not be reproduced without the prior written consent of National Gas Transmission" [S28], although ONS republishes the same daily gas price as official statistics [S29]. **VERIFIED.** CDP corporate data is a licensed product [S46].
4. **Publishing plumbing exists but must stay human-gated.** Framer's Server API can write CMS items (including drafts) and publish, and is free during a beta whose future price is "TBD" [S59, S61]. LinkedIn's Posts API cannot create a draft ("PUBLISHED is the only accepted field during creation"), and company-page posting needs a vetted Community Management API app [S63]. LinkedIn's User Agreement bans "bots or other unauthorized automated methods" to post or drive engagement [S64]. **VERIFIED.**
5. **LLMs still get regulation wrong often enough that a human must sign off every public sentence.** Commercial legal research tools hallucinated 17% to 33% of the time in a 2024 preregistered study [S65]. A March 2026 benchmark of ten frontier models found regulatory Q&A correct below 9% of the time [S68] (preprint, LLM-judged). Under the EU AI Act, AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be labelled from 2 Aug 2026 unless it "has undergone a process of human review or editorial control" with a person holding editorial responsibility [S70, S72]. **VERIFIED** for the texts, **SECONDARY** for the preprints.

## 2. Findings by topic

### 2.1 Regulation and policy feeds

1. **GOV.UK is fully machine-readable without a key.** The Content API "does not require authentication", allows "a maximum limit of 10 requests per second per client", is in beta, and content is under OGL v3.0 [S1]. **VERIFIED.** The public `search.json` endpoint is "powered by search-api", and "There is no equivalent publicly available endpoint for querying search-api-v2", the newer engine behind site search (page last updated 3 Jun 2026) [S2]. **VERIFIED.**
2. **A DESNZ query returned today's items.** Filtering the Search API by DESNZ and newest first returned 4,713 documents, the latest "Evaluation of the Boiler Upgrade Scheme" at 08:09 UTC today and "Energy prices: April to June 2026" (statistics announcement) yesterday [S3]. **VERIFIED.** The DESNZ Atom feed carries the latest 20 entries, updated 24 Sep 2026 15:51 [S4]. **VERIFIED.**
3. **The UK ETS Authority has no GOV.UK organisation page of its own.** Three likely slugs returned 404, and the UK ETS carbon price determination is published by DESNZ as primary publishing organisation [S3]. **VERIFIED** (three slugs tested, not every possible slug). So a monitor has to follow DESNZ plus keyword searches, not an "ETS Authority" feed. **INFERENCE.**
4. **A trap in the Search API.** An unquoted multi-word query combined with `order=-public_timestamp` returned unrelated items (travel advice, school guidance) for "climate change agreements"; the quoted phrase without ordering returned the right 274 results [S3]. **VERIFIED** (observed today). A monitor must use filters and quoted phrases and check relevance, or it will summarise noise. **INFERENCE.**
5. **The release calendar is queryable.** DESNZ statistics announcements via the Search API list "Energy prices: April to June 2026" confirmed for 29 Sep 2026 09:30 and the "Non-domestic National Energy Efficiency Data Framework (ND-NEED), 2026" confirmed for 30 Sep 2026 09:30 [S5, S6]. **VERIFIED.** The same list is available as Atom [S5]. **VERIFIED.**
6. **legislation.gov.uk has Atom feeds and a clear fair-use rule.** Formats are XML (`data.xml`), RDF and Atom (`data.feed`) [S7]. The feed of new legislation returned 20 entries, latest 25 Sep 2026 13:03 BST; a title filter for "emissions trading" in 2026 SIs returned 4 Orders and Regulations, including "The Greenhouse Gas Emissions Trading Scheme (Amendment) (Extension to Maritime Activities) Order 2026" [S9]. **VERIFIED.** The fair use policy requires an identifying User-Agent and says "You must not exceed the request rate limit of 1,500 requests in any 5-minute period" [S8]. **VERIFIED.** Limitation: "Secondary legislation is not revised (i.e amendments by subsequent legislation are not incorporated into the text)" [S7]. **VERIFIED.** An agent must not present an SI's text as the current consolidated law. **INFERENCE.**
7. **EUR-Lex offers six predefined RSS feeds** (Parliament and Council legislation, two case-law feeds, Commission proposals, Official Journal L, Official Journal C); custom RSS needs a registered account [S10]. **VERIFIED** (help page rendered). The SOAP web service "is freely available after registration" [S11]. **VERIFIED.** Reuse: "you can re-use the legal documents published in EUR-Lex for commercial or non-commercial purposes", editorial content CC BY 4.0 [S12]. **VERIFIED.** **The feeds themselves could not be fetched today**: curl timed out after 60 seconds three times and headless Chromium got a proxy 502. The EUR-Lex homepage answered 202 (a bot challenge). This is a fact about our fetch path, not about the feed. **UNVERIFIED** that the feeds work.
8. **The Cellar SPARQL endpoint works without a key and is the dependable EU route.** A query for English-language acts since 1 Jul 2026 with "emission" in the title returned results in 42.7 seconds, including "Commission Notice, Questions and Answers on Directive (EU) 2024/1785" dated 14 Sep 2026 (the revised Industrial Emissions Directive) and Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1815 of 24 Jul 2026 [S13]. **VERIFIED.** Slow queries need timeouts and retries. **INFERENCE.**
9. **The DG CLIMA news RSS is live and carried a directly relevant item this week.** 30 items, latest 25 Sep 2026 11:05 CEST [S14]. On 24 Sep 2026 the Commission "published the final Terms and Conditions (T&Cs) for its second Europe-wide auction for projects decarbonising industrial process heat (IF26 Heat Auction)", budget €1 billion from EU ETS revenues, "expected to open to bidders in early December 2026", covering "electrified technologies such as heat pumps", naming "food & beverage" among sectors, and expanding eligibility "to include projects producing industrial heat above 80ºC" [S15]. **VERIFIED** (RSS item and article, two routes). It is "open to projects of all sizes from every industrial sector across the European Economic Area" [S15]. **VERIFIED.** UK plants are therefore outside it; HotGreen's EU prospects are inside it. **INFERENCE.** This item is not in `market-context.md`, which is exactly the gap a weekly monitor closes. **INFERENCE.**
10. **UK Parliament Bills API works without a key.** A search for "energy" returned 78 bills, including the "Taxation (Energy and Vehicles) Act 2026" (updated 19 Aug 2026); "emissions" returned 23 [S16]. **VERIFIED.** The Swagger file lists 22 paths and a contact address [S16]. **VERIFIED.** The Open Parliament Licence page returned a Cloudflare 403 to us. **UNVERIFIED.**
11. **The CBAM certificate price page is an HTML table with no feed.** Q1 2026 €75.36 (7 Apr), Q2 2026 €75.28 (6 Jul), Q3 due 5 Oct 2026, Q4 due 4 Jan 2027; "From 2027 onwards, the Commission will calculate and publish weekly prices" [S17]. **VERIFIED.** A monitor needs a small HTML parser here and must expect the format to change when weekly prices start. **INFERENCE.**

### 2.2 Energy price data

1. **DESNZ quarterly non-domestic prices are an XLSX attached to a GOV.UK page, findable through the Content API.** The attachment "Prices of fuels purchased by non-domestic consumers ... (QEP 3.4.1 and 3.4.2)" downloaded (291,737 bytes) with sheets including "3.4.2 (incl CCL)"; last change note 30 Jun 2026 [S18]. **VERIFIED.** Next edition 29 Sep 2026 [S6]. **VERIFIED.**
2. **Eurostat's API returned the same industrial prices as `market-context.md`.** `nrg_pc_205`, band MWH20000-69999, excluding VAT: EU27 €0.1342/kWh, DE €0.1595, NL €0.1166 for 2025-S2; `nrg_pc_203` DE band GJ100000-999999 €0.0546; dataset "updated 2026-09-24" [S19]. **VERIFIED** (live call, matches the earlier file). Licence CC BY 4.0, commercial reuse allowed with attribution, with exceptions for some non-EU and third-party data [S20]. **VERIFIED.**
3. **ENTSO-E needs a token and has no Great Britain data.** An anonymous call returned 401 "Authentication failed" [S21]. **VERIFIED.** The official Postman documentation shows `securityToken` as an API key [S21]. **VERIFIED.** A 15 Jun 2021 notice from the Transparency Platform team, posted to the openmod list, says GB data publication stopped because of the Brexit Trade and Cooperation Agreement and moved to Elexon BMRS [S22]. **SECONDARY.** So ENTSO-E is useful for HotGreen's EU prospects only. **INFERENCE.**
4. **Elexon's Insights API gives free GB power prices with no key.** Market Index Data for 24 to 25 Sep 2026 returned 98 half-hour rows; the APX provider averaged £165.99/MWh (N2EX rows carried zero volume); system prices for 24 Sep returned 48 periods, daily mean system sell price £161.02/MWh [S23]. **VERIFIED.** The API documentation's terms say those "with an interest in the electricity industry" may "view, download, copy, distribute, modify, transmit, publish, sell or create derivative works", must "acknowledge the source" and follow the BMRS Data Licence Terms [S24]. **VERIFIED.** The older licence page still says "an API Key will be created which will be required to access the API" [S25], which contradicts today's keyless access. **VERIFIED** (both read). The BMRS Data Licence Terms document itself was not found (the obvious URL returned "Not Found"). **UNVERIFIED.** The Market Index Data originates from APX (EPEX); whether that carries extra restrictions is **UNVERIFIED**.
5. **National Gas publishes the daily GB gas price, but restricts reuse.** The gas day summary showed "SAP 24/09/2026" at 6.398 p/kWh (187.50 p/therm), 7-day average 6.512 p/kWh [S27]. **VERIFIED.** The REST API page says "Our SOAP APIs were decommissioned in June 2026" and points to a developer portal [S26]. **VERIFIED.** The data disclaimer says contents "must not be reproduced without the prior written consent of National Gas Transmission" [S28]. **VERIFIED.** Cross-check: a broker report cited in `market-context.md` gives NBP day-ahead at 190.00 p/therm for 25 Sep 2026, the same order of magnitude [market-context.md]. **SECONDARY.**
6. **ONS republishes both daily series as official statistics, which is the closest thing to a free, permissively licensed daily UK gas and power price.** "Daily data showing System Average Price (SAP) of gas ... Source: National Gas Transmission", released 24 Sep 2026, daily values through 20 Sep 2026 (6.7474 p/kWh); monthly SAP rose from 2.716 p/kWh in Sep 2025 to 5.159 in Aug 2026 [S29]. **VERIFIED** (file parsed). The electricity series, "Source: Elexon", released 17 Sep 2026, last value 14 Sep 2026 at 19.32 p/kWh [S30]. **VERIFIED.** ONS says "Most content on this website is ... published under the Open Government Licence" and "Some content is exempt" [S31]. **VERIFIED.** Whether these two third-party-sourced series are exempt is not stated on the dataset pages. **UNVERIFIED.** Safe practice: use them internally and in charts with attribution, and ask ONS before republishing the raw series on a public calculator. **INFERENCE.**
7. **Wholesale ratio today, for context only.** Elexon APX average £165.99/MWh (16.60 p/kWh) divided by SAP 6.398 p/kWh gives about 2.6 [S23, S27]. **INFERENCE (derived).** This is a wholesale ratio, not what a factory pays: the DESNZ delivered ratio for Q1 2026 was about 5.4 to 5.5 [market-context.md]. It shows why a monitor should track the wholesale move rather than wait for quarterly statistics, and why the calculator must use each site's contract prices. **INFERENCE.**
8. **Exchange data is paid.** ICE's policy lists annual fees including "Public Display $3,000" and redistribution licences of $25,000 to $30,000, and defines public display as any website "not security restricted" [S32]. **VERIFIED.** EEX's Internal Usage Licence forbids "onward dissemination to external third parties", and a public price ticker needs a Media Usage licence [S33]. **VERIFIED.** So no exchange price should appear on HotGreen's public site without a licence. **INFERENCE.**

### 2.3 Prospect intelligence for food and beverage sites

1. **The UK ETS compliance report identifies food and drink installations by NACE code.** The report (published 22 Jun 2026) has columns including "Installation name", recorded emissions 2021 to 2026, "NACE" and "NACE Description"; its field notes say NACE "Indicates the industrial or economic sector, to which the installation belongs" and is "equivalent to the UK's Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) codes" [S34]. **VERIFIED.** Counting NACE 10xx and 11xx: 78 installations, 68 open, 66 with a 2025 figure totalling 2,255,418 tCO2e, median 19,120 t, range 104 to 322,213 t; 44 of the 66 emitted under 25,000 t [S34]. **VERIFIED** (my count on the file). Largest: British Sugar Wissington 322,213 t, British Sugar Bury St Edmunds 227,756 t, Cargill Port of Liverpool CHP 154,179 t. For an inbound enquiry: a one-page brief on the site (emissions, sector, permits, public targets read by hand).

**Human review.** Georgia and Sanya choose who to contact and write the message. No automated outreach.

**What could go wrong.** Licence breaches (bulk SBTi use for sales, CDP without a licence, National Gas data); personal data handled without a lawful basis or privacy notice, and the dossier notes HotGreen's site has no privacy notice today; name matching errors (ARLANXEO as "Arla"); registered offices mistaken for plants; stale data (EU industrial data ends at 2024, GB missing after 2020); treating emissions as steam demand. Timing is the bigger issue: outbound starts after the 2027 deployment, so a weekly agent now would produce lists nobody works.

### 3.6 E. Investor update drafter

**What it would do.** Monthly, draft Georgia's investor email from a structured tracker (pipeline, grant milestones, hires, press), plus public items from A and B (for example GtR project status, press coverage).

**Human review.** Georgia writes the final version. She runs investor relations and Sanya could not answer investor questions [dossier].

**What could go wrong.** Confidential figures sent to an AI provider without suitable data terms; overclaiming progress (the CCEP demonstrator is a 50 kWth unit going in during 2027, not a trial underway) [dossier]; the draft sounding generic, which a founder with Georgia's narrative focus would reject. Value depends on whether a tracker exists; without one, this is a formatting aid, not an agent.

### 3.7 What AI cannot do for HotGreen, stated plainly for the proposal

- It cannot decide whether a rule applies to a customer's site. It can find the rule, quote it and flag it for a person.
- It cannot reconcile HotGreen's inconsistent savings, CO2 and temperature claims. Only the business case model's owner can.
- It cannot replace Charles on patents or a lawyer on the AI Act or data protection.
- It cannot build the relationship with CCEP's procurement team or with investors.
- It does not learn HotGreen's context by itself; someone has to feed it and correct it (the "learning gap" in [S76]).
- It should not publish anything, anywhere, without a named person approving it.

### 3.8 A rough order of build (INFERENCE)

1. A and B together as one weekly internal brief, sources D1 to D15 (leaving out D14 and D16 for anything public) and D26 to D33. Free data, mostly open licences, lowest risk.
2. C once HotGreen has one agreed set of numbers and Framer access is sorted.
3. D as a one-off list build before the first deployment, refreshed quarterly.
4. E only on Georgia's request.
