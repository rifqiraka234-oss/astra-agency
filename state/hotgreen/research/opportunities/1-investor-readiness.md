# HotGreen, investor readiness and narrative for a climate hardware seed raise

Research note for the Astra proposal. Written 25 September 2026. Research only, nobody was contacted, no form was submitted.

Context taken as given from `state/hotgreen/dossier.md` (not re researched here). HotGreen Ltd, company 16035994, about six people, about £1.9M equity raised (Oct 2025 pre seed led by Empirical Ventures with CCEP Ventures, June 2026 round with Ponderosa Ventures), a £399k Innovate UK grant for a 50 kWth demonstrator at a CCEP site going in around Q1 to Q2 2027, 100+ Accelerator Cohort 7, seed raise planned around Q3 2027. The site has no investor route, unlabelled backer logos, no news page.

**Labels.** VERIFIED means read on the primary source in this session. SECONDARY means trade press, aggregator, vendor blog or an interview published by a third party. COMPANY CLAIM means a company about itself. INFERENCE means my reasoning. UNVERIFIED means I could not open the source. Every page below was accessed on **25 Sep 2026** unless another date is given. Numbers in square brackets point to the source table.

## Headline findings

1. **Seed for climate hardware is harder by deal count, not by money.** Three independent datasets agree on the direction. Global seed deal count fell 19% in 2025 [6], H1 2026 had the lowest climate deal count on record [7], and UK seed deal numbers fell 27% in 2025 and took longer to close [9][10]. Capital is concentrating in fewer, larger, later deals [5][7]. The bar is higher, not closed [8].
2. **The website is a checking surface, not the decision tool.** The strongest primary evidence is from HotGreen's own accelerator. The 100+ Accelerator says a website is mandatory and "should clearly present your technology, traction, and company information to help evaluators assess your solution" [21]. Decks and meetings still carry the decision [11][62].
3. **What named investors say they check** is technology fundamentals and techno economics, industrialisation and time to scale, IP, team, and evidence behind every claim [1][18][19][15][60]. Climate funds also check that impact numbers are consistent and conservative [14].
4. **No private climate hardware peer I checked has an "Investors" page.** Of eleven startup homepages sampled, none has an Investors page. Only two mention investing at all. Tokamak Energy lists an investor relations email in its footer [39], and Antora links out to press coverage of its rounds [37]. The common pattern is a dated newsroom, named funders in text and a newsletter sign up [32][33][34][35][36].
5. **Two UK rules shape any investor page.** A public "invest with us" message risks being a financial promotion under FSMA section 21 [55][56]. And the standard Innovate UK grant terms require grantees to acknowledge Innovate UK, with the logo, in any online material about the funded project [25]. HotGreen's site mentions neither the grant nor Innovate UK (dossier).
6. **HotGreen is publicly listed at TRL 4** on the Net Zero Technology Centre site [29], with no date or scale on its own site. A dated, scale named "where we are" statement is the honest fix.

### 2.3 What counts as proof, per the sources

Technology fundamentals and techno economics [18], validated architecture and unit economics [3], IP [1], a team that owns the journey [1], evidence behind every statement [60], traction that is "immediately obvious" [11], climate numbers that are consistent, transparent and conservative [14], pilot readiness and "clearly prove its value" [21], third party validation such as advisers or NEDs [62].
