## Investors first, and customers are already coming in

The seed round is planned for around the third quarter of 2027, after the first unit goes into a Coca-Cola Europacific Partners site in the first half of 2027.

Customers are already coming to you through industry contacts and accelerators. Outbound can wait until
there's real world data to show.

You want HotGreen to read as "a very credible and reliable equipment provider rather than a startup", and
you don't want to lose what makes it different.

You'd like to handle the quick fixes, SEO and a news page, in house. Later on you'd like a smaller version
of the business case model you share with customers on the site, and something on the Scope 1, 2 and 3
and ETS rules that keep changing.

## Your proof already exists. It lives on other people's sites

Coca-Cola Europacific Partners names HotGreen in its 2025 annual report as one of three startups it
invested in that year. UK Research and Innovation lists your Innovate UK grant for a 50 kW thermal
demonstration at a CCEP site, running from June 2026 to May 2027. Empirical, Tech.eu and Vestbee
all cover the raise.

Your homepage says "Some of our key funders and partners are" and then shows logos, with no names in the
text. The raise, the grant and the demonstrator aren't mentioned.

The savings figure changes with where you read it. The Solutions page says "Reduce your energy bill by
30%". The LinkedIn page says energy costs 40% lower "compared to competitors". Empirical's announcement
says "up to 50% energy savings". They measure different things, and a reader sees three numbers.

The 100+ Accelerator's own guidance says a website "should clearly present your technology, traction, and
company information to help evaluators assess your solution". That's the standard we'd build to.
