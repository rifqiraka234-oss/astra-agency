# HotGreen proposal page, version 3. Design note

Raka, 25 Sep 2026: "make the whole layout easier to understand ... the numbers, the buttons ... use real
pictures ... think about the flow, where they start, what they do next, what they read ... declutter it ...
improve the quality really really really ... right now it just feels like it's very cheap".

## What the research says (read 25 Sep 2026)

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| Show only the few most important things first, the rest on request. Two levels at most, a clearly labelled trigger, one way in | NN/g, Progressive Disclosure, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/ |
| Every element that doesn't serve the message is noise. Empty space makes the message stronger. Pictures are remembered better than words. Large, legible, clear contrast and hierarchy | Garr Reynolds, design tips, https://www.garrreynolds.com/design-tips |
| One idea per slide, and a three second test for each | SlideModel and others, https://slidemodel.com/one-topic-per-slide/ |
| Customise the summary with what the buyer told you. Pricing is the most viewed part, interactive choice helps. Show the team with photos and titles | Proposify, https://www.proposify.com/blog/proposal-design-best-practices |
| Headings, short paragraphs, visuals, one specific call to action | Zendesk, Pipedrive, PandaDoc proposal guides |

## What was wrong with version 2

- 25 cards, each with a small sketch, a paragraph, a date and a button. Too much at once.
- Grey placeholder bars, dashed boxes and tiny mockups read as cheap.
- Four kinds of tag on the sketches (Sketch, Example, Real data, Your specs).
- Stock refinery photos that have nothing to do with food and drink.
- 3,400 words.

## Version 3

**The path, seven numbered steps, one idea each.** A slim bar at the top shows where you are.

| Step | One idea | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Your proof is stronger than your website | Press Start |
| 1 Where you are | Investors first, customers already coming in | Read three lines |
| 2 What we found | Your best proof isn't on your website | Look at two columns |
| 3 What we'd build | Six parts, A to F | Open any part to see it |
| 4 When | Which part fits which month | Glance at four columns |
| 5 Where we'd start | Start with B and D | One card |
| 6 Who we are | One team, real work | Three projects, two people |
| 7 Next step | Pick your parts on a 30 minute call | Press Email me |

**Progressive disclosure for the six parts.** Level one is six tiles, each a letter, a name, one line on
what it is, and a real picture of the example. Level two opens when they press "See it", one panel with a
large, finished looking example for each item and a short list on the side. Pick per part (A to F), not per
item, so there are six choices instead of 25.

**Examples at full size.** Every item has its own screen, designed 1200 wide at its own height and filled with HotGreen's
real facts wherever they exist, their logo, their product image, their specs, their published numbers, their
backers, the register data. Anything invented to show a layout is marked "Example" once, on that screen.
Results that would need their model are blurred and say so, never a made up figure.
