## 3. The finding that shapes the calculator and their market focus

My arithmetic on official prices, every input checked twice. 1 MW of heat for 6,000 hours a year, COP
2.8 (their spec), against a gas boiler at 85.7% efficiency (US DOE benchmark). Prices are Eurostat
industrial averages for H2 2025 (dataset updated 24 September 2026, pulled through the Eurostat API)
and DESNZ UK non domestic averages for Q1 2026 including the Climate Change Levy. Carbon is the EU CBAM
reference €75.28 per tonne for Q2 2026 and the UK ETS 2026 price of £49.41. The gas factor is 0.18231
kgCO2e per kWh from the UK government's 2026 conversion factors.

| Market | Electricity to gas price ratio | Saving a year, energy only | Saving a year, with carbon |
|---|---|---|---|
| Netherlands | 2.05 | €148,509 | €244,595 |
| France | 1.90 | €130,867 | €226,953 |
| Germany | 2.92 | €40,478 | €136,564 |
| Belgium | 3.29 | minus €1,582 | €94,504 |
| Ireland | 4.31 | minus €102,190 | minus €6,103 |
| UK, large users | 5.37 | **minus £200,707** | minus £137,641 |

A heat pump at COP 2.8 only beats that boiler on running cost when electricity costs less than about
3.3 times gas.

**What this means.**
- Their headline "€250k a year saved for a typical facility" matches the Netherlands with carbon priced
  in. It's a Continental number.
- In their home market the case rests on things an average can't show. The site's own contract prices,
  a cheap power deal, an internal carbon price (CCEP uses €100 a tonne on Scope 1 and 2 capex, per its
  20-F), waste heat (their spec gives CoP 4.5 from 50°C), and any subsidy.
- Carbon only bites on big sites today. The EU ETS covers sites over 20 MW and EU ETS2 arrives in 2028.
- It fits their March job ad preferring German speakers and the euros on their site. That's inference.
- **None of the 14 competitors we profiled offers an open savings calculator, and this is probably why.**
  A generic one would embarrass them in the UK. One built on their own model, with the assumptions shown
  and a country switch, is a real edge.
- Their CO2 claim, 1,500 t a year per MW, needs about 7,050 running hours on zero carbon power, or an
  older, less efficient boiler. It's defensible with the assumptions stated and risky without them.

This is internal. We don't lead with "your product loses money in the UK". We say the calculator has to
run on their model, with their assumptions, per country.
