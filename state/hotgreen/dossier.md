## 4. What Sanya said, pain by pain

Each pain in her words, then what it means, and what we still do not know. Nothing here is our
opinion of the site yet, the site audit tests her claims separately.

### Pain 1. The site is a year behind the company
> "the website wasn't really clearly reflecting the stage of where the business was at. And that is
> because it hasn't really been updated in about a year."

What has happened since the site was last touched, per Sanya. Accepted onto an accelerator programme
with Coca-Cola Europacific Partners. First product deploying in a Coca-Cola plant, aimed at Q1 to Q2
2027. In talks with other food and beverage majors. Per Companies House, a second funding round in
June 2026. Per her own hire, a commercial function now exists.

### Pain 2. Search basics are broken, so they do not show up
> "all our title pages have the same name. There's no kind of [alt] text in any of the images. A lot of
> the things are standalone images rather than HTMLs. So we're not really showing up."

Her diagnosis and her own priority. Tested in the site audit file.

### Pain 3. Everyone walks the same path
> "we're going to have a lot of investors and similar coming to the site anyways ... the website
> [isn't] optimized currently for that. It kind of everyone has to go down the same path."

Two audiences, investors (seed raise) and customers (inbound pipeline). This is the same thing our
August opener and the deck said, and she agreed with it on the call ("I identified, same as you
guys").

### Pain 4. It reads as a startup, and they are selling to the biggest buyers in food and drink
> "we don't really want to show that we're really a team of 6 because we are trying to engage with the
> big dogs. Obviously, going with Coca-Cola, we're in talks with other food and beverage majors. So we
> want to essentially show ourselves as a very credible and reliable equipment provider rather than a
> startup."

This is the one closest to money. The buyers are procurement and engineering teams at food and
beverage majors, and the thing being bought is capital equipment that sits in a production line for
years. A buyer like that is checking whether the supplier will still exist in ten years.

### Pain 5. Mission against credibility, and they want both
> "when looking at bigger companies and other equipment providers, I realized that they [weren't]
> mission focused and they really were building their site around being a credible equipment
> provider. So it's almost how do we integrate both together? Because mission focus, for me, shouts
> start up."
> "we're not scared to be different ... hot green really [isn't] a standard name that you'd have for a
> heat pump ... we don't want to kind of follow the blueprint of our competitors ... but we also want
> to show ourselves as a real player in the field. ... not like small"

The positioning brief in her own words. A "new gen equipment provider". Different, but not small.

### Pain 6. Nobody in the building knows websites, and one person carries all of commercial
> "we're only a team of six. Yep, the majority being kind of very technical engineers. And Georgia
> ... was the only one kind of holding down the fort on the commercial marketing, outbound outreach."
> "none of us are particularly experienced with website development"

Sanya confirmed she does marketing, sales, comms and branding by herself. She is also the contact for
customers, investors, grant writers and agencies.

### Pain 7. Money, and the instinct to do it in house
> "being a startup, we tend to try and do a lot of things in-house just because with the cost
> associated with outsourcing, it's not always something that can be at the top of our priority list."
> "However, I think the website's such an important tool to do right."
> "We might say not right now, but potentially after the deployment when we essentially know that we
> have a product that works."

The real objection. It is not "no", it is "maybe later", tied to the 2027 deployment.

### Pain 8. Has the site cost them anything yet? Unknown
Raka asked directly. Sanya could not say, "I've only been here a month", but read Georgia's push for
a refresh before the seed raise as a sign "she's had some feedback". **Only Georgia can answer this,
and it is the question that decides how big the proposal can be.**
