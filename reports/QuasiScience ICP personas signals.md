# Start QuasiScience outbound with renewables bid pricing

The best first campaign for QuasiScience is a renewables bid pricing offer sent to about 50 UK and EU developers and independent power producers, timed to the UK Contracts for Difference (CfD) Allocation Round 8 results window, which opens no earlier than 27 November 2026 (30 days from today) and closes by 17 February 2027. It wins on four grounds. The buyers already pay outsiders for this kind of analysis, the clock is public and dated, the deal involves no personal data or security certification gate, and the signals can be read from government and regulator pages. KYC has the hardest regulatory clock (EU AMLR applies on 10 July 2027, 255 days away) and the richest signal list, but QuasiScience publicly shows no security certification and the notes judge regulated buyers likely to reject it without one. Manufacturing has a clean deadline (EU Machinery Regulation on 20 January 2027, 84 days away) that is shorter than a 7 to 16 week delivery plus scoping. The marketing mix optimiser (MMO) faces free tools and large-buyer proof demands. The honest caveat sits under all four. Nothing QuasiScience says about its own results could be verified from public pages, its testimonials name roles and not people, and the sourced "cost of doing nothing" figures describe markets, not any buyer's own loss. The strategy sprint therefore has to produce permission to cite figures, one named or verifiable reference, and a security and insurance pack before a single message goes out.

## Persona cards by sector

### Renewables and energy

**Head of Commercial, Origination, Market Modelling, or Director of Regulatory Affairs and Auctions (owns bid pricing)**
- **Goals** Win at a price that still clears the hurdle rate; bring a defensible number to the investment committee and lenders.
- **Pains** Over-shading and losing, or under-bidding and holding an uneconomic contract. AR8 freezes administrative strike prices at AR7 levels (solar 75, onshore wind 92, offshore wind 113, floating 271 GBP/MWh in 2024 prices) but adds separate offshore clearing prices by connection zone, which is a new pricing problem (sourced fact, [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/cfd-ar8-desnz-publishes-final-allocation-framework-and-confirms-key-auction-details)). Models they cannot inspect or rerun.
- **Objections** "Cornwall Insight or Baringa already do this"; "our spreadsheet works"; bid economics are commercially sensitive; a seven-person supplier.
- **Where they spend time** RenewableUK's Global Offshore Wind, 16 to 17 June 2027, ExCeL London, and Intersolar Europe, 8 to 10 June 2027, Munich (sourced facts, [Tethys](https://tethys.pnnl.gov/events/renewableuk-global-offshore-wind-2027), [Intersolar](https://www.intersolar.de/en/about)). Trade press and newsletters (RenewableUK, WindEurope, Current News, Recharge, Windpower Monthly, Montel, Modo Energy, Cornwall Insight) are inference.
- **Purchase triggers** AR8 result, win or lose; AR9 preparation; Gate 2 connection offers; German offshore redesign for 2027.
- **How to message** One dated, quantified hook and one question about how they set a strike price today. Offer a scoped sketch run on public AR7 results (inference), not "machine learning".

**CFO, Head of Project Finance, Investment Director (returns and lender view)**
- **Goals** Returns that survive cannibalisation; numbers lenders accept.
- **Pains** Solar capture factors fell to about 0.10 in April 2026 from about 0.42 a year earlier across five core EU markets, with negative price hours up to 139 from 90 (sourced fact, [PV Europe](https://www.pveurope.eu/markets/negative-pricing-surges-eu-solar-outpaces-grids)). This makes merchant tail and PPA assumptions volatile (inference). Developers say funding cannot close without formal grid offers, which have slipped ([sourced fact](https://www.forvismazars.com/uk/en/insights/energy-infrastructure-and-environment-insights/navigating-asset-valuations-on-grid-reform)).
- **Objections** Model risk, auditability, lender acceptance of a non-standard model.
- **Where** Finance and trade press; infrastructure fund networks (unresearched).
- **Triggers** Gate 2 phase 2 offers by 31 January 2027; financial close; refinancing; M&A.
- **How to message** A rerunnable returns simulation that re-prices when a capture rate or grid date moves.

**Head of Sustainability or ESG Reporting (Scope 3)**

## Recommended first campaign

**One product.** A rerunnable CfD and auction bid price optimiser, sold as a scoped sketch followed by a build (a hypothesis for QuasiScience to accept, since it must confirm it can deliver this and show an example). **One sector.** UK and EU renewable developers and IPPs. **About 50 accounts.** Build them from the AR8 applicant and result pages if NESO or GOV.UK publishes them, otherwise from the published AR7 results page (14 January 2026, [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/contracts-for-difference-cfd-allocation-round-7-results)), then add German participants after the 1 November and 1 December bid dates. The mix and tiering are for the sprint to decide. Seed with the people already in QuasiScience's referral network, since all past work was referral-driven (from brief).

The reasoning has five parts.

First, buyer habit. Developers already pay Cornwall Insight and Baringa for bid strategy, so the sale is a switch or an addition, not a category education job. The pitch is a tool they can rerun on their own cost and risk inputs, where an adviser hands over a point-in-time report (inference to validate in discovery).

Second, timing and signals. AR8 results will arrive between 27 November 2026 and 17 February 2027, and every bidder will review the outcome, win or lose. Public primary pages carry the calendar, and no personal data processing is needed to know who the accounts are, which keeps the GDPR exposure small compared with KYC.

Third, the weakest links are the most fixable. Energy buyers are less likely than banks to demand ISO certification, but they will still ask about key person risk, references and IP. The sprint can address those, while a KYC campaign would first have to close the certification, DORA and unverified Padova gaps.

Fourth, the alternatives are worse now. KYC is the best second wave, because its clock is the longest and hardest and its signals the richest. The notes say the product probably cannot sell to regulated banks without certification, so the first KYC test should be fintechs, EMIs and CASPs where procurement is lighter (inference), with the Cyber Essentials, ISO readiness and DORA pack built during the energy campaign. Manufacturing needs a verified case study and a customer-sized test cost before it is worth building an account list. MMO competes with free tools and needs a validated uplift design first.

Fifth, expected sales mechanics are unknown. The only benchmarks found are SaaS-derived, vendor-published and inconsistent. They give about 70 days for a $100k deal (Gong via [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/gong-100k-deals-take-about-days-to-close)), 192 days for enterprise SaaS (Ebsta, via [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/enterprise-sales-metrics), search summary only), and a 15 to 25 percent placeholder win rate that the notes label an inference. No benchmark for cold outbound reply rates among technical boutiques was found. Define success in meetings held and scoped sketches requested, and set the targets from QuasiScience's own CRM history. Outbound drafts should go to Raka for explicit approval before anything sends, as Astra's own rule requires.

The counter-argument is real. The energy notes found no sourced figure for what a bid pricing error costs a developer, and the Ortus Climate proof point could not be located on the site. The campaign leans on the sourced market context and the buyer's own sizing, not a QuasiScience result. If QuasiScience cannot produce one verifiable energy reference within the sprint, the campaign should open as a research-led, no-claims message and a request for a conversation.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "which sector is biggest" to "which sector lets a seven-person firm without public references win a first meeting". Every sector has a real regulatory or market clock, and the biggest dated pain (AMLR) points at the buyers least able to accept a supplier with no security certification. The energy ICP offers a smaller pain, a weaker sourced cost story, and a friendlier buyer. That tradeoff favours starting there, with KYC held for a second wave once the procurement pack exists. The data also implies that QuasiScience's own website is the first obstacle. Its anonymous testimonials, copy-pasted case card and unnamed awards will undermine the strongest outbound message if a buyer checks, which they will.

The practical implication for the strategy sprint is that the product is not a message template. It is a proof pack with a named or verifiable reference, a certification path, a published entry price, and signed permission to quote figures. Without it, a well-timed Clay and Lemlist workflow will book meetings that stall at due diligence. With it, the timing evidence gathered here (AR8 results from 27 November 2026, AMLR on 10 July 2027, Machinery Regulation on 20 January 2027) gives Raka a sequenced plan for three campaigns across the next nine months, each tied to a date a buyer already has in their diary.
