# Astra prices Kyson's system under agency floors

Clay and GTM engineering agencies sell workflow builds as a monthly retainer, and the floor they signal is about **$5,000 a month on a three month minimum**. Revenanas, the Dutch Clay partner, publishes no price anywhere but tags itself "$5k+ a month" with a 3 month minimum in Clay's partner directory, and the agencies that do publish land at €4,000 to €8,000 a month (closed:in), £6,500 (GTM Studios) and $5,000 to $7,000 (The GTM Engineering Company). Setup fees run from nothing to $8,000, almost nobody prices a single workflow, and most established shops refuse performance pay in writing. Freelancers sit far below, with Fiverr starting prices at a median of $20 to $50, credible sellers charging $150 to $1,000 for one Clay table or one workflow, and French freelancers quoting €800 to €1,500 for a simple workflow. Astra's option A (€3,500 setup, €750 a month) lands at the low end of small agency setup fees and inside the maintenance retainer band, and its whole first year of €12,500 costs about what closed:in charges for three months of its cheapest plan. Option B (€1,500 setup, €350 a month, €2,500 per signed mandate) uses the hybrid shape the guides favour, but it gives Kyson the risk premium for free and pays Astra less than option A unless three sellers found by the system sign inside year one. The recommendation is to keep A as drafted with a three month minimum, keep B's setup and monthly fee with a six month minimum, and raise B's mandate fee to **€3,500**, so both options cost Kyson the same at two mandates, the low end of the twelve month goal he gave on the call, with every tool billed in his name.

## Clay agencies like Revenanas ask $5,000 a month and turn away solo founders

Revenanas is a real Clay partner in the Netherlands, an Advanced Artisan whose directory record lists English and Dutch, a "$5k+ a month" budget tag and a 3 month minimum engagement ([Clay directory entry](https://www.clay.com/en/experts/partner/revenanas)). It grew out of a lead generation shop called Leadbananas, founded by Jack Cane and Dominykas Rukas, and claims 75+ client processes automated ([Revenanas About](https://www.revenanas.com/about)). Its services page sells "Fractional GTM Engineering Services" and shows no price, and neither does any other page on the site. It sells with a free Clay audit and a free workflow library instead ([Revenanas services](https://www.revenanas.com/service); [Revenanas Clay](https://www.revenanas.com/clay)). The homepage names the client it wants, with a "$15K+ deal size", "5+ sales reps" and a team that has moved past founder led selling ([Revenanas homepage](https://www.revenanas.com/)). So the entry ticket is at least **$15,000 for the first quarter**, and a one person advisory fails two of those tests before price comes up. An actual Revenanas quote needs a call.

Revenanas is typical of its peer group. Of the 186 partners in Clay's directory, 65 tag themselves "$5k+ a month" and 17 "$10k+ a month", 48 say any budget and 56 leave it blank, while 98 ask for three months, 59 for one, 19 for six and one for twelve ([Clay directory](https://www.clay.com/experts)). The directory never shows a project price, only those coarse self chosen bands. The biggest names screen harder. Frontal, the renamed ColdIQ agency, carries the same $5k+ tag and guarantees its data work by week four or refunds $25k, which implies its 90 day build costs at least that ([Clay directory, Frontal AI](https://www.clay.com/en/experts/partner/frontal-ai)). Growth Engine X lists "Lifetime Gross Profit Under $3,000" and companies with no sales system to work the leads as usually not a fit ([Growth Engine X](http://coldoutbound.com/)).

Four agencies publish real numbers, and they agree with the directory.

Per build pricing is rare. closed:in is the only agency found with a price for a standalone build, €2K to €6K for a Clay waterfall with AI research and CRM sync, and it bills its unpublished setup fee together with month one and answers no when asked about performance pricing ([closed:in pricing](https://www.closedin.io/pricing)). B2B Boosted sells the whole system once from £4,000 and lets the client keep it ([B2B Boosted](https://www.b2b-boosted.com)). A survey of 228 practitioners puts the median agency fee at $5K to $8K a month, finds about 70% of agency operators on monthly retainers, and puts performance bonuses at 10% to 20% on top of the base fee ([GTME Pulse](https://gtmepulse.com/careers/agency-pricing/)). Tools come on top as well. UK Clay partners bill Clay credits and data separately ([Salesforge UK list](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/clay-agencies-uk)), and one agency writes that some of its peers mark tools up 20% to 40% ([Forma Nôrden](https://formanorden.com/blog/gtm-engineering-cost/)). What these agencies compete on is ownership, with GTM Studios, B2B Boosted, Growth Engine X and Revenanas all promising the client keeps every table, domain and sequence ([GTM Studios](https://www.gotomarketstudios.com)).

## RevOps and outbound shops charge for setup and mostly refuse to work on results

RevOps agencies publish more, and their cards share one shape, a cheap fixed entry product that feeds a long retainer. RevPartners sells HubSpot implementations from $1,500 to $4,000 in 14 to 30 days, a RevOps retainer from $9,850 to $27,000 a month on a six month initial term plus a $5,000 onboarding fee, and a $25,000 Clay implementation over 90 days ([RevPartners pricing](https://revpartners.io/pricing)). If every capped hour gets used, that's roughly $200 to $230 an hour. European shops charge about half. Addmark in the Netherlands starts at €4,500 a month for 30 to 40 hours with no setup fee and quarterly notice ([Addmark pricing](https://www.addmark.nl/en/services/revops-as-a-service/pricing)), and Bound in the UK charges £2,750 or £3,750 a month, or £2,000 for a one off block of 20 hours ([Bound pricing](https://www.boundm.com/packages-pricing)). No rate card prices a single workflow. An Irish agency puts workflow setup at €2,500 to €5,000 ([About Inbound](https://www.aboutinbound.com/blog/hubspot-consultant-pricing-brwhat-it-really-costs-to-hire-expert-help)), and a dataset of 600+ accepted HubSpot projects gives a $5,700 median for medium jobs such as workflow automation ([Profoundly](https://profound.ly/blog/the-real-price-of-hubspot-help-the-first-ever-data-backed-benchmark)). The cheapest real agency retainers start at $2,000 at INSIDEA, which locks clients in for 6 to 12 months, and $2,500 at MASH, which doesn't ([INSIDEA](https://insidea.com/hubspot/managed-operations); [MASH](https://marketingandsaleshelp.com/revops-partner-retainer/)). A UK developer agency puts maintenance only retainers at £800 to £2,000 a month ([SpotDev](https://www.spotdev.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-a-hubspot-developer-cost-uk)).

Outbound agencies split on setup fees. CIENCE charges $5,000 for a five day setup sprint, then $2,000 a month for the team plus $499 for its platform, month to month ([CIENCE pricing](https://www.cience.com/pricing)). Danish Lead Co charges $8,000 for a 45 day build and then $3,000 to $6,000 a month with a three month minimum ([Danish Lead Co pricing](https://danishleadco.io/outbound-lead-generation-pricing)). SalesHive and SalesRoads charge nothing to start and let clients cancel any time ([SalesHive pricing](https://saleshive.com/pricing/); [SalesRoads pricing](https://salesroads.com/pricing/)). Vendor guides put setup at $500 to $2,000 ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/appointment-setting-pricing-models)), or $500 to $3,000 with three months as the minimum most reputable shops ask for ([Tomba](https://tomba.io/blog/cold-email-agency-pricing)). The managed floor is about $2,000 to $3,800 a month, and Sopro, a UK agency, starts at about $3.8K with no minimum contract ([Sopro pricing](https://sopro.io/pricing/)). For UK buyers, a data vendor's guide puts cold email programmes at £3,000 to £8,000 a month, an average meeting at £250 to £500, and an in house SDR at £60K to £67K a year all in ([Prospeo UK](https://prospeo.io/s/lead-gen-companies-uk)).

Performance pay is where the guides and the agencies part ways. SalesHive "does not price by the meeting", Leadium gives no performance guarantees, closed:in says no, and Danish Lead Co sells its M&A programme "with no success fee" ([SalesHive pricing](https://saleshive.com/pricing/); [Leadium pricing](https://www.leadium.com/pricing); [Danish Lead Co, M&A](https://danishleadco.io/industries/investment-banking-m-and-a)). CIENCE is the exception, with a per meeting fee worked out from an agreed return target, usually four times ([CIENCE pricing](https://www.cience.com/pricing)). The vendor guides describe a hybrid of $2,000 to $4,000 a month plus $150 to $400 per qualified meeting, and warn that a base under $3,000 "may not provide enough base coverage" ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/appointment-setting-pricing-models)). They quote revenue shares of 5% to 15% of closed deals, workable only where sales cycles run under six months ([Reachoutly](https://reachoutly.com/cold-email/agency-pricing/)), and say pure performance deals cost the client 30% to 50% more because the agency prices in its risk ([Prospeo cold email](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-email-agency-pricing)). The complaints repeat across sources. Pay per meeting fills calendars with weak meetings and burns sending domains to hit volume, and once commission enters a contract, attribution becomes the fight ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/appointment-setting-pricing-models); [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/pricing-and-payment-terms-for-uk-lead-generation-agency-contracts/)).

Sell side origination for M&A advisers barely exists as a priced category. The one agency found with a price, Danish Lead Co, sells it from $4,000 a month with no success fee and a 90 day minimum per thesis, says owner replies start three to six weeks after its sending setup settles, and calls it "the thinnest category in the market". Its own comparison article lists the same service from $3,500 ([Danish Lead Co, M&A](https://danishleadco.io/industries/investment-banking-m-and-a); [Danish Lead Co comparison](https://danishleadco.io/blog/best-b2b-lead-generation-agencies-private-equity-ma)). What a signed seller is worth sets the scale for any bonus. UK advisers charge success fees of 3% to 5% on £1m to £10m sales and work fees anywhere from £0 to £55k ([Forbes Burton](https://www.forbesburton.com/?p=20245)), so a £3m sale at 3%, the rate Kyson used as his own example, pays him **£90,000** ([Acquitas call analysis](../logs/meetings/2026-10-07-Acquitas-analysis.md)). A UK legal guide puts introducer fees on business sales at 1% to 10% of deal value, paid on completion ([Sprintlaw finder's fees](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/understanding-finders-fee-agreements-what-uk-businesses-need-to-know/)), and an older practitioner post that couldn't be opened says brokers pass 10% to 20% of their own fee to introducers ([AccountingWeb](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/blogs/clinton/fee-scandal-business-brokers-corporate-finance)). Completion is far from certain. US brokers surveyed by the IBBA saw as many deals terminate as close in the last three months of 2022 ([IBBA Market Pulse](https://www.ibba.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/q4_2022_market_pulse.pdf)).

## Freelancers sell one workflow for a few hundred dollars

The bottom of the market is a Fiverr search. Across 48 n8n gigs the median starting price is $50 and the median hourly offer $20, across 31 Clay gigs the median start is $25, and across 48 lemlist gigs it's $20, with sellers in Nigeria, Pakistan and Bangladesh dominating the results ([Fiverr n8n](https://www.fiverr.com/gigs/n8n); [Fiverr Clay](https://www.fiverr.com/gigs/clay); [Fiverr lemlist](https://www.fiverr.com/gigs/lemlist)). Those starting prices buy a scoped down first package, and Fiverr adds a 5.5% buyer fee. The package ladders of established sellers show what a working system costs. A Top Rated seller in India charges $150 for one Clay table, $450 for enrichment plus CRM and $750 for a full outbound engine ([Fiverr, riteshosta](https://www.fiverr.com/riteshosta/be-your-clay-automation-expert)). A Dutch seller charges $1,500 for a CRM lead engine with eight automations and $2,460 for a full B2B engine ([Fiverr, sanderver](https://www.fiverr.com/sanderver/build-complete-crm-automation-systems-to-boost-your-b2b-lead-generation)), and an Israeli seller's ladder runs $1,000, $2,000 and $4,000 ([Fiverr, dbpmark](https://www.fiverr.com/dbpmark/build-n8n-automations-ai-agents-and-smart-business-workflows)). On Contra a Romanian builder sells one clearly defined n8n or Make workflow from $750 ([Contra](https://contra.com/s/AFBPoMMr-build-a-n8n-or-make-automation-in-7-days)).

Western European freelancers charge by the day. A French guide puts confirmed n8n consultants at €450 to €700 a day and seniors at €700 to €950, a simple workflow at €800 to €1,500, a workflow with API calls, error handling and documentation at €2,000 to €4,500, and a full sales system at €6,000 to €15,000 ([lefreelance.fr](https://lefreelance.fr/articles/devenir-consultant-automation-n8n-freelance/)). The same guide prices maintenance at **€300 to €600 a month** for light monitoring and small fixes, and €800 to €1,500 for monitoring with continuous improvement. Clay's own community gives smaller numbers, $250 to $350 per table for someone building a portfolio, $300 to $400 for a basic pipeline rising to $700 to $1k+ with CRM sync, and $1k to $3k for a small workflow build followed by a retainer ([Clay community, tables](https://community.clay.com/x/gtm-career-advice/vbth4zxangcs/pricing-strategy-for-table-making-charging-250-350); [Clay community, pipeline](https://community.clay.com/x/general/msg_Bo4AkTP9F3OJ/how-to-price-setting-up-a-clay-pipeline-for-client); [Clay community, rates](https://community.clay.com/x/general/msg_XQYtcfdRGaHe/typical-pricing-and-rates-for-gtm-engineering-serv)). Senior contract work runs higher, with a fractional GTM engineer role posted at $80 to $90 an hour for US and European candidates ([Clay community job post](https://community.clay.com/x/part-time-jobs/b0d6qfabon6z/hiring-fractional-gtm-and-operations-engineer-for)), and the practitioner survey puts solo GTM operators at $3K to $5K a month per client ([GTME Pulse](https://gtmepulse.com/careers/agency-pricing/)).

The useful reading is where the gap sits. On setup there's almost no daylight between the best packaged freelancer systems ($750 to $4,000 once) and the cheapest agency builds (€2K to €6K at closed:in, £4,000 at B2B Boosted). The gap is on the monthly line, where freelancers sell maintenance in the hundreds and agencies sell managed programmes in the thousands.

## Option A sits inside the market, option B gives away the risk premium

Kyson's tool bill is the cheapest LinkedIn plus email stack in the research. lemlist Multichannel costs $109 a month, or $87 on yearly billing, and it's the cheapest lemlist plan with LinkedIn automation, since the $69 Email plan has none ([lemlist pricing](https://www.lemlist.com/pricing)). Claude Pro costs $20 a month, or $17 a month on a $200 yearly plan ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). That's **$129 a month**, against $138 for Smartlead plus HeyReach plus an AI seat, and $331 plus €24 for a Clay centred stack with Instantly, HeyReach and n8n, which rises by another $310 if the build needs Clay's HTTP API or CRM sync ([Clay pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing); [HeyReach pricing](https://www.heyreach.io/pricing); [Smartlead pricing](https://www.smartlead.ai/pricing)). The owner data costs nothing. Companies House gives its API free at up to 600 requests every five minutes and publishes a free monthly file of every live company ([Companies House rate limits](https://developer-specs.company-information.service.gov.uk/guides/rateLimiting); [Companies House download](https://download.companieshouse.gov.uk/en_output.html)), where FAME starts at £34,100 a year for up to five users on a 12 month contract ([G-Cloud 14 pricing](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-14/documents/720211/761658342676099-pricing-document-2024-05-01-1447.pdf)). Two small costs are missing from the draft. lemlist's data add on is $20 a month for 400 emails' worth of credits if lemlist has to find owners' addresses, and if the ranking and drafting run unattended they'll call the Claude API, about $12 per 1,000 leads on Sonnet 5.5 under the research notes' token assumptions ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). Billing tools in Kyson's name matches the open end of the market, where Addmark puts software licences in the client's name and others mark them up.

On setup, option A's €3,500 sits at the low end of what small agencies charge for a whole system. It's under B2B Boosted's £4,000 floor, inside closed:in's €2K to €6K for a Clay build, below CIENCE's $5,000 and Danish Lead Co's $8,000, and well under the €6,000 to €15,000 French freelancers quote for a full sales system. What Astra builds for that money goes past a single table. It pulls owners from Companies House, ranks them and drafts messages with AI, and runs LinkedIn and email sequences in lemlist, so €3,500 isn't high. Option B's €1,500 matches the top of the French simple workflow band and the Dutch Fiverr seller's $1,500 lead engine, and it sits inside the $500 to $2,000 setup band in the outbound guides.

The monthly fees are priced as maintenance. €750 sits just under the French €800 to €1,500 band for monitoring with continuous improvement and under SpotDev's £800 floor, and €350 sits inside the €300 to €600 light monitoring band. Against managed programmes both are a fraction. closed:in starts at €4,000, so €750 is under a fifth of its cheapest plan, and Revenanas signals $5,000+, Danish Lead Co's M&A programme $4,000 and the cheapest real RevOps retainers $2,000 to $2,500. That comparison holds only if Astra doesn't run the campaigns. The analysis note still lists "setting up and running the outreach with him" as a point to confirm with Raka ([Acquitas call analysis](../logs/meetings/2026-10-07-Acquitas-analysis.md)). If the monthly fee ends up covering sequence writing and reply handling, it's priced far below every managed floor found, and the scope needs writing down before the price goes out.

As drafted, option B costs Astra €6,800 of first year fees against option A and earns it back only at 2.72 mandates, which in practice means three sellers signed from the system's list inside the year. In year two, with no setup fee, the crossover falls to 1.92. The market builds hybrids the other way round. GTM agencies add bonuses worth 10% to 20% on top of a full base, and the outbound guides keep hybrid bases at $2,000 to $4,000 ([GTME Pulse](https://gtmepulse.com/careers/agency-pricing/); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/appointment-setting-pricing-models)). Option B cuts the base by more than half and asks mandates to make up the rest, which is the risk established agencies refuse and the guides say clients pay 30% to 50% extra to shift. It's worth less than £2,500, under 3% of the £90,000 a £3m sale at 3% pays Kyson, and below the 10% to 20% introducer convention even after it's halved for the IBBA odds of a deal completing.

## Raise B's mandate fee to €3,500 and put the terms in writing

Keep option A at €3,500 setup and €750 a month. Both numbers sit inside the bands above, and no benchmark argues for cutting them. Add the market's standard term, three months minimum and then month to month on 30 days notice, which is the closed:in pattern and the most common minimum in Clay's directory ([closed:in pricing](https://www.closedin.io/pricing); [Clay directory](https://www.clay.com/experts)). Write the monthly scope as an improvement retainer, a monthly refresh of the owner list from Companies House and one new sequence or test each month, with reply handling excluded unless it's priced as a managed service. Kyson's committed spend is then €5,750 for the first three months and €12,500 for a full year.

Keep option B's €1,500 setup and €350 a month, add a six month minimum, and raise the mandate fee to **€3,500**. A six month minimum is the term 19 Clay partners and RevPartners use, and UK contract guidance calls a minimum reasonable where there's real setup work ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/pricing-and-payment-terms-for-uk-lead-generation-agency-contracts/)). It makes B's guaranteed part €3,600, about what A charges for the build alone. At €3,500 a mandate the two options meet at two mandates (€12,700 against €12,500), the low end of the two or three deals Kyson gave as his twelve month goal, which the garbled call transcript leaves to confirm on the second call ([Acquitas call analysis](../logs/meetings/2026-10-07-Acquitas-analysis.md)). At three mandates B pays Astra 30% more than A, the bottom of the risk premium the guides describe. Kyson still pays less than £3,500 per signed seller, under the halved introducer floor of £4,500, and nothing at all in a year when the system finds nobody.

The mandate fee needs a tight definition to hold up. Trigger it on the signed engagement letter and Kyson's first retainer payment, so he pays from money he has just received, and never on completion, where long sale cycles and attribution fights sink revenue shares ([Reachoutly](https://reachoutly.com/cold-email/agency-pricing/)). Count a seller as found by the system only if the owner is on the system's dated list and was first contacted through it, with a written carve out for owners Kyson already knew, since UK solicitors report introducers claiming fees on clients the business found itself ([Huntley Legal](https://www.huntleylegal.co.uk/blog/post.php?s=2019-10-24-paying-an-introducer)). Set a 12 month tail after first contact, inside the six months to three years the UK sources show ([Sprintlaw finder's fees](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/understanding-finders-fee-agreements-what-uk-businesses-need-to-know/); [Huntley Legal](https://www.huntleylegal.co.uk/blog/post.php?s=2019-10-24-paying-an-introducer)). A UK solicitor should read the clause before it's signed, because no source found settles how introducer rules apply to an agency paid on mandate signature.

Keep every tool in Kyson's name and on his card, with lemlist Multichannel and Claude Pro on monthly billing for the minimum term and yearly after it, which takes the bill from $129 to about $104 a month. Put the $20 lemlist data add on and a few dollars of Claude API usage in the proposal, so the first tool invoice isn't a surprise. Then put B first. Kyson has no cash before his first deal, B costs him €2,550 in the first three months against A's €5,750, and at his own goal the two options cost the same.

## Conclusion

Revenanas and its peers aren't Kyson's alternative. They price at $15,000 a quarter and screen out one person firms, so his real choice is a Fiverr seller with a $750 to $4,000 package or Danish Lead Co's origination programme at $4,000 a month, and cheapest of all is running the same $129 of tools himself. Astra's case against those rests on two things none of them sell, a free Companies House data feed that stands in for a £34,100 database, and a fee that waits for a signed seller, which the established agencies refuse in writing.

The number that decides whether €3,500 is generous or tight is how many owner conversations it takes to sign one mandate, and no source measures it for UK sell side advisers. Track conversations, meetings and signed mandates from the first send, and reprice B at the six month mark on Kyson's own conversion, since no benchmark in this report can supply it.
