Week one of the new plan, 5 to 13 October

Every LinkedIn thread that moved this week, read in full, plus the email deals. What people said, what they ignored, which messages and which lists earned it, and what to change.

328 LinkedIn threads read one by one. 66 new accepts matched against lemlist's own campaign numbers. Three email deals read start to finish. Written 13 October, evening.

The short version

  1. Your sketch line is the best opener we've got. "I'd suggest X. Shall I send you a sketch of X?" got a yes from Chris Hale within three hours, 1 of 8 sent. The older "Shall I send you over what X looks like" opener got 1 yes from 39. Small numbers, but the right direction.
  2. Every real yes came after the owner had already said yes once. Builds sent after a yes turned into two calls and a "how do you bill?". Four builds sent without being asked got nothing back.
  3. Most of the no's were us being wrong about the business. Five people said no and gave a reason. In four of those, the gap we'd pointed at was a choice they'd made, wasn't theirs, or was on a site they'd just paid for.
  4. Budget sank or stalled three deals, and you're right that we could have asked sooner. Kyson told us on the call there was no money before his first deal. Tomatoworld is a foundation with no budget. Ciara said she wasn't earning much, and we built her a full site the next day.
  5. The plain bump gets fast answers and no yeses. 111 went out on 12 October. Six people answered within a day, none said yes, and one wrote "Stop spam please".
  6. W1b is the list that works. The founders list doesn't, and W1c never started. W1b gave us the yes and five warm hellos. Founders gave 0 replies from 11 openers, plus the stop request. W1c and W4 sent no invites at all, because the 18 a day ran out first.

Why the sketch line works

Both yeses had the same three things. A thing they could picture, named in their own words. A link to how they make money. And an offer to show it instead of a request to talk. Your "I'd suggest" line forces the first one, because you can't write it without naming the project.

So the fix is a short qualifying step before any proposal or any build bigger than a sketch. Find out three things on the first call. What they spent last time or have set aside, who signs, and when they need it. For a founder who hasn't made money yet, lead with a small paid first piece or the share of results option. Never send a full package first.

W1b is the list to feed. Founders isn't

  • W1b brought 31 accepts, five people who said hi without being pitched, and Chris Hale's yes. Twelve of its 31 accepts are in hospitality, food and drink, and five run shops. That's the work the sketch opener suits best.
  • The founders campaign has the highest accept rate (39%) and the worst replies. That's 0 replies from 11 openers, and the one stop request. It was built in your account without the owner rule, so its titles include a "Group Commercial Director". Seven of its sixteen accepts this week are professional services, including two agencies (Restless and Archipel Marketing), which makes them closer to competitors than to clients.
  • W5 has the lowest accept rate (19%), but its one answered opener was a yes. It worked because the angle was a deadline in Ulrich's market, not GDPR. That matches the 12 October rule, so research W5 leads for the bigger problem.
  • W1c and W4 never started. Nothing is wrong with either. Their setup matches W1b's step for step, and they're simply last in line for the shared 18 a day. So the 19 October comparison has no W4 data, and the 398 established owners in W1c haven't been touched.
  • The v0.1 backlog is close to used up. This week it got 9 openers, 5 second angles, 94 bumps, 8 nudges and 4 unasked builds, and none of that produced a new yes. What it did produce this week was the two calls, both from September builds.

Who said yes this week were owner run firms of one to ten people, with a business customer behind the website (office lunches, airline claims teams) and a reason to act now. Who said no were owners who'd just paid for a site, B2B sellers who price per customer, and owners whose minds were elsewhere.

Smaller things that cost us

  • Saying hi back, then going quiet. Seven people answered the connect note itself. We replied to each with a question about their business, and 0 of the 5 who've had time answered it. Today's warm sketch, an offer instead of a question, is the fix. Check on it Friday.
  • Three pitches in a week to strangers. The opener, a nudge four days later and a bump three days after that. Michaël said stop, and two others got the same run.
  • 111 messages in one day. All of them were sent by hand through lemlist, against the 20 a day the campaigns are set to. Nothing has been flagged, but spreading bumps over two or three days costs nothing.
  • Proof. "Brands like Unilever, AXA, Pertamina" invites the question Sergey asked, and we can't give links or references for partner work. Two or three Astra case pages we can link to would answer it. Qualigraf is ready now, and justclaims and Garnish could follow if Ulrich and Chris agree.
  • Our own mail server. On 9 October astraagency.nl refused the copies of your Kyson reply to josh@ and raka@ as spam. Kyson got his copy. The email carried a Google redirect link instead of the clean prototype link, and that's my best guess at the trigger. Worth a word with whoever runs that server.
