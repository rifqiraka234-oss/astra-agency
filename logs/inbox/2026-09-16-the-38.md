## How they were identified

The queue rows carried a name and a contactId and no company. One `get_inbox_conversations`
call on `sentOnly` filtered to v0.1 and dated 5 to 9 September returned all 38 with their
LinkedIn URLs in a single response, which is far cheaper than paginating `search_campaign_leads`.
The slug is then the key. Every identification below was accepted only when the slug itself
resolved to the company, never when a name match alone suggested it.

## What the falsification pass actually caught

Four claims died before they reached a draft, which is the point of running it.

- **Matvelo.** The blog URLs read `example-blog-1` through `example-blog-4`, which looks
  like unpublished theme placeholders. Fetching one returns a real article called Fundamentals
  of Effective Leadership. Only the slugs are leftover. The opener would have been wrong.
- **P&S Consulting Engineers.** I was about to say they have no careers page. They have one.
  Fetching it turned the angle into something better and true, the page body is two headings,
  Senior Engineer and Principal Engineer, with no description and no way to apply.
- **Aurevia Syndic.** `aurevia.be` returns a disabled Shopify store and is not her domain.
  Hers is `aureviasyndic.be`, and it already links a Klantenportaal running on app.dobby.be,
  so a missing portal claim would have been false. She stays a candidate with no angle forced.
- **Stephane Chalendard.** A search result made him the founder of ODEFY. That is a different
  person on a different slug. His slug is Gerflor France.
