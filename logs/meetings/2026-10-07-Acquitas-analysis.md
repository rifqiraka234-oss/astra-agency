# What we can do for Kyson. Analysis after the call, 7 Oct 2026

**In one line.** This year Acquitas lives or dies on signing sellers, and Kyson loses them in two places. He can only
reach as many owners as he can message by hand, and the ones he does reach walk away when the fee comes up. Both can be
fixed cheaply, before he hires anyone.

Built from the call transcript (logs/meetings/2026-10-07-Acquitas-call.md), his site read in full on 6 Oct, his
Companies House record, and the outside sources listed at the end, each opened on 7 Oct.

## What one deal is worth to him

An estimate, never for a message. His own example fee is 3% of the sale price. Firms in his sectors sell for roughly 6
to 10 times profit (CapEQ, Aug 2026). A firm making £500k to £1m profit sells for about £3m to £10m, so one completed
sale is worth roughly £90k to £300k to Acquitas. Anything we build that brings him one extra signed seller pays for
itself many times over. That's the pay test passed, as long as we price for a man with no deals closed yet.

## Where he loses sellers, step by step

| Step | How it works today | Where it leaks | How we know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finding owners | LinkedIn search by hand on Mondays, MDs who've run the business 15 years or more | LinkedIn caps his weekly invitations, he has no age, ownership or size data, the paid database is out of budget | His words on the call |
| Reaching them | One batch of LinkedIn messages a week, on Thursdays | One channel, one person, one day a week | His words on the call |
| Being trusted | A Wix site with no name, photo, track record, fee page or privacy page | A seller can't check who he is | Site read in full, 6 Oct |
| The fee talk | He explains it on the first call | Every seller who got close and stopped, stopped here | His words, "that's really been the main objection" |
| Running the sale | Him, alone | Once he has a mandate, there's no time left to find the next one | His words, plus Companies House shows him as sole director |

The fee leak is the biggest and best proven. The finding leak is the one that caps his growth. The running leak arrives
the day he signs his first mandate.

## What we can build, in the order I'd sell it

### 1. A fee first website. Fixes the biggest proven leak, cheapest and fastest

- His profile and founder story, which he said he needs "regardless".
- A plain page on how his fee works. What the retainer covers, that the success fee is only paid on completion, and what
  a seller gets for it. The seller reads it before the first call, so it's no longer a surprise on it.
- The valuation tool upgraded into "what you'd actually keep". A value range, then the tax at today's Business Asset
  Disposal Relief rate of 18% (gov.uk), then his fee, so the fee shows up as one line in the seller's own sum. It needs a
  clear "rough guide, speak to your accountant" note, because the relief works on gains, not the sale price.
- A privacy page. He has none, and he needs one before any email outreach anyway.
- One deal size range. His homepage says under £50m and his services page says under £20m.

Why it works. UK fees are all over the place, with work fees "anywhere between £0 and £55k" for small businesses and
success fees of 3% to 5% on £1m to £10m deals (Forbes Burton), so sellers arrive confused and wary. A direct competitor
in his niche, CapEQ, already leads with "You always know what we are doing, why, and what it costs. No hidden fees." and
offers a free exit readiness quiz. Kyson's site has neither.

### 2. A seller finding engine. Fixes the leak that caps his growth

- Built on free Companies House data. The monthly snapshot of every live company is free to download, and each
  director's record shows when they were appointed and their birth month and year. That's his "MD for 15 years" filter,
  plus owners close to retirement age, plus who holds the shares, plus sector codes and filed accounts for size.
- AI ranks the list and drafts a personal first message for each owner. lemlist sends on LinkedIn and email.
- Email to limited companies doesn't need prior consent under the email marketing rules, as long as he says who he is
  and offers an opt out. UK GDPR still covers named people, and sole traders do need consent (ICO). So limited companies
  only, which is who he targets anyway.
- It replaces the database he can't afford and the outreach hire he planned.

### 3. A short monthly note to the list. Keeps him remembered

41% of SME decision makers are considering an exit in the next five to ten years (Shawbrook, 2024), so most owners on
the list aren't ready this year. A short monthly note in his name, on what lifts a price, how the fee works, the tax, and
who's buying (Phenna, Normec, Kiwa, Certania and SOCOTEC are among the consolidators doing deals, per CapEQ), keeps him the
name they think of when they are ready. AI drafts it, he approves it. It also teaches the fee long before any call.

### 4. A deal room toolkit, after the first mandate

A buyer longlist from public deal news, first drafts of the teaser and the information memorandum, and one tracker for
buyers, NDAs and documents. It lets one person run two or three sales at once, which is his twelve month goal, before the
office and the hire.

### 5. His own week, folded into step 2

Call notes, follow ups and a simple record of every conversation, so nothing he's promised slips while he's doing it all.

Steps 1 to 4 are the four moves in the deck he's already seen, so the proposal continues the story he liked.

## What I wouldn't sell him now

A rebrand, social media management, a custom app, or anything large before his first deal. And no promised lead numbers
or "70 to 80% chance" claims about the AI. Say it ranks owners so he calls the likeliest first.

## How to package and price it. Your call, band €500 to €50k

- **Phase 1, launch the site.** Fixed and small, live within the fortnight the deck promised. It can land even with no
  budget.
- **Phase 2, the seller engine plus the monthly note.** A setup fee plus a monthly fee, or a lower setup plus a fixed
  bonus each time a seller from the list signs a mandate. I'd avoid a share of his completion fee. It only pays out when
  a sale completes, months after signing, and arguing over which seller came from where gets messy. A bonus at signing
  keeps your offer of a revenue share without those problems.
- **Phase 3, the deal toolkit.** Priced after his first completion.

## Risks

- He closes deal one and hires a person instead. Position the engine as cheaper than that hire, or as what makes the hire
  productive from day one.
- LinkedIn restricts accounts that automate hard. Keep volumes at what a person would send and let email carry the scale.
- Data rules. Privacy page and opt out first, limited companies only.
- He's short of cash until the first deal. Phase it, and keep phase 1 small.

## Web apps for the site that bring in sellers

Ranked by how directly each one fixes a leak from the table above.

| App | What the owner does | What Kyson gets | Leak it fixes | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What you'd keep | Moves the sliders, sees value, then tax, then the fee, then what lands in his account | A seller who's seen the fee before the first call | The fee talk | Small, it upgrades the tool already built |
| How a buyer sees your company | Types his company name, the app reads the public Companies House record and AI writes a one page buyer's view | The company number and a full record of a likely seller, before any call | Finding and trust | Medium |
| Exit plan builder | Says when he'd like to sell and what worries him, gets a dated plan by email | Owners one to five years out, with their timing and worries written down | Finding, the long wait | Medium |
| Who's buying in your sector | Reads recent deals in his niche and signs up for alerts | A growing list of owners watching the market, and material for the monthly email | Staying remembered | Medium, needs a monthly update |
| Niche value pages | Searches "what is my fire safety business worth" and lands on a page with the calculator set for that niche | Owners who find him on Google instead of him finding them | Finding | Small per page |
| Partner page for accountants and solicitors | An adviser refers a client who's thinking of selling | A second route in, people who hear about a sale before anyone else | Finding | Small |
| Buyer registration | An acquirer says what he's looking for | A buyer list for his buy side work, and something true to tell sellers when buyers ask for firms like theirs | Trust | Small |
| Ask first, anonymously, and book a call | Asks a question without naming the company, or books a confidential call | Fewer owners put off by having to identify themselves first | Trust | Small |

Notes on each.

- **How a buyer sees your company** is the one nobody in his niche offers as far as we've seen. The Companies House API
  needs only a registered key and allows 600 requests every five minutes (Companies House developer guidelines). It can
  read filing history, directors and how long they've been in post, registered loans and who owns the shares. Small
  companies file limited accounts, so the report has to say what it can and can't see.
- **Exit plan builder** speaks to the 41% thinking about an exit in five to ten years (Shawbrook), who won't fill in a
  contact form today but will take a plan.
- **Who's buying** and the **niche pages** only use sourced deals and numbers, the same rule as the deck.
- **Partner page.** Whether accountants and solicitors already refer to him is a question for the second call.
- **All of it needs a site we can build apps on.** Wix limits that, so ask whether he's happy to move off it. Our concept
  site is already built outside Wix.
