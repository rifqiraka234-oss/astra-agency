## 2. Written for someone who knows nothing

Every lesson in every track follows these rules. A reviewer checks them before anything is recorded.

1. **Every new word is explained the first time it appears**, in one plain sentence, and it's in the glossary
   (section 9). No lesson assumes the learner remembers a word from a lesson they skipped.
2. **One idea per lesson.** If a lesson needs the word "also" twice, it's two lessons.
3. **Show, then do.** Each lesson is a short explanation, then a screen recording of the exact clicks, then the learner
   does the same on their own data.
4. **Click by click for anything on screen.** "Open HubSpot, click Contacts in the top menu, click Import" and not
   "import your contacts".
5. **A finished example of every assignment**, plus one bad example with what's wrong with it. Learners copy the
   shape of the good one.
6. **A "stuck?" box under every assignment** with the three mistakes people make most and how to fix them.
7. **No jargon in the tests.** Questions use the same plain words as the lessons.
8. **Short.** Videos are 5 to 12 minutes. If it runs longer, it's split.

## 3. How a lesson is built

Every lesson in the track files is written in this shape.

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Title and length** | What it's called and how many minutes of video |
| **In plain words** | Two or three sentences a 12 year old would understand |
| **You'll be able to** | What the learner can do after it, written as actions |
| **What's shown** | The talking points and the screen demo, step by step, so the lesson can be recorded from it |
| **Do it now** | A 5 to 15 minute task right after the video, on their own data |

## 4. Assignments

**Every module ends with one assignment.** It's always something real the learner builds and hands in: a sheet, a table,
a live workflow, a sequence, a dashboard, a written page.

Each assignment in the track files has the same parts.

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | One sentence |
| **Time** | Honest estimate |
| **You need** | Tools, accounts, files, earlier assignments |
| **Steps** | Numbered, click by click where it's on screen |
| **Hand in** | Exactly what to submit and in what form (a link, a screenshot, a filled template) |
| **Marking rubric** | 4 to 6 criteria, each scored 0, 1 or 2 |
| **Stuck?** | The three most common mistakes and their fix |

**How the rubric scores.**

| Score | Means |
|---|---|
| 0 | Missing or wrong |
| 1 | There but incomplete, or works with problems |
| 2 | Done properly, matches the good example |

**Pass.** At least 70% of the points, and no 0 on a criterion marked **must**. A fail isn't the end. The learner fixes
what scored low and hands it in again. Self learn learners can resubmit as often as they like. 1:1 learners get two
reviewed resubmissions per assignment.

**Who marks it.**

| Format | Marking |
|---|---|
| Self learn | The learner marks their own work against the rubric and the good example, then ticks a self check box. Optional, a paid review by Raka (single review, €49) |
| 1:1 | Raka marks every assignment, writes feedback within 2 working days, and goes through it in the next session |

## 5. Tests

There are three kinds of test in every track.

### 5.1 Module quiz

| | |
|---|---|
| When | At the end of every module, before the assignment |
| Length | 8 to 10 questions, about 10 minutes |
| Question types | Multiple choice with one right answer, true or false, and short scenarios ("Anna has... what should she do first?") |
| Pass mark | 80% |
| Retakes | Unlimited, but questions are drawn from a pool so the second try isn't the same test. The platform shows which lesson to rewatch for each wrong answer |
| Answer key | Every question in the track files has its answer and a one line "why", which the learner sees after submitting |

The track files give each quiz's questions. When building on the course platform, write a second variant of each
question to make the retake pool.

### 5.2 Practical test

Track 1 has one at the halfway point and Tracks 2 and 3 have one before the final exam. It's timed and hands-on: "here's
a broken table, fix it in 45 minutes", "here's a messy CRM export, clean it". It's marked with a rubric like an
assignment.

### 5.3 Final exam

| | |
|---|---|
| When | After all modules are passed |
| Length | Track 1, 30 questions in 40 minutes. Tracks 2 and 3, 25 questions in 30 minutes. Timed |
| Mix | About 60% knowledge questions, 40% scenarios |
| Pass mark | 80% |
| Retakes | Two, at least 48 hours apart, from a different question pool |

### 5.4 Capstone

A project that uses everything in the track on the learner's own market, team or patch. It has its own brief and rubric
in each track file. In 1:1 it's presented to Raka in the last session.

## 9. Glossary, in plain words

Every track links here. Each lesson still explains a word the first time it uses it.

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **GTM (go to market)** | Everything a company does to find customers and sell to them: who they target, what they say, which channels they use |
| **GTM engineering** | Building the systems behind that: the lists, data, automations and dashboards, so selling runs on a machine instead of on memory |
| **GTM engineer** | The person who builds those systems |
| **RevOps (revenue operations)** | The team or person who runs the sales, marketing and customer success processes, tools and data |
| **ICP (ideal customer profile)** | A description of the companies most likely to buy from you and stay. "Logistics firms in the Netherlands with 20 to 200 staff that use a TMS" |
| **Persona** | The type of person inside those companies you talk to, like the operations director |
| **Firmographics** | Facts about a company: size, industry, location, revenue |
| **Segment** | A group of companies inside your ICP that you treat the same way |
| **Tier** | How good a fit an account is. Tier 1 is the best, gets the most effort |
| **Account** | A company you might sell to |
| **Contact or lead** | A person at an account |
| **List building** | Finding the accounts and contacts that match your ICP |
| **Lookalike** | Companies that look like your best existing customers |
| **Enrichment** | Adding missing facts to a record, like an email address, job title or company size |
| **Waterfall** | Trying one data source, and if it doesn't find the answer, trying the next, and the next. Saves money and finds more |
| **Verification** | Checking an email address exists before you send to it |
| **Bounce** | An email that couldn't be delivered |
| **Signal** | Something that happens which makes a company more likely to buy now: hiring, funding, a new boss, a new tool, a website visit |
| **Intent** | Signs a company is actively looking for something like what you sell |
| **Fit score and intent score** | Fit says "are they the right type of company", intent says "are they ready now" |
| **Scoring** | Giving accounts or leads points so you know who to contact first |
| **Eavesdrop loop** | Raka's name for listening to what customers and prospects say (in calls, posts, reviews) and feeding it back into your ICP, your messages and your lists |
| **Outbound** | You contact people first |
| **Inbound** | People contact you first, for example through a form on your website |
| **Allbound** | Joining inbound and outbound: someone who visits your site or clicks an ad then gets a well timed outbound message |
| **Sequence or cadence** | A planned series of messages over days or weeks, across email, LinkedIn and calls |
| **Sequencer** | A tool that sends sequences, like lemlist |
| **Multichannel** | Using more than one channel, like email plus LinkedIn |
| **Opener** | The first message to someone |
| **Personalisation** | Making a message specific to that person or company |
| **A/B test** | Sending two versions to two similar groups to see which works better |
| **Reply rate** | Replies divided by people messaged |
| **Positive reply rate** | Replies that show interest, divided by people messaged |
| **Deliverability** | Whether your emails land in the inbox rather than spam |
| **Sending domain** | A separate web address you send cold email from, so your main one isn't put at risk |
| **Warm up** | Slowly increasing how many emails a new inbox sends, so it builds a good reputation |
| **SPF, DKIM, DMARC** | Three settings on your domain that prove your emails really come from you. Without them, mail lands in spam |
| **CRM** | The system where a company keeps its accounts, contacts, deals and history. HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive |
| **Object** | A type of record in a CRM: contacts, companies, deals |
| **Field or property** | One piece of information on a record, like "job title" |
| **Field map** | A document listing every field, what it means, who fills it and where the data comes from |
| **Pipeline** | All open deals and where each one is |
| **Stage** | A step in the pipeline, like "meeting booked" or "proposal sent" |
| **Exit criteria** | What must be true before a deal moves to the next stage |
| **CRM hygiene** | Keeping CRM data correct, complete and without duplicates |
| **Deduplication (dedup)** | Finding and merging records that are the same person or company |
| **Routing** | Automatically giving a lead to the right person |
| **Speed to lead** | How fast someone follows up after a lead comes in |
| **API** | A way for one piece of software to talk to another and ask it for data or tell it to do something |
| **Webhook** | A message one tool sends to another the moment something happens |
| **Automation or workflow** | A set of steps that runs by itself, like "when a form is filled in, create a contact and tell the seller" |
| **Agent (AI agent)** | An AI set up to do a task in several steps, like researching an account and writing a summary |
| **Prompt** | The instruction you give an AI |
| **Dashboard** | A page of charts and numbers that shows how things are going |
| **Scorecard** | A short list of the few numbers you check every week |
| **Conversion rate** | The share of things that move to the next step, like meetings that become proposals |
| **Forecast** | Your best estimate of what will be sold this month or quarter |
| **Win and loss analysis** | Studying why deals were won or lost |
| **Call intelligence** | Tools that record and transcribe sales calls and pull out what was said, like Gong or Leexi |
| **Sales bible or playbook** | The written way your team sells: who, how, what to say, what each stage means |
| **Operating model** | Who does what in the revenue team and how work moves between them |
| **Loyalty or retention flows** | Messages and steps after the sale that keep customers happy, using and buying |
| **Customer health** | A score that shows whether a customer is likely to stay or leave |
| **Churn** | Customers who stop buying |
| **Commission data** | The sales data used to calculate what sellers get paid, which has to be correct |
| **GDPR** | The EU law on personal data. It applies to cold outreach too |

## 10. Rules learners must hear, and where they're taught

These go into the lessons named in the track files. They're not legal advice, and every lesson that covers them says so
and tells the learner to check their own country and company.

| Topic | What the course teaches |
|---|---|
| **GDPR and personal data** | You need a lawful reason to process someone's data (for B2B outreach this is usually "legitimate interest", which you must be able to justify and write down), you must tell people where you got their data if they ask, you must honour an opt out at once, and you keep only what you need |
| **Cold email differs by country** | The rules for emailing businesses without consent differ across Europe. The UK and the Netherlands are more permissive for business addresses than Germany, where unsolicited B2B email is risky without consent. Learners check the rules for each country they email, and when in doubt use LinkedIn or a call first |
| **LinkedIn limits** | LinkedIn restricts automation and caps connection requests. Tools that push too hard get accounts restricted. The course teaches safe daily limits as set in the tools and to check LinkedIn's own terms |
| **Scraping** | Only collect data in ways the source allows, and never collect more than the task needs |
| **Honesty in messages** | No fake "Re:" subjects, no invented personal connections, no claims you can't back up |
